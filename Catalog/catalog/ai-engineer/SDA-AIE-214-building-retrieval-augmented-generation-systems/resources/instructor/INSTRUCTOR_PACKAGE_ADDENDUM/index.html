    SDA-AIE-214 — Instructor Package Addendum v1.0¶

    Building Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems · بناء أنظمة التوليد المعزز بالاسترجاع¶

    Status: additive. INSTRUCTOR_PACKAGE.md remains the authoritative source for course structure, module content, code listings and the rubric. This addendum records four changes to the material the course runs on, and the assets that implement them.

    # Change Replaces Affects
    1 Dalil's corpus is now real. Layer A is the published SDAIA/NDMO regulatory corpus; Layer B is a thin synthetic overlay supplying only what public regulation cannot. "a deliberately messy 300-document corpus" of invented documents M1–M7, capstone, every lab
    2 Three public benchmarks — MIRACL-ar, BEIR, TechQA/eManual — as instruments that validate the participant's measuring tools. self-graded metrics only M3, M4, M6
    3 Two DeepLearning.AI short courses wired into the schedule: Chroma Advanced Retrieval for AI as required Day-2 pre-work; LlamaIndex Building and Evaluating Advanced RAG as the Day-3 companion. no external prep pre-course, D2, D3
    4 A complete, runnable asset repository — corpus builders, the dalil package, evaluation harnesses, probe/golden/red-team sets, answer keys, CI gate. code listings in the guide delivery, all labs

    1 · The corpus: Layer A + Layer B¶

    1.1 Why this changes the course, not just the data¶

    The original corpus was invented, and invented corpora flatter the systems built on them. Their vocabulary is consistent because one author wrote it; their structure is regular because one script produced it; their questions are answerable because the same person wrote both. A participant can reach recall@10 of 0.95 on such a corpus and learn nothing transferable.

    More importantly: the documents SDAIA publishes are the documents the participants' own organisations are legally bound by. An engineer who spends four days debugging retrieval over the Personal Data Protection Law, its Implementing Regulation, the Data Classification Policy and the Generative AI Guidelines is not doing an exercise. They are building the index they will be asked for the month they get back to their desk. The self-referential moment — a RAG system whose corpus contains the rules governing RAG systems — is worth the whole redesign on its own.

    1.2 Layer A — real, published, citable¶

    corpus/manifest/sdaia_layer_a.yaml lists 17 real documents across four tiers:

    Tier Documents
    Law Personal Data Protection Law (RD M/19, amended M/148)
    Regulation PDPL Implementing Regulation · Data Transfer Regulation
    National data management Data Classification · Data Sharing · Open Data · Data Monetization policies; Anonymisation and Destruction guidelines; Transfer Risk Assessment guideline
    AI AI Ethics Principles · GenAI Guidelines (Government) · GenAI Guidelines (Public) · AI Adoption Framework · Deepfakes Guidelines
    Workforce National Occupational Standards for Data & AI

    Each entry carries url_candidates, a discovery page, the department and access tier to stamp on its chunks, what it teaches, and its share of the golden set. Two entries are load-bearing for specific teaching moments:

    • PDPL Law + Implementing Regulation are the multi-hop pair. The best questions in the golden set cross them: "what does the Law require, and what does the Regulation say that means in practice?" No single chunk answers that, which is Module 7's entire thesis, demonstrated on real instruments.
    • Data Sharing Policy + Open Data Policy are the near-duplicate pair. They are semantically adjacent, dense retrieval confuses them, and the reranker separates them. It is the cleanest live demonstration in the course of why a cross-encoder earns its latency.

    Operational reality: the URLs move¶

    SDAIA reorganises its publication pages. corpus/fetch_corpus.py therefore tries each candidate URL, then falls back to link discovery on the index pages, then records the document as UNRESOLVED and continues. A partially resolved Layer A is a supported state, not a failure.

    T-minus-1-week instructor task. Run make corpus-fetch && make corpus-verify on a networked machine. Commit corpus/LOCK.json (SHA-256 hashes, resolved URLs, fetch date). Every cohort must index byte-identical documents, or their benchmark numbers are not comparable to each other or to last cohort's.

    corpus/validate_corpus.py then prints a per-lab readiness verdict — which labs the corpus as actually assembled still supports, and which are degraded. Discovering on Day 2 that the near-duplicate demo has no near-duplicates is an avoidable way to lose a teaching moment.

    Licensing¶

    These are public government publications used for training. Do not re-host them. Ship LOCK.json, not the PDFs, in any public repository. See corpus/LICENSING.md.

    1.3 Layer B — the four things public regulation cannot teach¶

    Public regulators publish only current, public, digital, prose documents. That removes four teaching surfaces the course depends on, so Layer B supplies exactly those and nothing else:

    Missing from Layer A Layer B supplies Serves
    Access control — every public document is public 48 restricted + 62 internal documents across 6 departments M5 access filter; capstone "0 cross-department leaks"
    Supersession — only current editions are published 12 matched v1/v2 circular pairs; v2 lands only in corpus_v2 F8, the Day-4 staleness drill
    Scanned Arabic — SDAIA PDFs have clean text layers 22 image-only Arabic policy PDFs + hand-quality transcripts F1, the OCR path, the CER gate
    Tabular facts — few retrievable numeric tables 8 XLSX allowance tables, plus in-PDF tables F1, "the table nobody could retrieve"

    Layer B is a fictional adopter of the Layer A rules — the National Digital Services Authority (NDSA) — and every Layer B document cites a Layer A instrument by title. That is the design decision that makes this one corpus rather than two, and it creates the best question class in the course:

    "Our Data Export Procedure requires an approval step before execution — which SDAIA instrument imposes that, and what does that instrument require the assessment to cover?"

    Hop 1 lands in Layer B. Hop 2 lands in Layer A. Neither alone answers it.

    Every generated file is stamped X-Dalil-Synthetic in its metadata and carries a visible disclaimer footer in both languages. Nothing in Layer B can be mistaken for a real instrument.

    Generation is deterministic from a seed (corpus/layer_b_specs/layer_b.yaml), so every cohort worldwide gets byte-identical documents and comparable numbers.

    1.4 What the corpus actually measures out of the box¶

    Numbers below are from the shipped generator, measured — not estimated.

    Property Value
    Layer B documents 134 (22 scanned AR · 60 digital PDF · 20 DOCX · 24 HTML · 8 XLSX)
    corpus_v1 chunks (structure-aware) 492 · tables kept whole 56 · mid-sentence starts 4.1%
    corpus_v1 chunks (naive 500-char) see data/index/ingest_report_naive.json for the contrast
    corpus_v2 chunks 552 · 12 superseded/current pairs live
    OCR CER, naive settings (ara+eng, psm 4) 0.084 — fails the gate
    OCR CER, tuned (ara, psm 6, binarise, 300 dpi) 0.053 mean · 0.062 p95 · 0.100 worst
    CER gate as shipped 0.06 (stretch target 0.05 — the fast-finisher task)

    Why the gate is 0.06 and not 0.05. It was measured, not chosen. The tuned path lands at 0.053, so 0.06 is a gate a participant can pass only by tuning — the naive settings land at 0.084 and fail. A gate nobody can fail teaches nothing; a gate nobody can pass teaches despair. Reaching 0.05 requires per-document DPI escalation and is the distinction task, documented in assessments/keys/lab2_ocr_key.md.

    The retrieval story, measured on the shipped labelled set¶

    BM25 alone, 138 labelled queries answerable in corpus_v1:

    Query class n recall@10
    identifier 60 1.000
    tabular 24 1.000
    ambiguous 12 1.000
    procedural 20 0.300
    factoid 22 0.239
    aggregate 138 0.777

    This is the shape Module 4 needs and the original synthetic labels did not produce. The labelled queries are deliberately paraphrases, not restatements of document titles: a label set whose queries copy the title measures string overlap, reports recall@10 = 1.00 for BM25 alone, and teaches participants that their retriever is already perfect and that Modules 3 and 4 were a waste of a day. Every query in data/retrieval_labels.jsonl is phrased the way a colleague would ask it — "Am I allowed to paste an internal document into ChatGPT?", not "What does the Approved LLM Gateway Usage Procedure say?"

    2 · Public benchmarks as instruments¶

    The Dalil corpus proves the system works. These three prove the participant's instruments work. Fifteen minutes each, run as sidebars, never as labs.

    2.1 MIRACL Arabic — Day 2, after Lab 3 (15 min)¶

    python -m dalil.benchmarks.miracl_ar --limit 200

    Module 3 shows bge-m3 beating an English-only model on Dalil's Arabic subset (~0.88 vs ~0.29). That number is compelling and self-graded — we wrote the corpus and we wrote the labels. MIRACL-ar is human-annotated Arabic retrieval by people with no stake in this course.

    The diagnostic value is in the disagreement:

    Dalil Arabic recall MIRACL-ar recall@100 Reading
    high in the published range for the model pipeline is sound
    high far below range the corpus was flattering you — your queries and passages share vocabulary a real user's phrasing would not
    low in range the pipeline is fine; ingestion or normalisation is dropping the Arabic

    That middle row is the one worth the fifteen minutes.

    2.2 BEIR — Day 3, before Lab 6 (15 min)¶

    python -m dalil.benchmarks.beir --dataset scifact

    BEIR's 18 datasets and nine task types all ship the same three objects — corpus, queries, qrels — so recall@k / MRR / nDCG code written once for Dalil runs unmodified across all of them. That uniformity is why it belongs in the metrics lab specifically. Participants point their own metric implementations at a dataset with published baselines. If their nDCG@10 on SciFact is far off the published figure for a known model, the bug is in their metric code — and they have just avoided grading their entire capstone with a broken ruler.

    Recommended, in order: scifact (tiny, graded relevance), nfcorpus (vocabulary mismatch — where dense wins), trec-covid (0/1/2 graded judgements, where nDCG and recall visibly diverge — the lesson).

    2.3 TechQA / eManual (RAGBench) — Day 3, in the red-team hour (20 min)¶

    python -m dalil.benchmarks.techqa --tau 0.35

    The only public set in this course that grades refusal. Every other benchmark rewards retrieving something; a meaningful share of TechQA's questions are deliberately unanswerable from the supplied corpus. It also mirrors the assistant most participants will be asked to build at work: heterogeneous manuals, jargon-dense queries, an audience that will act on the answer.

    It reports four numbers, and the fourth is the one that stops participants gaming the third:

    • recall@k, ndcg@10 on the answerable subset
    • refusal_recall — unanswerable questions correctly refused
    • false_answer_rate — unanswerable questions answered anyway (F7)
    • over_refusal_rate — answerable questions wrongly refused

    A system that refuses everything scores 1.00 on refusal recall and is worthless. That is the anti-pattern the capstone rubric caps at 70%, and this is the benchmark that exposes it. Sweep --tau and plot refusal_recall against over_refusal: that curve, not a single number, is how a refusal threshold is chosen, and DECISIONS.md must show it.

    2.4 Where they slot in¶

    When Benchmark Duration Replaces
    D2 H2, after Lab 3 MIRACL-ar 15 min part of the Lab 3 buffer
    D3 H3, before Lab 6 BEIR/scifact 15 min part of the M6 lecture
    D3 H5, red-team hour TechQA + tau sweep 20 min folded into the existing red-team

    Net schedule impact: zero added hours. All three fit inside existing buffers, and the tau sweep replaces the hand-waving that used to occupy the same slot.

    3 · The DeepLearning.AI courses in the schedule¶

    3.1 Chroma — Advanced Retrieval for AI → required Day-2 pre-work¶

    ~52 minutes. deeplearning.ai/courses/advanced-retrieval-for-ai

    It addresses, in under an hour, precisely the thing participants otherwise learn the hard way at 11:00 on Day 2: semantic similarity does not guarantee useful retrieval.

    Lesson Maps to What SDA-AIE-214 then adds
    Embeddings-based retrieval M3 bilingual model choice; Qdrant payload filters; the ef/recall curve
    Pitfalls of retrieval — when simple vector search fails M4 §1 the identifier failure specifically, and why no embedding model fixes it
    Query expansion M7 §2 routing: expansion only for the AMBIGUOUS class, with measured cost
    Cross-encoder re-ranking M4 §3 the two-stage budget as an economic argument, per query class
    Embedding adapters — out of scope; mentioned as the "last resort, for the embedder not the generator" from the M1 case study

    Gate. Lessons 2 and 4 (Pitfalls and Cross-encoder re-ranking) are mandatory before Day 2. prework/README.md carries the assignment, and prework/readiness_check.md is the five-question self-check participants bring to Day 2 Hour 1. It is not graded; it is visible, which is what makes pre-work happen.

    Instructor note. Do not re-teach the Chroma material. Day 2 Hour 3 opens by assuming it: "You saw last night that semantic search misses exact tokens. Here is what that costs on our corpus, by query class." The identifier row at 0.31 lands ten times harder on a room that has already seen the mechanism.

    3.2 LlamaIndex — Building and Evaluating Advanced RAG → Day-3 companion¶

    ~1h55m with code exercises. deeplearning.ai/short-courses/building-evaluating-advanced-rag

    This is Day 3's syllabus in another vocabulary, which is exactly why it is a companion rather than pre-work: participants meet the same ideas twice, in two implementations, and learn which parts are the idea and which are the framework.

    DLAI lesson SDA-AIE-214 Relationship
    Sentence-window retrieval M2 chunking · M5 context Same problem — retrieve small, generate with context. Ours prepends the section heading; theirs widens the window. Compare them in Lab 5's fast-finisher task.
    Auto-merging retrieval M2 chunking Hierarchical merge vs our structure-aware split. Ask which is right for policy documents and demand evidence.
    RAG triad: context relevance M6 context_precision Same metric, different name
    RAG triad: groundedness M6 faithfulness Same metric, different name
    RAG triad: answer relevance M6 answer_relevancy Same metric, different name
    TruLens evaluation M6 RAGAS harness Different tool, same discipline. Point out that the metric names differ across every framework and the concepts do not. A participant who can map RAGAS↔TruLens↔the triad will not be lost by whatever their employer standardised on.

    Where it goes in the day. Assigned at the end of Day 2 as optional evening work; referenced explicitly three times on Day 3 (M5 context construction, M6 metric introduction, Lab 6 step 1). Participants who did it recognise every metric before it is defined; participants who did not lose nothing.

    Assign the idea, not the framework. SDA-AIE-214 does not standardise on LlamaIndex. The crosswalk table above is in prework/dlai_crosswalk.md and is worth putting on a slide, because the honest lesson of Day 3 is that RAG evaluation is a small set of concepts wearing many brand names.

    4.1 Design decisions the instructor should know about¶

    Everything runs offline. MemoryBackend implements BM25 plus optional dense vectors in-process, so the evaluation harness, the practical-assessment kits and all 53 tests run with no Docker, no model download and no gateway key. This is not a convenience: it is what stops a 20-hour course losing an hour to infrastructure. The labs still use Qdrant — that is the point of Lab 3 — but nothing is blocked on it.

    Degradation is always announced, never silent. A missing reranker returns the input order and sets reranked: False. A missing dense model produces BM25-only results and stamps a warning in the report. The offline judge labels every metric judge: "offline-proxy" with a warning inside the JSON. The hash-384 test embedder stamps its name into every report so the numbers can be discarded. The single worst outcome in this course is a participant who trusts a number produced by a stage that never ran.

    Answer keys are derived, never typed. scripts/build_eval_sets.py reads the facts recorded by the corpus generator and emits the probes, the 138 labelled queries, the 120-question golden set and the three red-team sets. Regenerate the corpus with a different seed and every key regenerates with it. tests/test_corpus_integrity.py fails the build if they ever drift apart.

    Layer A ground truth is honestly deferred. Golden questions about real SDAIA documents ship with verify: true and an evidence_hint rather than a fabricated answer, because the repository cannot know the wording of a PDF downloaded at delivery time. scripts/verify_golden.py resolves them against the fetched corpus, one at a time, for instructor approval. An unverified golden set is not a measuring instrument — it is a list of hopes. Run it once per delivery and commit the result.

    4.3 Changes to specific labs¶

    Lab Change Reason
    1 Probe sets are now generated (data/probes/*.jsonl); the runner prints a blank failure-class column The runner must not do the diagnosis for them
    2 OCR settings are the naive ones by default; the CER gate is a tunable 0.06 Tuning is the lab. The winning combination is in the answer key, not the code
    3 Labelled queries are paraphrases, not title restatements; MIRACL-ar sidebar added A label set that copies titles reports recall 1.00 for BM25 and kills Modules 3–4
    4 Ablation is one command (make ablation) producing the four-column per-class table The table is the lesson; make it cheap to produce
    5 Access control is re-asserted at context construction, and assemble_context reports what it dropped and why Defence in depth, and the drop counters make it visible
    6 Offline proxy judge available; gate warns on config drift and judge change Lab 6 must run on a laptop; and comparing across configs is the commonest false regression
    7 Router decides the path; sub-question dedup is enforced in decompose "Loops everywhere" and the twin-sub-question bug, structurally prevented
    Capstone make demo scripts all five rubric demonstrations, including the staleness toggle and the access-control contrast A demo that dies at stage 1 tells the grader nothing about stages 2–5

    4.4 What the tests are for¶

    tests/ is 53 tests, and roughly a third of them are teaching material — they encode the failures the course is about, executably:

    • test_aggregate_hides_a_collapsed_class — 90 perfect factoids and 10 broken identifiers produce an aggregate of 0.90 and a class at 0.00. This is the rubric's anti-pattern, as an assertion.
    • test_gate_warns_when_config_drifted / test_gate_warns_when_judge_changed — the two commonest false regressions.
    • test_grader_can_say_none_so_the_loop_can_refuse — the Module 7 knowledge check, executable.
    • test_index_and_query_paths_agree — the single most important invariant in the course.
    • test_tables_are_never_split_mid_row, test_access_is_reasserted_at_context_construction, test_invented_citation_is_detected.

    Worth ten minutes on Day 3: open the test file, not the slide.

Document count was never the difficulty; heterogeneity, supersession, access tiers and Arabic scans are.
