    Decision Memo — template and worked example¶

    SDA-DSC-213 Capstone · Injaz (إنجاز) · SDAIA Academy

    The memo is one page. Not one page of 8-point type — one page a board member reads in three minutes and can act on. It is worth 15 of 100 points, and it is the artefact SDA-DSC-311 consumes as an input.

    Three rules, and they are the ones that get marked:

    1. Effects and confidence intervals in business units. Percentage points of completion, and where it helps, completions per month. No p-values. No "statistically significant" as a synonym for "true".
    2. A recommendation per intervention, in the first five lines. Ship, scale, hold-and-extend, or stop. Leadership should not have to hunt for it.
    3. Assumptions stated as risks, plus one explicit "what would change this." A specific, costed next step — not "further research is needed."

    Part A — The skeleton¶

    Copy everything between the rules. Keep the headings; they are what the rubric is read against.

    MEMO¶

    To: decision-maker / board From: your name, experimentation lead Date: date Subject: the two decisions, in one line Analysis plan hash: plan_hash from freeze_plan() — frozen before results

    Recommendation

    • [Intervention 1]: [SHIP / SCALE / HOLD AND EXTEND / STOP]. One clause of why.
    • [Intervention 2]: [SHIP / SCALE / HOLD AND EXTEND / STOP]. One clause of why.

    What we found

    Intervention Effect on completion 95% CI In business units Decision threshold Verdict

    Two or three sentences of plain-language reading. What the interval rules out and what it does not.

    Guardrails

    One line per guardrail family: did anything get worse, and by how much, after the correct multiple-testing correction.

    How much to believe it

    Intervention Design Identifying assumption Evidence it holds What would break it

    Risks

    1. Risk stated as a risk, with its consequence. 2. 3.

    What would change this

    One sentence. A specific, costed action and what it would tell you.

    Decision plot

    Insert decision_plot(...) — effects with intervals, and the threshold line.

    Part B — Worked example¶

    This is the standard. It is the autofill decision from the capstone data, written to length, with the real numbers a correct analysis produces. Your memo will have different wording and must cover both interventions; the point here is the shape, the register, and the honesty.

    MEMO¶

    To: Injaz Programme Board From: Experimentation Lead, Injaz Analytics Date: Day 4 of the cohort Subject: AI autofill — national launch decision; regional reminders — scale decision Analysis plan hash: b47e305fe7c0… (frozen before any result was computed)

    Recommendation

    • AI autofill: HOLD AND EXTEND — do not launch nationally yet. The feature works, but the evidence cannot yet show it is large enough to pay for. One more week of the current test would settle it.
    • Regional reminders: SCALE to the four regions that never activated. The effect is well above the bar and survives every check we can run on it.

    What we found

    Intervention Effect on completion 95% CI In business units Threshold Verdict
    AI autofill (A/B, CUPED-adjusted, n = 49,510 triggered users) +1.6 pp +0.8 to +2.4 pp ≈ +4,400 completions/month, but plausibly as few as +2,100 or as many as +6,600 +1.0 pp Real, but not shown to clear the bar
    Regional reminders (difference-in-differences, 13 regions × 24 months) +2.6 pp +2.5 to +2.7 pp ≈ +100 completions/month in the four never-activated regions +1.0 pp Clears the bar

    Autofill raised service completion by 1.6 percentage points, from 55.1% to 56.6% among users who actually reached the autofill step. That effect is real: the interval does not include zero. It is not, however, the question the board asked. The board's bar is +1.0 pp, and the interval runs from +0.8 to +2.4 pp — it straddles the bar. The most likely value is comfortably above the threshold; a value below it has not been ruled out. Launching nationally today means accepting a genuine chance that the feature does not repay its build and run cost.

    The reminder programme is a different picture. It was never randomised, so the estimate rests on an assumption rather than on a design — but the assumption is supported, the interval is narrow, and it sits entirely above the bar.

    Guardrails

    Four guardrails were pre-registered and tested as one family with a Bonferroni correction (m = 4). None regressed. Support-contact rate rose 0.2 pp (−0.2 to +0.5 pp before correction; not significant after it) — the only metric worth watching in a longer run. Upload errors were flat (−0.3 pp, indistinguishable from zero). Two guardrails moved in our favour and survived the correction: time-to-complete fell about 10 seconds per session, and drop-off fell 1.3 pp.

    How much to believe it

    Intervention Design Identifying assumption Evidence it holds What would break it
    Autofill Randomised A/B, user-level hash assignment Randomisation — no assumption beyond the mechanics working Sample-ratio check passes (35,016 vs 34,984; p = 0.90); all 21 covariate cells balanced, worst |SMD| = 0.019 A logging bug that drops treated users non-randomly; the SRM check is our detector and it is clean
    Reminders Difference-in-differences, region-clustered errors Parallel trends — absent the programme, activated and never-activated regions would have moved together Event study: the five estimated pre-activation coefficients (relative months −6 to −2, baseline −1) all sit within ±0.5 pp of zero and none is distinguishable from zero A region-specific initiative launched at the same time as activation; we cannot rule this out from the data

    Risks

    1. The autofill estimate is precise enough to detect the effect, not precise enough to price it. Acting on the point estimate alone treats an outcome the data still permits — a true effect below +1.0 pp — as though it had been ruled out.
    2. The reminder effect is observational. Regions chose their activation order. Parallel trends is supported in the pre-period but is untestable for the post-period, and one unmeasured region-level initiative would overturn it.
    3. The reminder number is probably an underestimate. The programme ramps in over roughly two months, and the standard two-way fixed-effects estimator averages the weak early months in with the mature ones. A cohort-aware estimator puts the mature effect nearer +3.3 pp. This risk points in the safe direction for a scale decision.
    4. Support contacts. The one guardrail that moved against us did so modestly and did not survive correction, but a national launch multiplies the volume; it should be a stop-condition in the extended run.

    What would change this

    Run the autofill test for one more week — about 50,000 more triggered users, which the platform supplies in roughly 7 running days. That would narrow the interval by a factor of about 1.4 and is enough to place the whole interval on one side of the +1.0 pp bar. If the lower bound then clears +1.0 pp, launch; if the interval settles below it, the feature is real and not worth the build, and we stop.

    Part C — How the example scores, line by line¶

    Read this next to the rubric row for Decision communication (15 points).

    What the example does Why it earns the mark
    Recommendation in the first five lines, one per intervention, with a verb (HOLD AND EXTEND, SCALE) The board can stop reading after five lines and still act
    "+1.6 pp, 95% CI +0.8 to +2.4 pp" and "≈ +4,400 completions/month, plausibly as few as +2,100" Effect and interval, in business units. The interval is translated too — this is where most memos quietly drop the uncertainty
    "The interval straddles the bar" The practical-vs-statistical distinction made in one sentence a non-statistician understands
    No p-values anywhere in the body The one p-value that appears (SRM, p = 0.90) is a validity check, not a finding
    Guardrails reported as a family, with the correction named Criterion 4 wants the correct correction family, and criterion 6 wants it stated plainly
    A table of assumptions with "what would break it" Assumptions as risks, not as boilerplate. The right-hand column is the sentence you will be asked for live
    Risk 3 says the estimate is probably too low Honest uncertainty runs in both directions. A memo whose caveats all conveniently point one way reads as advocacy
    "One more week — about 50,000 more triggered users, roughly 7 running days" "What would change this" is specific and costed. "More data is needed" earns nothing
    The plan hash in the header Criterion 7 evidence, at the cost of one line

    Length check. Part B is one page at 11 pt. If yours is longer, the first things to cut are: method names, the word "statistically", and any sentence that explains what a confidence interval is.

    Part D — Phrases to use, and phrases that cost you marks¶

    Instead of Write
    "The result was statistically significant (p < 0.05), so we recommend shipping." "The effect is +1.6 pp (95% CI +0.8 to +2.4). Our bar is +1.0 pp, and the interval straddles it — so we cannot yet say it is big enough to pay for."
    "There was no effect." "We could not distinguish the effect from zero at this sample size; effects up to +X pp remain consistent with what we saw."
    "We assume parallel trends." "The comparison assumes activated and never-activated regions would have moved together. The five estimated pre-activation coefficients sit within ±0.5 pp of zero, which supports it; a region-specific initiative timed with activation would break it."
    "Further research is needed." "One more week — 50,000 more triggered users, about 7 running days — would place the whole interval on one side of the bar."
    "The model controls for support calls, distance and device age." (delete — and check that support calls is not a mediator you have just controlled away)
    "The lift was 1.6%." "The completion rate rose 1.6 percentage points, from 55.1% to 56.6%." Percent and percentage points are different numbers and reviewers check.
