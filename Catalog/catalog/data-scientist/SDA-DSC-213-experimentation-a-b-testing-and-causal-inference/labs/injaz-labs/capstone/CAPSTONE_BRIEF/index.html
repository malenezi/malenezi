    Capstone Brief — The Injaz Experimentation and Impact Evaluation Package¶

    SDA-DSC-213 · Experimentation, A/B Testing and Causal Inference · SDAIA Academy Participant-facing brief · Day 4 · 40% of the course grade

    1. Scenario¶

    You are the experimentation lead assigned to Injaz (إنجاز), the unified Saudi digital-government services platform where citizens renew licences, issue certificates, update addresses and pay fees online.

    Leadership faces two linked decisions before the next budget cycle.

    Decision 1 — the AI-assisted autofill feature. A new AI-assisted document-and-form autofill feature has been running as a randomised A/B test. It is intended to raise the service-completion rate. Leadership wants to know whether to launch it nationally. The build-and-run cost is real, so the programme board has set a practical-significance threshold of +1.0 percentage point on completion: an effect smaller than that does not pay for the feature, however clean the p-value. Data: injaz_autofill_ab.csv (+ injaz_pre_period.csv for CUPED).

    Decision 2 — the regional reminder-notification programme. Last year a reminder-notification programme was rolled out region by region, in waves, without randomisation. Leadership wants to know whether to scale it nationally. There was never a control arm; there is a panel of regions over 24 months and four activation waves, with four regions never activated. Data: injaz_regions_panel.csv (and injaz_regions_panel_pretrend.csv, injaz_users.csv if you use them).

    The two decisions are linked because they compete for the same budget and will be presented on the same slide. Your mandate: design and analyse a clean experiment for the feature, produce a credible causal evaluation of the pilot where randomisation was impossible, and deliver a decision memo leadership can act on.

    Everything you built in Labs 1–7 is a component. The capstone is the integration, plus your own rigour and communication.

    2. Mandatory requirements¶

    These map one-to-one onto the grading rubric in §6.

    # Requirement Maps to
    1 Design. A frozen design doc for the autofill A/B test: randomisation unit + justification, the OEC, ≥ 3 guardrails, the trigger definition, the unit of analysis, and a written analysis plan produced before analysis. LO1
    2 Power. A sizing analysis: required per-arm sample and run-length plan for your chosen MDE, including the arm multiplier and the trigger rate, plus a CUPED-adjusted alternative with its quantified saving. LO2
    3 Experiment integrity. Deterministic hash assignment, a passing SRM check, and a covariate-balance table (all SMD < 0.1). An experiment that fails these must be reported as invalid, not analysed anyway. LO1 / LO3
    4 Analysis. The autofill effect with correct (robust or clustered) variance, a CUPED adjustment, guardrails checked with the correct multiple-testing family, and a clear statistical-vs-practical-significance verdict against the design threshold. LO3
    5 Causal evaluation. A quasi-experimental evaluation of the reminder pilot — DiD with an event-study parallel-trends check, or matching with a sensitivity analysis — including the DAG / identifying assumption and ≥ 2 refutation or validation checks. LO4 / LO5
    6 Communication. A one-page decision memo: effect sizes and confidence intervals in business units, a ship / scale / hold recommendation per intervention, the key assumptions stated as risks, and an explicit "what would change this." LO6
    7 Reproducibility & rigour. A clean, runnable notebook or repo; a fixed random seed; the frozen analysis plan versioned before results; no peeking-driven stopping; honest treatment of any null or non-identifiable result. LO3

    Choose at least one extension¶

    Extension What it must show
    Heterogeneous treatment effects (CATE) on the autofill test with EconML (causal forest) the subgroup structure, and an honest note on multiple testing across subgroups
    Always-valid / sequential inference on the autofill test a confidence sequence that would have made continuous monitoring legitimate, contrasted with the fixed-horizon interval
    Staggered-DiD estimator (Callaway–Sant'Anna or Sun–Abraham) on the full multi-wave reminder rollout the cohort-aware estimate compared against naive TWFE, and why they differ
    Instrumental-variables estimate of the effect of filing channel on completion a defended instrument (all three conditions argued) and the first-stage F
    Power / variance-reduction study how much CUPED and stratification would shorten future Injaz experiments, in days

    Extensions add up to +5 bonus, capped at 100, and only if your mandatory scope is already ≥ 80. A shaky extension bolted onto a weak core scores nothing.

    3. Target architecture¶

    Frame ──> Design doc (frozen plan) ──> Power/sizing ──> [A/B: assign → SRM → balance → analyse]
                                                     └─> [Pilot: DAG → identify → estimate → refute]
   A/B effect (CUPED, robust CI) ─┐
   Pilot effect (DiD/matching CI) ┼──> reconcile ──> Decision plot (vs threshold) ──> Decision memo
   guardrails + validation ───────┘                                                    (ship/scale/hold)
Reproducible notebook: fixed seed · frozen plan versioned before results · honest nulls

    causal_utils gives you every piece of this. You are not being graded on re-implementing did_twfe; you are being graded on whether you used it on the right rows, with the right standard errors, and said the right thing about it.

    4. Deliverables¶

    1. A runnable analysis notebook (or repo) covering design, power, A/B analysis and causal evaluation, with a fixed seed and a clean top-to-bottom run. Start from notebooks/capstone_starter.ipynb.
    2. The frozen design doc + analysis plan — timestamped and hashed before the results section. Use capstone/DESIGN_DOC_TEMPLATE.md and causal_utils.freeze_plan(); put the plan hash in the memo.
    3. Experiment-integrity evidence — the SRM result and the covariate-balance table, printed in the notebook.
    4. A causal-evaluation section — DAG, identifying assumption, estimate with CI, and refutation/validation output.
    5. A one-page decision memo — the decision plot, per-intervention recommendations, quantified uncertainty, and "what would change this." Use capstone/DECISION_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md.
    6. A 6-minute presentation — the two decisions, the evidence and its limits, and the recommendation.

    Submit the notebook, the frozen design doc and the memo. Run capstone/SELF_AUDIT_CHECKLIST.md before you submit; every item is answerable yes or no.

    5. Milestones¶

    Day 4 clock times. The gate must be met to move on — an instructor will spot-check.

    Milestone Due Time Gate
    M-A framed question + frozen design doc + sizing Day 4 H3 start · 11:00 instructor spot-check of the plan — the plan is hashed before any results cell has been run
    M-B A/B analysed: SRM, balance, effect, guardrails Day 4 H3 end · 11:50 integrity checks pass (or the experiment is declared invalid, with evidence)
    M-C pilot evaluated: DiD or matching + validation Day 4 H4 mid · 13:05 a refutation or validation check is present and its result is stated
    M-D decision memo + extension complete Day 4 H4 end · 13:30 full self-audit checklist completed
    M-E presentation + submission Day 4 H5 · 13:40–14:30 rubric scoring, live

    If you are behind at M-A, cut the extension, not the freeze. A plan frozen after results is worth zero on criterion 1 and caps criterion 7.

    6. Grading rubric (100 points)¶

    Criterion Weight 90–100% band 70–89% band < 70% band
    Experiment design & analysis plan 15 Unit justified, OEC + guardrails sound, plan frozen before results Minor gaps (thin guardrails, late freeze) No plan / metrics chosen after seeing data
    Power & sample sizing 15 Correct sizing incl. arms, trigger, floor; CUPED saving quantified Mostly correct; one factor omitted Wrong or missing sizing; under-run unrecognised
    Experiment integrity 10 SRM + balance correct; invalid experiments flagged Checks present but partial No SRM/balance; invalid test analysed anyway
    A/B analysis correctness 20 Robust/clustered variance, CUPED, correct correction family, practical-vs-statistical verdict 1–2 issues (naive SE or missing correction) Naive t-test, no correction, p-value worship
    Causal evaluation 20 DAG + assumption stated; DiD/matching valid; ≥ 2 refutations; sensitivity honest Method sound; validation thin No identification argument; unvalidated claim
    Decision communication 15 One-page memo: effects + CIs in business units, clear recommendation, risks + "what would change this" Memo works; uncertainty understated p-values to leadership; overstated certainty
    Reproducibility & integrity 5 Clean seeded run; honest nulls; no peeking Minor reproducibility gaps Non-runnable; peeking-driven stop

    Pass ≥ 70. Distinction ≥ 90.

    Anti-patterns that cap a criterion at 70%¶

    • Metrics chosen after seeing the data.
    • Peeking-driven stopping.
    • Controlling for a mediator or a collider.
    • Presenting an observational estimate with no identifying assumption and no refutation.
    • Reporting bare p-values to leadership.

    One thing you will be asked live¶

    During your briefing you will be asked to state, out loud: the identifying assumption behind your causal estimate, and the single unmeasured variable that would break it. Prepare that sentence.

    7. "We cannot conclude" is a full-marks answer¶

    Read this twice.

    A correct, well-argued "we cannot conclude" earns full marks on the criteria it touches. A defensible hold, keep running or not identifiable — here is what would be needed is a top-band answer. This is not a consolation prize and it is not grading on a curve: it is the graded skill.

    What makes it full marks rather than a shrug is that all four of these are present:

    1. The number and its uncertainty, stated in business units — not "no effect", but "+X.X pp, 95% CI [a, b]".
    2. What that interval rules out and what it does not. An interval that excludes zero but straddles the decision threshold means "real, but we cannot show it is big enough to pay for" — a completely different sentence from "no effect".
    3. Why it is not conclusive — under-powered for the threshold, an assumption that fails its check, an estimand that is not the one leadership asked about.
    4. What would change it — a specific, costed next step: run N more days, measure variable V, find a wave with a clean never-treated comparison.

    What is not full marks: "the p-value was 0.06 so nothing happened", or a verdict of ship justified by significance alone when the confidence interval crosses the decision threshold.

    8. What excellent looks like¶

    The distinction band is not about more code. It is about six habits.

    1. The estimand is named before the data is touched. Excellent: "The ATT of autofill exposure on service completion among triggered users, over the 21-day run." Weak: "the effect of autofill." If your memo says "the effect" without saying on whom, over what window, you have not answered the question that was asked.

    2. The analysis population is the triggered population, and you say so. The trigger is not a technicality. Assigned users who never reached the changed step cannot have been affected by it, so including them dilutes the estimate toward zero by roughly the trigger rate. Excellent work states the trigger definition in the frozen plan, filters on it, reports the trigger rate, and — if it also reports the all-assigned number — labels it correctly as the intention-to-treat estimate for a different question.

    3. The confidence interval is compared to the decision threshold, not to zero. Zero is not the decision boundary; the programme board's +1.0 pp is. Excellent work draws the decision plot with the threshold line on it and reads it out: "the point estimate clears the bar, the interval does not." Statistical significance answers is it there; practical significance answers is it worth it. Both sentences appear in a top-band memo.

    4. Assumptions are named, checked where checkable, and priced where not. Parallel trends is not "assumed" — it is supported by flat pre-period event-study coefficients, or it is not, and you say which. Unconfoundedness in a matching design is never checkable; excellent work replaces "we assume unconfoundedness" with "a hidden confounder that changed the odds of enrolling by more than Γ ≈ x would overturn this." Naming the specific unmeasured variable that would break your estimate — and admitting you cannot rule it out — reads as confidence, not weakness.

    5. Disagreements are reconciled, not cherry-picked. When the experimental and observational estimates point different ways, or when TWFE disagrees with a cohort-aware estimator, an excellent answer explains the gap through the assumption most likely to be violated (staggered timing, ramp-in, a different estimand, a different population) and reports both numbers. A memo that quietly keeps the convenient one is an integrity flag.

    6. The memo would survive a hostile board member. One page. Effects and intervals in percentage points of completion, translated into completions per month where it helps. A recommendation per intervention, stated in the first three lines. Risks as risks. One sentence of "what would change this." No p-values, no jargon, no hedging that hides the recommendation.

    Two more things that separate top-band work in practice: the notebook runs top-to-bottom on a clean kernel with a fixed seed, and the plan hash in the memo matches the plan hash in the notebook. Both take two minutes and both are checked.

    9. Bonus tasks (early finishers / distinction seekers)¶

    1. Reproduce the peeking hazard on your own A/B data, then contrast a sequential method that would have been valid.
    2. Add a Rosenbaum-bounds sensitivity analysis quantifying how strong hidden bias must be to overturn the pilot conclusion.
    3. Package your causal_utils work as a documented mini-package (assignment, SRM, balance, sizing, DiD, refutation).
    4. Produce a second decision plot showing how the recommendation changes under the most pessimistic credible assumptions.

    10. Academic integrity¶

    Identical simulated seeds and identical results across submissions are checked. Work in your own notebook. Reuse causal_utils and your own Lab 1–7 code freely — that is the point of building it. The Causal badge requires ≥ 70 overall, capstone ≥ 70, and zero integrity flags.
