    Answer Key¶

    SDA-DSC-213 · Experimentation, A/B Testing and Causal Inference · SDAIA Academy Instructor document — do not distribute.

    Every number in this document was computed from the shipped datasets with the shipped causal_utils. If a participant's number differs by more than the stated tolerance, something is wrong with their analysis, not with the data — unless python environment_check.py fails its ground-truth smoke tests, in which case the seeds have moved and this whole document needs regenerating.

    How to use the tolerances. They are set so that a correct analysis with a defensible variation in method (two-proportion versus OLS+HC3, matching with a slightly different caliper, a different covariate list) lands inside, while the three deliberate traps in §6 land outside. A number outside tolerance is a prompt to ask "which rows did you analyse, and what did you control for?" — in that order, because those two questions explain almost every miss.

    Teaching note. Labs 3 and 7 call run_length(..., floor_days=14): the KSA working week means a run must cover whole weeks, and two of them, so that both weekday and weekend users are sampled in proportion.     The uploader is the interesting guardrail case: the one metric that moves against us survives the family correction, and is still not material. A participant who never tests session_duration_sec at all has skipped the family; a participant who reports "significant, therefore a breach" has skipped the business threshold. The correct write-up says the feature raises completion by 2.2 pp at a cost of about 4.4 seconds on a 159-second baseline — a 2.8% rise, statistically unambiguous on 49,653 users and a business judgement rather than a statistic. The exact count depends on the segment definition, so do not mark against a number: mark against whether BH was applied and whether survivors were labelled as hypotheses for a future test rather than as findings.

    4. The three deliberate traps¶

    Each trap is a real analytical error, planted so that falling in produces a plausible number rather than an obviously broken one. That is what makes them teachable: the participant cannot tell they are wrong by looking at the output.

    Trap 1 — analysing all assigned users instead of triggered users¶

    Where it bites. Lab 4, Lab 7 and the capstone. It is the most common error in the course and the most common error in industry.

    Why it is plausible. The all-assigned number is smaller but still positive and still highly significant. Nothing looks broken.

    Uploader experiment (injaz_experiment_results.csv):

    Correct — triggered Trap — all assigned
    n per arm 24,838 / 24,815 40,056 / 39,944
    Effect +2.16 pp +1.34 pp
    95% CI [+1.29, +3.04] [+0.65, +2.03]
    p 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ 1.4 × 10⁻⁴
    Matches the planted +2.2 pp? yes no — diluted by the trigger rate: 0.6207 × 2.16 = 1.34

    Autofill experiment (injaz_autofill_ab.csv), where the trap changes the verdict:

    Correct — triggered Trap — all assigned
    n per arm 24,736 / 24,774 34,984 / 35,016
    Effect +1.37 pp +0.97 pp
    95% CI [+0.49, +2.24] [+0.23, +1.70]
    Distance from the +1.0 pp threshold point estimate 0.4 pp above point estimate 0.03 pp below
    Effect on the memo HOLD/EXTEND, with a real effect that may or may not clear the bar the diluted point estimate now sits under the bar, so a participant who ran it reads "real but too small" and recommends stopping. Same data, opposite decision

    The arithmetic is exact: trigger rate 0.7073 × the planted +1.40 pp = +0.99 pp, and the observed all-assigned figure is +0.968 pp. Put that multiplication on the board — it is the cleanest demonstration of the trap in the whole course.

    The built-in A/A test. In both experiments the non-triggered stratum is a clean null by construction: −0.007 pp on the uploader and +0.007 pp on autofill. Those users cannot have been affected by a feature they never reached, so the stratum doubles as an A/A test on the randomisation itself. Have participants estimate the effect on triggered == 0 and read the ±0.01 pp back: it shows the dilution is arithmetic, not noise, and it validates the assignment at the same time.

    How to spot it. Check the reported n. Triggered n per arm is ≈ 24,800 (uploader) or ≈ 24,750 (autofill); all-assigned is ≈ 40,000 or ≈ 35,000. The sample size gives the trap away instantly, and on both files the estimate confirms it: the diluted figure is the correct one multiplied by the trigger rate.

    The fix, and the thing to say. Filter on triggered == 1. Then add the nuance: the all-assigned number is not meaningless, it is the intention-to-treat estimate, which answers "what happens if we ship this to everyone, including the people who never see it?" That is a legitimate question — it is just not the one that was asked, and reporting it as "the effect of the feature" is wrong. A participant who reports both, correctly labelled, is performing above the bar.

    Trap 2 — controlling for the support_calls mediator¶

    Where it bites. Lab 6 Task 5, PA-2, and any capstone regression where a participant adds "sensible" controls.

    Why it is plausible. support_calls correlates with completion and with digital literacy. It looks exactly like a confounder to anyone who has not drawn the DAG. Adding it changes the estimate by only about one percentage point, so the result still looks reasonable.

    Why it is wrong. In the data-generating process, support_calls is drawn after the outcome: it depends on latent literacy and on 1 − completed. It is downstream of both the treatment and the outcome — a mediator on one path and a collider on another. Conditioning on it blocks part of the causal path and opens a spurious one. Two errors, one variable.

    Online-filing effect (adjusting for digital_literacy_score and device_age_years):

    Specification Estimate What happened
    Correct adjustment set +10.90 pp the backdoor-adjusted estimate
    + support_calls +9.77 pp 10% of the effect erased

    Training-programme effect (same adjustment set):

    Specification Estimate What happened
    Correct adjustment set +6.92 pp close to the planted +6.0 pp ATT
    + support_calls +6.11 pp 12% of the effect erased

    How to spot it. Look for support_calls in any smf.ols formula or any covariate list. It is the only post-treatment variable in injaz_users.csv, so it is easy to grep for. In the capstone this caps criterion 5 at 70%.

    The fix, and the thing to say. Drop it, and derive the adjustment set from the DAG (InjazDAG(...).minimal_adjustment_set() returns {digital_literacy, device_age} and lists support_calls under "mediators — never adjust for these"). Then connect it to Day 2: this is the same rule as CUPED's pre-treatment requirement. A variable measured after the treatment can absorb the treatment's effect. Pre-treatment covariates remove noise; post-treatment covariates remove signal.

    Trap 3 — reading the TWFE ramp-in attenuation as the answer¶

    Where it bites. Lab 5b and the capstone's causal-evaluation section.

    Why it is plausible. TWFE gives +2.61 pp with a tight interval [+2.48, +2.74] and a p-value indistinguishable from zero. Nothing about the output suggests it is an underestimate. Every diagnostic passes: pre-trends are flat, the placebo is null, the standard errors are clustered.

    Why it is an underestimate. The programme was planted with a two-month ramp-in — 60% of the effect lands in the first two months — and it was rolled out in four staggered waves (periods 10, 13, 16, 19). TWFE averages the weak early months in with the mature ones, so the coefficient sits below the mature effect. The event study shows the whole story:

    Relative month Coefficient Reading
    −6 to −2 +0.30, +0.13, −0.14, +0.42, +0.26 pp flat — parallel trends supported
    −1 0 (baseline)
    0 +2.06 pp ramp-in, month 1
    +1 +1.64 pp ramp-in, month 2
    +2 to +8 +3.38, +3.24, +3.28, +3.41, +3.35, +3.22, +2.94 pp mature: mean +3.26 pp, just above the planted ATT
    Value
    Planted ATT +3.00 pp
    TWFE (the trap, if reported as "the effect") +2.61 pp, CI [+2.48, +2.74]
    Event-study ramp-in months (rel 0, +1) mean +1.85 pp
    Event-study mature months (rel ≥ +2) mean +3.26 pp
    Gap 0.39 pp, and the planted value sits outside the TWFE interval

    How to spot it. A participant reports +2.61 pp and stops. The tell is not the number — the number is correct — it is the absence of the event study, or an event study that is plotted and then not read.

    The fix, and the thing to say. Reporting +2.61 pp is not wrong; reporting it as "the effect of the reminder programme" without qualification is. Distinction- band work says: "TWFE gives +2.6 pp. The event study shows the programme ramps in over two months and matures at about +3.3 pp; TWFE averages the weak early months in, so +2.6 pp understates the steady-state effect. For a scale decision this error is in the safe direction." That sentence is also the motivation for the staggered-DiD capstone extension, where a Callaway–Sant'Anna or Sun–Abraham estimator recovers the mature effect directly.

    Note the direction. Unlike Traps 1 and 2, this one makes the intervention look worse than it is. It is worth saying so — participants learn to expect that all bias is self-serving, and this is the counterexample.
