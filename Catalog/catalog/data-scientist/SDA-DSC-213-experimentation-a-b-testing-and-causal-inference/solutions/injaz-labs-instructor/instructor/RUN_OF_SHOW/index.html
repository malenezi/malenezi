    Run of Show — minute-by-minute facilitator plan¶

    SDA-DSC-213 · Experimentation, A/B Testing and Causal Inference · SDAIA Academy 4 days × 5 learning hours · Instructor document · print double-sided

    How to read this document¶

    Each learning hour is 50 minutes of instruction + a 10-minute buffer. The buffer is not slack you may spend: it absorbs the overrun you will have, covers prayer and comfort breaks, and lets people move between rooms. The long break sits around Dhuhr, between Hours 3 and 4.

    Clock Block
    08:45 – 09:00 Room open · machines on · instructor smoke-test
    09:00 – 09:50 Hour 1
    09:50 – 10:00 Break
    10:00 – 10:50 Hour 2
    10:50 – 11:00 Break
    11:00 – 11:50 Hour 3
    11:50 – 12:40 Dhuhr and long break (50 min)
    12:40 – 13:30 Hour 4
    13:30 – 13:40 Break
    13:40 – 14:30 Hour 5
    14:30 – 14:45 Close-out · collect deliverables · preview tomorrow

    Adjust the whole grid, not individual hours, if Dhuhr moves seasonally — keep the long break aligned to it and keep the 50/10 rhythm. Announce the day's grid on the board every morning; a cohort that knows when the next break is stops asking.

    Slides. Deck: Experimentation_and_Causal_Inference_P6.1.04_SDAIA_Academy_Enriched.pptx. This document refers to deck sections (M1 … M7) rather than slide numbers, because slide numbering shifts between deck revisions. Figures are named by file, from figures/; they are the same images as in the deck, and they are what you should project during a lab when the deck is closed.

    Checkpoint. Every segment ends with a checkpoint: a thing that must be observably true before you move on. Check it by looking at two or three screens, not by asking "everyone good?" — the room will always say yes.

    Cut guidance. Every hour has an explicit "if you are running late, cut this". Cut in the order given. Two things are never cut: the freeze step in any lab, and the event-study / parallel-trends observation in Lab 5b.

    DAY 1 — Causal thinking and honest experiments¶

    Modules: M1 Causal Thinking & Potential Outcomes · M2 Experiment Design & Randomisation End-of-day deliverable: a selection-bias simulation + a fully specified, frozen Injaz experiment design doc.

    Hour 1 · 09:00–09:50 — "What actually works?" + kickoff + the Injaz golden thread¶

    Time Segment Instructor does Slides / assets
    09:00 The poll (5 min) Ask, before any introduction: "Injaz completion is 8 points higher for users who received an SMS reminder. Should we send reminders to everyone?" Take a show of hands. Let the room argue for three minutes. Do not resolve it. —
    09:05 Name what just happened (5 min) Someone will say "maybe reminders go to people who already engage." Write that sentence on the board verbatim and tell them it is the entire course, made rigorous. m1_confounding_triangle.png
    09:10 Course frame (10 min) Outcomes LO1–LO6, the 4-day shape, the assessment split (labs 30 / PA 20 / quiz 10 / capstone 40). State the rule now and repeat it daily: a correct "we cannot conclude" is worth full marks. Deck: cover + delivery plan
    09:20 The Injaz golden thread (12 min) Introduce the platform and the four questions (uploader, reminders, training, autofill). Emphasise: no throwaway examples; one notebook grows all week; the toolkit they build is the capstone's toolkit. Deck: M1 opener
    09:32 Failure story (8 min) Tell one real-shaped failure: a programme that looked beneficial because healthy people enrolled. Ask the room what evidence would have caught it. m5_ladder_of_evidence.png
    09:40 Environment check (10 min) Everyone runs python environment_check.py. Walk the room. Anyone red goes to the Colab fallback now, not at Lab 1. environment_check.py

    Checkpoint: every participant's screen shows RESULT: PASS, or is on Colab with the install cell run. Names of anyone still broken go on a list for the break.

    If you are running late, cut: the failure story (8 min) — the poll already did its work. Never cut the environment check; a broken machine costs an hour at 11:00 instead of ten minutes at 09:40.

    Hour 2 · 10:00–10:50 — Potential outcomes and the fundamental problem (M1)¶

    Time Segment Instructor does Slides / assets
    10:00 Two worlds (10 min) Define Y(1), Y(0), τᵢ = Yᵢ(1) − Yᵢ(0) on the board. Use one named Injaz user, not a population. m1_two_worlds.png
    10:10 The fundamental problem (8 min) Only one potential outcome is ever observed; causal inference is a missing-data problem where exactly half the data is missing by construction. Deck: M1 §3
    10:18 Estimands (10 min) ATE, ATT, CATE. Drill the discipline: name the estimand before touching data. "The ATT on completion for triggered users in Q2" is answerable; "the effect" is not. Deck: M1 §2
    10:28 The decomposition, derived live (14 min) Whiteboard: E[Y|T=1] − E[Y|T=0] = ATT + (E[Y(0)|T=1] − E[Y(0)|T=0]). Do it slowly, in full. This is the algebraic spine of the week. m1_bias_decomposition.png
    10:42 Why randomisation works (8 min) (Y(0), Y(1)) ⊥ T is manufactured, not hoped for. It also balances the confounders you never thought to measure — the property no matching model can buy. Deck: M1 §4

    Checkpoint: ask three participants at random to state the selection-bias term in words. If two of three cannot, re-derive it in two minutes before the break — Lab 1 measures this quantity directly and lands flat if the room has not internalised it.

    If you are running late, cut: the CATE half of the estimands segment (4 min) — it is re-introduced properly on Day 3. Never cut the live derivation.

    Hour 3 · 11:00–11:50 — Lab 1: simulate potential outcomes and selection bias¶

    Notebook: notebooks/lab1_start.ipynb · Data: simulated_potential_outcomes.parquet · Pairs

    Time Segment Instructor does Checkpoint
    11:00 Frame the lab (3 min) This is the only file in the course containing both potential outcomes. That is impossible in reality, and that is exactly why we start here: you can watch an estimator succeed or fail against a truth you can see. —
    11:03 Task 1–2 (15 min) Complete the simulator; implement both assignment mechanisms and the naive estimator. Walk the room. Planted ATE prints ≈ +0.050
    11:18 Task 3 (10 min) Compute the naive difference under each mechanism; write the markdown cell explaining why they differ although the true effect is identical. Self-selected ≈ +0.193, randomised ≈ +0.050
    11:28 Task 4 (8 min) Compute the selection-bias term directly and verify naive ≈ ATT + bias. Bias term ≈ +0.143
    11:36 Task 5 — the emotional core (9 min) The n ∈ {1k, 10k, 100k, 1M} sweep. Say nothing; watch faces as the "big data" estimate converges confidently to the wrong answer. Plot shows convergence to 0.193, not 0.050
    11:45 Debrief (5 min) One participant reads their three-sentence manager summary aloud. Land the line: more data buys precision, never truth. —

    Checkpoint before the long break: every pair's notebook prints the four numbers (0.050 / 0.193 / 0.050 / 0.143) and has the convergence plot.

    If you are running late, cut: Task 4's manual bias computation (8 min) — the simulator prints it anyway; assign it as an optional over-lunch exercise. Never cut Task 5. If you must choose, cut the debrief and let the plot speak.

    Common stumbles: naive randomised estimate far from 0.050 → n too small or unlucky seed, average over seeds. Self-selection estimate ≈ true ATE → opt-in is not actually correlated with literacy; print the correlation. Bias term ≈ 0 under self-selection → they revealed the wrong potential outcome; check observed = where(T==1, y1, y0).

    11:50 – 12:40 · Dhuhr and long break¶

    Use ten minutes of this to fix any machine still failing environment_check.py. Do not use it to prepare Hour 4; prepare Hour 4 the night before.

    Hour 4 · 12:40–13:30 — Experiment design and randomisation (M2)¶

    Time Segment Instructor does Slides / assets
    12:40 Anatomy of an experiment (8 min) Unit → assignment → trigger → exposure → outcome. Walk the diagram once, end to end, before any detail. m2_experiment_anatomy.png
    12:48 Randomisation unit (10 min) The decision tree: does the user return? do units interact? how coarse can you afford to go? Coarser units cost power; finer units cost validity. m2_randomisation_unit_tree.png
    12:58 SUTVA and interference (8 min) No interference, one version of the treatment. Shared devices, household spillover, region-to-region talk. Name the containment design for each: cluster, switchback. m2_interference.png
    13:06 OEC and guardrails (10 min) Exactly one OEC. Guardrails are a family and are tested as one. Introduce the trigger and say plainly that the analysis population is triggered users. Deck: M2 §metrics
    13:16 Assignment mechanics and SRM (14 min) Why hash rather than draw: deterministic, stateless, reproducible, independent across experiments via the salt. Then SRM: what it detects, and the rule that it is never adjusted away. m2_srm_diagnostic.png

    Checkpoint: the room can answer, without notes, "what is the OEC and how many should an experiment have?" and "what do you do when SRM fails?" The second answer must contain the words do not analyse.

    If you are running late, cut: the switchback design detail inside the SUTVA segment (4 min) and the balance-check preview (it is done hands-on in Lab 2 anyway).

    Hour 5 · 13:40–14:30 — Lab 2: design and randomise the uploader experiment¶

    Notebook: notebooks/lab2_start.ipynb · Data: injaz_users_pool.csv, injaz_experiment_results_srm_broken.csv · Pairs

    Time Segment Instructor does Checkpoint
    13:40 Task 1 — the design doc (10 min) They fill hypothesis, unit + justification, OEC, three guardrails, trigger, unit of analysis. Circulate and read two aloud, one strong and one thin, without naming authors. Every pair has a written unit justification of two sentences
    13:50 Task 2 — hash assignment (8 min) hash_assign on the 200k pool with salt="injaz-uploader-2026q2". Split ≈ 50/50 and stable across reruns
    13:58 Task 3 — SRM, both ways (7 min) Run SRM on the clean assignment (passes), then on the broken file (fails). Let them see what a real bug looks like. Clean p ≈ 0.61; broken chi² ≈ 16.5, p ≈ 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ → INVALID
    14:05 Task 4 — balance table (10 min) Build the balance table over age, literacy, prior sessions, device age, region (one-hot). Watch for the re-randomisation temptation. All |SMD| < 0.1; worst ≈ 0.009
    14:15 Task 5 — interference audit (5 min) Two plausible SUTVA violations and the design change each would require. Two written violations per pair
    14:20 Task 6 — freeze the plan (10 min) AnalysisPlan(...) → freeze_plan(). The hash is printed and written into the design doc. This is pre-registration in miniature and it is graded on Day 4. Every pair has a plan_hash; lab2_frozen_plan.json written

    Stop the room the moment someone proposes re-randomising an imbalanced covariate. Two minutes, standing up: re-rolling until the covariates look nice is choosing your assignment based on the data, and it biases the design. The correct response is adjust in analysis, never re-roll.

    Checkpoint before close: every pair has a printed plan_hash and a complete design doc.

    If you are running late, cut: Task 5's interference audit (5 min) — set it as a 10-minute homework and review it in Day 2 Hour 1. Never cut Task 6. A cohort that leaves Day 1 without a frozen plan has no Day 4.

    14:30–14:45 close-out: collect nothing; remind them the design doc travels with them all week. Preview Day 2: "tomorrow we find out how long this experiment has to run, and all the ways an honest analyst accidentally lies."

    DAY 2 — Powering and analysing experiments¶

    Modules: M3 Power Analysis & Sample Sizing · M4 Analysis Pitfalls End-of-day deliverable: a sample-size calculator + a correct, corrected analysis of the uploader experiment. PA-1 is assessed today.

    Hour 1 · 09:00–09:50 — Power, MDE and sample size (M3)¶

    Time Segment Instructor does Slides / assets
    09:00 Yesterday's homework (5 min) Two pairs read their interference audits. Thirty seconds each, then move. —
    09:05 Type I and Type II (10 min) The two errors, α and β, and why teams obsess over the first and ignore the second. Power = 1 − β = the probability of detecting a true effect of a given size. Introduce the Arabic term alongside: power / القوة الإحصائية. m3_type_i_ii.png
    09:15 The four-way relationship (10 min) α, power, effect size, n — fix any three and the fourth is determined. Drive this with the slider, not with algebra. m3_four_way_tradeoff.png
    09:25 The quadratic (10 min) n ∝ 1/MDE². Halve the MDE, quadruple the sample. Project the curve and leave it on screen. m3_mde_vs_n.png
    09:35 Traffic to calendar (10 min) Three things bite teams: n is per arm; not every eligible user triggers; a whole number of weeks is a floor. The KSA working week matters — Fri–Sat traffic is about 58% of a weekday's in the Injaz forecast. m3_run_length_pipeline.png
    09:45 CUPED preview (5 min) A pre-treatment covariate removes noise, not signal. Variance falls by ρ². Full treatment in Hour 3. m3_cuped_variance.png

    Checkpoint: ask the room "if you halve the MDE, what happens to n?" The answer four times should come back instantly, from several voices.

    If you are running late, cut: the CUPED preview (5 min) — Hour 3 covers it properly. Pin the MDE-vs-n curve to the wall; you will point at it for the rest of the course.

    Hour 2 · 10:00–10:50 — Lab 3: the sample-size calculator and run-length plan¶

    Notebook: notebooks/lab3_start.ipynb · Data: injaz_pre_period.csv, injaz_traffic_forecast.csv

    Time Segment Instructor does Checkpoint
    10:00 Task 1 (8 min) Implement sample_size_proportions; cross-check against statsmodels at baseline 0.55, MDE 0.02. Both agree; n/arm ≈ 9,670
    10:08 Task 2 (8 min) Plot required n vs MDE over [0.005, 0.05]; annotate the blow-up. The curve is on screen and annotated
    10:16 Task 3 (8 min) MDE achievable at live traffic in a 4-week window. MDE ≈ 0.006 → a 2-point lift is comfortably detectable
    10:24 Task 4 (8 min) Run-length plan for 2 arms, then 3; note the traffic multiplier and the whole-week floor. At MDE 0.02 both round to the 14-day whole-week floor the lab uses (arithmetic: 3 and 4 days) — rerun at MDE 0.005 to make the multiplier bite: 42 → 56 days
    10:32 Task 5 (10 min) Estimate CUPED variance reduction from the pre-period file; recompute n and run length. corr ≈ 0.37 → variance reduction ≈ 13.5%
    10:42 Task 6 (8 min) Write the one-paragraph sizing recommendation into the design doc. Every pair's design doc §6 is filled

    Watch for: a run length that looks impossibly short — they forgot the arm multiplier or the trigger rate. A negative or absurd n — the MDE pushed p₂ outside (0,1).

    Note on expected numbers: the CUPED reduction here is ≈13.5%, driven by a pre/post correlation of ≈0.37. If a pair reports 40%, they have used a post-treatment covariate. That is a teaching moment, not an arithmetic slip.

    If you are running late, cut: Task 4's three-arm variant (4 min) — state the multiplier verbally instead. Never cut Task 5; CUPED is a mandatory capstone requirement.

    Hour 3 · 11:00–11:50 — Analysis pitfalls: peeking and multiple testing (M4)¶

    Time Segment Instructor does Slides / assets
    11:00 Unit of analysis (10 min) Randomise users, analyse sessions, and your standard errors are fiction. Show the widening: same point estimate, honest interval. m4_unit_of_analysis.png
    11:10 Ratio metrics and the delta method (8 min) A random denominator breaks the ordinary variance formula. Deck: M4 §variance
    11:18 CUPED, properly (7 min) θ = cov(Y,X)/var(X); the covariate must be pre-treatment or the adjustment is biased. Same lesson as the mediator trap on Day 3, in a different costume. m3_cuped_variance.png
    11:25 Peeking, live (13 min) Run the peeking simulation in front of them. Nominal 5% becomes ≈25% at 20 looks. Then the rule: watch continuously only with a sequential method. m4_peeking_staircase.png
    11:38 Multiple testing (12 min) Bonferroni controls FWER; BH controls FDR. Which family is which: guardrails → Bonferroni; segment sweep → BH. Finish with Simpson's paradox. m4_correction_family.png, m4_simpsons_paradox.png

    Checkpoint: the room can state which correction goes with which family, and why they are different questions.

    If you are running late, cut: Simpson's paradox (5 min) — it reappears in the Day 3 DAG material. Never cut the live peeking run; it is the demonstration that changes behaviour.

    Hour 4 · 12:40–13:30 — Lab 4: analyse the uploader experiment correctly¶

    Notebook: notebooks/lab4_start.ipynb · Data: injaz_experiment_results.csv, injaz_pre_period.csv, injaz_sessions.csv.gz This is one of the two most overrun-prone labs. Protect it at full length.

    Time Segment Instructor does Checkpoint
    12:40 Task 1 — the effect (8 min) Two-proportion test and OLS+HC3 on triggered users; confirm identical point estimates. +2.2 pp, 95% CI [+1.3, +3.0], both methods agree
    12:48 The trap, sprung (4 min) Ask them to rerun on all 80,000 assigned users. The effect falls to +1.3 pp. Ask why before explaining: assigned users who never reached the step cannot have been affected. Both numbers in the notebook, correctly labelled
    12:52 Task 2 — CUPED (8 min) Adjust with prior_completion_rate; the interval narrows. +2.1 pp, CI [+1.3, +2.9]; variance reduction ≈ 13.5%
    13:00 Task 3 — guardrails (8 min) Four guardrails, Bonferroni across the family. Drop-off falls and survives correction; nothing regresses after correction. Correction applied, m stated
    13:08 Task 4 — the segment sweep (10 min) Let a pair announce "it works great for Android users in Riyadh!" Then apply BH and watch it evaporate. Survivors are hypotheses for the next test, not findings. BH applied; survivors labelled as hypotheses
    13:18 Task 5 — clustering (7 min) Session-level analysis of a user-randomised test: the interval widens from ±0.33 pp to ±0.52 pp on the same estimate. Clustered SE reported
    13:25 Task 6 — the decision paragraph (5 min) Lift, CI, guardrail verdict, and an explicit statistical-vs-practical sentence. Every pair has written it

    If you are running late, cut: Task 5 (7 min) — demo it from the solution notebook instead of having them type it; the concept survives, the practice does not. Do not cut Task 4; the BH moment is the highest-value 10 minutes of Day 2.

    Hour 5 · 13:40–14:30 — Reading results honestly + PA-1¶

    Time Segment Instructor does Assets
    13:40 CIs versus p-values (10 min) The interval tells you what has been ruled out; the p-value tells you almost nothing on its own. Practise the sentence: "not distinguishable from zero at this sample size". Deck: M4 close
    13:50 Practical significance (10 min) Introduce the decision threshold as a business quantity. Preview the capstone: an interval that excludes zero but straddles the threshold is the capstone's central lesson. m7_uncertainty_to_decision.png
    14:00 PA-1 — assessed, 30 min (30 min) Hand out the broken experiment: an SRM failure and a session-level analysis of a user-randomised test. They diagnose both defects and produce a corrected, correctly-powered analysis. Scored: diagnosis 40% · corrected analysis 40% · verification evidence 20%. injaz_experiment_results_srm_broken.csv, injaz_sessions.csv.gz

    Checkpoint: PA-1 scripts collected before anyone leaves. A participant who "fixed" the SRM by reweighting has the diagnosis wrong — mark it and address it in Day 3 Hour 1, because the same instinct will reappear in the capstone.

    If you are running late, cut: the CI-versus-p-value segment down to 5 minutes. PA-1 is assessed and cannot be shortened below 30 minutes — if you cannot protect 30 minutes, move PA-1 to the start of Day 3 Hour 1 and say so at close-out.

    14:30–14:45 close-out: preview Day 3 with one sentence: "tomorrow, everything we did today becomes impossible, and we do it anyway — carefully."

    DAY 3 — When you cannot randomise¶

    Modules: M5 Quasi-Experiments (matching, DiD, IV) · M6 Causal Graphs & Confounding End-of-day deliverable: a DiD estimate of the reminder rollout + a DAG-justified observational analysis with refutation tests.

    Hour 1 · 09:00–09:50 — Quasi-experiments and matching (M5)¶

    Time Segment Instructor does Slides / assets
    09:00 The ladder of evidence (8 min) RCT at the top; everything below buys a weaker guarantee in exchange for being possible. Every method today is an attempt to approximate a coin flip. m5_ladder_of_evidence.png
    09:08 Confounding, formally (10 min) Back to the Day 1 decomposition. Observational adjustment can only handle confounders you named and measured — randomisation handles the ones you never knew existed. m1_confounding_triangle.png
    09:18 Propensity scores (12 min) P(T=1 | X) as a balancing score; matching, weighting, and what each does with the units it cannot use. m5_propensity_overlap.png
    09:30 Overlap and common support (10 min) No comparable control means no counterfactual. Trimming changes the estimand, and the memo must say so. m5_propensity_overlap.png
    09:40 The assumption you are spending (10 min) Unconfoundedness given X is assumed and unverifiable. What can be checked: overlap, post-matching balance, and how strong a hidden confounder would have to be (Rosenbaum). Deck: M5 §assumptions

    Checkpoint: the room can say what matching can and cannot fix. If anyone says "matching removes bias", correct it on the spot: matching removes bias from the confounders you measured.

    If you are running late, cut: the weighting variants inside the propensity segment (4 min); IPW is demonstrated in the lab.

    Hour 2 · 10:00–10:50 — Lab 5a: match to evaluate the training programme¶

    Notebook: notebooks/lab5a_start.ipynb · Data: injaz_users.csv

    Time Segment Instructor does Checkpoint
    10:00 Task 1 — propensity and overlap (8 min) Estimate propensity on age, literacy, prior sessions, device age, region; plot overlap and identify common support. Overlap plot produced
    10:08 Task 2 — match (10 min) Nearest-neighbour with a caliper; post-matching balance table. Worst |SMD| falls from ≈0.57 to ≈0.03
    10:18 Task 3 — estimate (10 min) Matched ATT, cross-checked with IPW, contrasted with the naive difference. Naive +11.5 pp → matched ≈ +6.5 pp, IPW ≈ +6.6 pp
    10:28 Task 4 — refute (12 min) Random-common-cause, placebo outcome, and a Rosenbaum-bounds sensitivity statement. Note: the placebo outcome must not be in the adjustment set. Two refutations run; Γ ≈ 1.20–1.25* stated
    10:40 Task 5 — write the finding (10 min) "Naive +11.5 points → matched ≈ +6.5 points," with the caveat about unobserved confounders. Make the Day-1 connection explicit: this is the same headline, now defensible. Written finding, with the caveat

    Instructor note on Rosenbaum. Γ here is around 1.20–1.25 — the conclusion survives a modest hidden confounder but not a strong one. Do not let the room read that as failure. Say the honest sentence out loud: "this result would be overturned by an unmeasured variable that changed the odds of enrolling by about 20%, and I cannot rule that out."* That sentence is what a top-band capstone contains.

    If you are running late, cut: the IPW cross-check in Task 3 (5 min) and one of the two refutations in Task 4. Keep the sensitivity statement — it is a capstone requirement.

    Hour 3 · 11:00–11:50 — Difference-in-differences and instrumental variables (M5)¶

    Time Segment Instructor does Slides / assets
    11:00 Two lines (10 min) DiD as the difference of two before/after differences. Draw it before writing any regression. m5_did_two_lines.png
    11:10 Parallel trends (12 min) The identifying assumption, in the exact sentence they will be asked to say on Day 4. Untestable for the post period; supported by flat pre-trends. m5_event_study.png
    11:22 Event studies (8 min) How to read one: pre-period coefficients near zero, a jump at activation, and the shape of the ramp-in afterwards. m5_event_study.png
    11:30 Staggered rollouts, honestly (8 min) With waves and time-varying effects, TWFE is a weighted average of 2×2 comparisons that can use already-treated units as controls, with some negative weights. Flag the modern estimators; do not teach them here. Deck: M5 §staggered
    11:38 Instrumental variables (12 min) The valve metaphor; the three conditions; first-stage F; and the fact that IV identifies the LATE, for compliers only. m5_iv_valve.png

    Checkpoint: three participants, at random, state the parallel-trends assumption in one sentence. This is the exact question asked live during capstone briefings, so practise it now.

    If you are running late, cut: the staggered-rollout segment to 4 minutes (say it, do not derive it) — the ramp-in evidence appears in Lab 5b anyway.

    Hour 4 · 12:40–13:30 — Lab 5b: DiD on the regional reminder rollout¶

    Notebook: notebooks/lab5b_start.ipynb · Data: injaz_regions_panel.csv, injaz_regions_panel_pretrend.csv The second overrun-prone lab. Protect it at full length.

    Time Segment Instructor does Checkpoint
    12:40 Task 1 — the panel (10 min) Inspect 13 regions × 24 months; mark treated_post from the activation waves (periods 10, 13, 16, 19; four regions never treated). Panel shape and wave structure confirmed
