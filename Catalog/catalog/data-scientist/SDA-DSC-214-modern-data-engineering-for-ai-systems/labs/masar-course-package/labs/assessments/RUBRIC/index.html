    RUBRIC — Masar Mini-Lakehouse Capstone (100 points)¶

    SDA-DSC-214 · Instructor instrument. Not distributed to participants in this form — participants receive CAPSTONE_BRIEF.md, which states the criteria and weights.

    Field Value
    Total 100 points across 7 criteria
    Pass ≥ 70 · Distinction ≥ 90
    Bonus Extensions +5 max, capped at 100, only if mandatory scope ≥ 80
    Grading order Platform and repository first, demo second. The point of the course is that the platform speaks
    Scoring moment Repository and run evidence reviewed in Day 5 Hour 4 (collected at the end of the hour); demo scored live in Hour 5

    Scale note. Figures quoted in this document assume the full-scale generator run (--scale 1.0); the shipped sample is --scale 0.15 — 7,230 trips/day over 5 days (36,150 trips), 873,763 GPS pings, 3,450 drivers, 3,864 vehicles, 48 zones. Grains, ratios and file-count reductions hold at either scale; absolute row counts do not.

    0. How to use this instrument¶

    1. Before demos, run the participant's make pipeline from their submitted commit and open their BENCHMARKS.md, GOVERNANCE.md, MINI_LAKEHOUSE.md, DECISIONS.md and LAB8_LEAKAGE.md. Fill the worksheet in §9 as far as the artefacts allow. Most criteria are decidable here.
    2. During the demo, confirm the beats you could not verify statically and ask one live-verification question (§10).
    3. Score each criterion into a band, then pick a point within the band. Do not average sub-criteria arithmetically — the bands are holistic and the sub-criteria are evidence prompts, not a checklist to tally.
    4. Apply anti-pattern caps last (§8). A cap overrides the band.
    5. Moderate against §11 before releasing marks.

    Evidence hierarchy. When two sources disagree, trust in this order: (1) a run you executed, (2) a committed transcript with a matching commit SHA, (3) the demo, (4) the participant's prose. A claim that exists only at level 4 is unevidenced and scores at the Developing band for that sub-criterion however well written.

    1. Architecture & medallion contracts — 15 points¶

    What is being assessed: whether the layers are real boundaries with enforced contracts, or three folders with data in them.

    Band Points Observable evidence
    Distinction 14–15 Bronze / silver / gold cleanly separated; no gold table reads a bronze path (verifiable by grep and by the DAG graph). MINI_LAKEHOUSE.md states for every table: grain · source · write strategy · the file and line where the contract is enforced · who may read it · what breaks if it breaks. Enforcement is in code — _assert_grain, CHECK constraints, unique/not_null tests — not prose. Lineage columns present on every bronze table and used at least once in an investigation the participant can describe
    Proficient 11–13 Layers separated and contracts documented, with one gap: a table whose grain is stated but not enforced, or one enforcement location undocumented, or a single defensible bypass (e.g. an observability task reading bronze) that the participant identifies and justifies
    Developing 8–10 Layers exist but contracts are prose only; grain is asserted nowhere in code; one gold table derives from bronze; or MINI_LAKEHOUSE.md documents grain and source but omits enforcement for most tables
    Insufficient 0–7 Layers muddled; gold built from bronze as the normal path; no documented contracts; bronze mutated in place

    Prompts for the marker: Which file enforces silver.trips grain? Show me a gold table's grain assertion. What is in bronze that is not in silver, and why?

    2. ELT & incrementality — 15 points¶

    What is being assessed: whether a re-run is safe and a late-arriving trip is counted exactly once.

    Band Points Observable evidence
    Distinction 14–15 Incremental model merges on trip_id with a stated, justified lookback (3 days) — the justification appears in DECISIONS.md. ≥ 6 tests passing including unique and not_null on the silver key plus a relationships test. Source freshness declared and shown firing. Backfill proven with both runs: inserted -> 1 then inserted -> 0. The naive-filter counter-experiment is recorded with both counts side by side. Row derivation (96,400 → 42,038) accounted for step by step
    Proficient 11–13 Incremental merge works and the backfill is demonstrated, but the test suite is thin (6 tests, all generic), or the freshness SLA is declared but never shown firing, or the counter-experiment is described rather than run
    Developing 8–10 Incremental configured but the lookback is absent or arbitrary; the backfill is asserted, not shown; fewer than 6 tests; the participant cannot account for their own row loss
    Insufficient 0–7 Full-refresh only; a late-arrival bug present (date = current_date()); no tests on the silver key

    Prompts: Run dbt test in front of me. Why 3 days and not 1 or 7? Where did 6,162 rows go between bronze dedup and silver?

    3. Delta Lake mastery — 15 points¶

    What is being assessed: whether the table defends itself, whether re-running is a no-op, and whether retention is a decision rather than a default.

    Band Points Observable evidence
    Distinction 14–15 positive_fare and valid_duration CHECK constraints visible in SHOW TBLPROPERTIES; three negative-write tests captured with their exact error text and the row count shown unchanged. MERGE idempotency shown as updated=5 / updated=0, with the participant volunteering that a commit is still written on the second run. Time-travel diff of two versions included. VACUUM ... RETAIN 168 HOURS with a DRY RUN first; retentionDurationCheck enabled; the retention justified against all three inputs (audit window · PDPL erasure SLA · storage cost) with a stated trigger for changing it. PDPL erasure demonstrated across every affected table, silver.trips_quarantine included. OPTIMIZE/ZORDER with numFiles before/after ≥ 10× and a measured read-time improvement
    Proficient 11–13 Constraints, idempotent MERGE, time travel and OPTIMIZE all present; retention stated but justified on one input only, or the erasure omits the quarantine table, or the negative-write errors are summarised rather than captured
    Developing 8–10 Delta used as a file format: constraints absent or only one present; MERGE works but idempotency shown by a single run; VACUUM run with an unexamined default; no time-travel evidence
    Insufficient 0–7 No constraints; non-idempotent writes; VACUUM RETAIN 0; _delta_log manipulated by hand

    Prompts: Run your corrections MERGE twice, now. Why 168 hours? What else holds this rider's data?

    4. Streaming — 15 points¶

    What is being assessed: whether exactly-once is engineered or hoped for, and whether the window is about when things happened.

    Band Points Observable evidence
    Distinction 14–15 One dedicated checkpointLocation per query under ./lakehouse/_checkpoints/<query_name>, no sharing; txnAppId/txnVersion idempotent-write guard present. Kill/restart proof table with three counts (before, immediately after a mid-batch kill, after drain) and pipeline-introduced duplicates = 0, with source duplicates (~0.4%) explicitly distinguished. Event-time window with a stated watermark, the beyond-watermark drop count recorded and non-zero, and the watermark value defended at both ends. The 2.5.0 m/s normalisation applied and its detection explained. OPTIMIZE run on the streaming sink with numFiles before/after
    Proficient 11–13 Streams reliably with a dedicated checkpoint and event-time windowing, but the dedup story is thin (watermark chosen without defence, or the drop count is zero and unexplained), or the kill was taken between batches, or the source/pipeline duplicate distinction is stated without evidence
    Developing 8–10 Stream runs and lands data; checkpoint present but a watermark is missing (unbounded state), or the kill/restart is asserted rather than shown
    Insufficient 0–7 Duplicates on restart; processing-time aggregation; no checkpoint or a shared one; the sink deleted to make counts look right

    Prompts: Kill it now and restart it — read me the count. Which of your two queries owns that checkpoint directory? Why five minutes and not thirty?

    5. Quality, observability, governance — 15 points¶

    What is being assessed: whether the gate acts, whether the signals fire, and whether governance is enforced by code rather than described in a document.

    Band Points Observable evidence
    Distinction 14–15 GX suite with ≥ 7 expectations, versioned and reviewed; data docs rendered and committed. The gate acts on both paths: a null-key batch blocked (0 promoted, 0 quarantined) and a bad-fares batch quarantined (120 isolated, 41,976 promoted), with silver.trips_quarantine holding real rows and a reason column. All four observability signals shown firing on injected faults, including the distribution drift on fare_sar by city and on speed_kmh by producer_version. The participant identifies the currency-shift case as not quarantinable and proposes a third response class. PII classification complete with differentiated retention by lawful basis; retention job runs; erasure runbook names every table
    Proficient 11–13 Gate acts on both paths and the suite is adequate, but one observability signal is described rather than demonstrated, or the quarantine has no reason column, or the classification applies one blanket retention to all personal data
    Developing 8–10 Suite exists and runs; the gate alerts but promotes anyway, or blocks everything indiscriminately; observability is row-level only; governance is a document with no enforcement
    Insufficient 0–7 Alerts only, no action; no quarantine table or an empty one; raw PII retained with no policy; --baseline auto drift checks presented as working monitoring

    Prompts: Show me a row in quarantine and tell me why it is there. Make a signal fire. Why do rider and driver retention differ?

    6. Orchestration & recovery — 15 points¶

    What is being assessed: whether the platform can be run and recovered by someone who is not the author.

    Band Points Observable evidence
    Distinction 14–15 One DAG, dependency-ordered land >> gate >> silver >> observability >> [3 gold] >> maintain, every task idempotent and retryable. make pipeline runs from a clean clone and writes a timestamped log. Injected mid-DAG failure: downstream tasks UPSTREAM_FAILED, gold unchanged (before == during by checksum). Full re-run reproduces the identical end state (before == after, checksums included) — and the participant re-runs the whole DAG, not the failed task. They can explain why re-running land_bronze is safe in three parts (bronze contract · duplication stops at the keyed MERGE · txnAppId on the stream). Maintenance is a DAG task
    Proficient 11–13 DAG runs end to end with correct dependencies and one-command execution; recovery demonstrated but partial — the failed task is re-run rather than the DAG, or idempotency is proven by row counts without checksums
    Developing 8–10 Tasks orchestrated but some steps still run by hand; the failure drill is described rather than performed; maintenance run manually
    Insufficient 0–7 Hand-run sequence of notebooks or scripts; non-idempotent gold (append aggregates); no recovery path

    Prompts: Delete a gold table and recover it with one command. Why is re-running land_bronze safe? Show me the during snapshot.

    7. Serving (AI + BI) & demo — 10 points¶

    What is being assessed: whether the two consumers read one truth, and whether the participant can defend the honest number.

    Band Points Observable evidence
    Distinction 9–10 gold.eta_features has exactly the 13 CONVENTIONS columns at one row per trip_id; all 13 classified in LAB8_LEAKAGE.md with written justifications; every LEAKAGE/STALE/UNAVAILABLE column fixed at the computation, not by renaming or dropping. Both metrics recorded (pre-fix inflated, post-fix honest) with a paragraph on why the drop is correct. Offline == online proven across all zone keys and columns, and shown to exit 1 on an injected divergence. Star reconciles with the demand mart at rel_diff = 0. PDPL boundary asserted (no rider_id, no raw lat/lon, no hashes). Demo is crisp, rehearsed, and finishes inside 6 minutes
    Proficient 7–8 Both consumers work; the classification misses one or two columns (typically weather_condition or driver_recent_trip_count), or reconciliation is asserted rather than shown, or consistency is spot-checked on one key rather than all; demo runs slightly long or needs a retry
    Developing 5–6 Serving works mechanically but a leak remains, or the online path recomputes a feature, or the star does not reconcile and the participant adjusts a number rather than finding the divergent filter
    Insufficient 0–4 Leakage present and undetected; online ≠ offline; raw personal identifiers served; no demo or a demo of someone else's platform

    Prompts: Which column leaked, which bound caused it, and which diagnostic showed you? Break Redis and run your consistency check.

    8. Anti-pattern flags — automatic caps¶

    Any one of these caps the named criterion at 70% of its points, regardless of the band the evidence would otherwise support. Caps are not negotiable and are not averaged away. Record every flag on the worksheet with its evidence line; a flag with no evidence line is not a flag.

    # Anti-pattern How it presents Caps
    A1 Feature leakage A feature computed from data at or after feature_ts; an inclusive window upper bound (0); a metered/outcome column used as an input; a metric > 0.85 correlated feature undefended Criterion 7 → max 7.0
    A2 Non-idempotent gold .mode("append") on an aggregate; overwrite without replaceWhere; a double run changing row counts; a grain assertion absent or failing Criterion 6 → max 10.5
    A3 VACUUM RETAIN 0 Zero or sub-168h retention; retentionDurationCheck disabled anywhere in the repository Criterion 3 → max 10.5
    A4 Alert-only quality gate Validation failures logged or emailed while the batch promotes regardless; no quarantine table, or one that is empty Criterion 5 → max 10.5
    A5 Raw rider IDs in BI outputs rider_id, raw lat/lon, national_id_hash or phone_hash present in any gold product, serving view, or Redis key Criterion 7 → max 7.0 and triggers the PDPL badge check (see EVALUATION_PLAN.md)
    A6 Streaming without a dedicated checkpoint No checkpointLocation; two queries sharing one; a checkpoint under /tmp or inside the sink table Criterion 4 → max 10.5

    Two additional caps specific to this instrument:

    # Condition Caps
    A7 Benchmark, quarantine or metric artefacts identical to another participant's Overall score withheld pending integrity review (see §12)
    A8 A demo of a platform the participant cannot explain when asked one live-verification question Criterion 7 → max 7.0, and re-verify criteria 3, 4 and 6 from the repository rather than the demo

    10. Live-verification questions — pick exactly one¶

    The assessment notes authorise one live check per participant. Choose the one that targets the criterion you were least able to decide from the repository. Say up front: "I am going to ask you to verify one claim live."

    Target Question Distinction answer
    Streaming (4) "Kill the stream and restart it. Read me the count before and after." Kills mid-batch, reads three numbers, distinguishes source from pipeline duplicates unprompted
    Serving (7) "Run the leakage drill and explain the inflated metric." Names the specific column, the specific window bound, the specific diagnostic, and both numbers — without opening a file
    Delta (3) "Run your corrections MERGE twice and read the metrics." updated=5 then updated=0, and notes that a commit is still written on the second run
    Orchestration (6) "Delete a gold table and bring it back with one command." make pipeline; explains why a full re-run is the recovery procedure rather than a targeted repair
    Quality (5) "Make an observability signal fire." Injects the fault, shows the alert, and names what the signal would not have caught
    Architecture (1) "Which file and line enforces the grain of gold.driver_daily?" Names it from memory; opens it to confirm rather than to search

    11. Moderation guide — keeping two graders consistent¶

    Why this section exists. Seven holistic criteria and two graders will drift. The observed drift on this rubric is systematic and predictable, and these five practices remove most of it.

    11a. Calibrate before you grade¶

    Before the first demo, both graders independently score the same two submissions — one expected mid-band, one expected strong — and compare. Discuss every criterion where you differ by more than 2 points. Do not proceed until you agree on what "enforced in code" and "proven" mean for this cohort. Twenty minutes here saves an appeal later.

    11b. The four disagreements that actually happen¶

    Disagreement The rule to apply
    "Documented" vs "enforced" — one grader accepts a stated grain in MINI_LAKEHOUSE.md, the other demands an assertion in code Enforcement means a failing path exists: an assertion, a constraint, or a test that would fail if the contract broke. Prose is documentation and belongs in the Proficient band, not Distinction
    "Shown" vs "asserted" — a participant writes that the backfill worked A claim needs a transcript with numbers, or you ran it. "It works on my machine" is level-4 evidence (§0) and scores Developing for that sub-criterion
    Partial evidence across labs — the kill/restart is in LAB5_NOTES.md but not repeated for the capstone Lab evidence counts, provided the commit SHA is in the same repository and the code has not changed since. The capstone is integration, not re-performance. Check git log on the streaming file
    Effort vs outcome — a participant who clearly worked hard but whose platform does not run Score the platform. Note the effort in §E for the badge conversation. The rubric measures a deliverable, and saying so kindly is part of the job

    11c. Anchor statements¶

    Read these before scoring. They fix the ends of the scale.

    A 15/15 on any criterion means: an engineer who has never seen this repository could reproduce the claim from the repository alone, in under ten minutes, without asking the author a question.

    A 70% score means: the platform works and would survive a normal week, but a specific named failure mode would take it down and the participant has not defended against it.

    A sub-70 score means: a failure mode is present in the submitted platform right now, not merely undefended.

    11d. Score independently, then reconcile¶

    Both graders score all seven criteria without seeing each other's sheet. Then compare.

    Gap Action
    ≤ 3 points total Take the mean. No discussion needed
    4–9 points total Discuss only the criteria differing by ≥ 2. Each grader names the evidence line, not the impression. Re-score together
    ≥ 10 points total, or a disagreement about a band on any criterion A third reviewer reads the repository blind (no scores shown) and scores the disputed criteria. Take the median of three
    Disagreement about an anti-pattern flag The flag stands or falls on the evidence line. If neither grader can write the line, there is no flag

    11e. Order effects and fatigue¶

    • Demos are scored in the order they are given; graders reliably score the first two submissions harshly and the last two generously. Re-check the first two after the last one.
    • Do not score more than five demos without a break.
    • Never score a participant you paired with during labs on criterion 7 alone; swap with the co-grader.
    • Record the score before discussing the participant with anyone, including them.

    11f. Feedback that is worth reading¶

    For each criterion below Distinction, write one sentence in this shape:

    "[What you observed] — [what would have moved it a band] — [where in your repo that would live]."

    Example: "Your backfill is described in LAB3_NOTES.md but the second run's inserted -> 0 is not captured — one more paste of the repeat run would have moved ELT to Distinction — it belongs beside the first transcript in the same file."

    Participants act on that. They do not act on "good work, thin evidence".

    12. Integrity handling within this instrument¶

    Do not confront a suspected integrity issue during a demo. Score the criteria from the repository, tick A7, write the evidence line (which artefacts, which other repository, which identical values), mark Integrity flag in §E, and hand the sheet to the lead instructor. The badge decision is made in EVALUATION_PLAN.md, not here. A participant whose flag is not upheld receives their uncapped score with no note in the record.

    13. Criterion-to-requirement-to-lab map¶

    Use this when a participant asks why a criterion scored where it did, or when deciding which lab to point a re-sit at.

    Criterion Pts Capstone req. LO Primary lab PA that pre-tests it
    1 Architecture & medallion contracts 15 R1, R2 LO1, LO7 LAB-01, LAB-07 —
    2 ELT & incrementality 15 R3 LO3 LAB-03 —
    3 Delta Lake mastery 15 R4, R2 LO4 LAB-04 PA-1
    4 Streaming 15 R5 LO5 LAB-05 PA-2
    5 Quality, observability, governance 15 R6 LO6 LAB-06 —
    6 Orchestration & recovery 15 R7 LO7 LAB-07 —
    7 Serving (AI + BI) & demo 10 R8 LO8 LAB-08 PA-3

    A participant who failed PA-1, PA-2 or PA-3 is at elevated risk on the corresponding criterion. Tell them so before Day 5, not after.
