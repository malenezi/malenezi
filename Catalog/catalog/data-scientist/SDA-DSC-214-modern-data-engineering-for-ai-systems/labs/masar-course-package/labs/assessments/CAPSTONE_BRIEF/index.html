    Capstone — The Masar Mini-Lakehouse¶

    Participant-facing brief. SDA-DSC-214 · Modern Data Engineering for AI Systems · SDAIA Academy

    Field Value
    Title The Masar Mini-Lakehouse — a production data platform for a Saudi mobility operator
    Weight 40% of the course grade (labs 30% · PA-1/2/3 20% · quiz 10%)
    Work mode Individual repository; pairs may share a build during labs but each participant submits and demos their own
    Build window Day 1 Hour 3 through Day 5 Hour 4 — every lab is a component, not a detour
    Demo Day 5 Hour 5 · 6 minutes · rubric-scored live
    Pass ≥ 70 overall. Distinction ≥ 90. Extensions add up to +5 (capped at 100) only when mandatory scope is ≥ 80
    Graded from The running platform and the repository first; the demo second

    1. Scenario¶

    You are the data platform engineer for Masar (مسار), the national smart-mobility operator running ride-hailing and public-transit services across Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, Mecca and Medina.

    The analytics organisation hands you raw feeds and two non-negotiable demands.

    The feeds. Batch trip records and driver and vehicle masters arrive as daily CSV. GPS telemetry arrives continuously as a Kafka event stream at roughly 25 pings per trip — 1.2 million events a day — from an SDK fleet that is not fully version-aligned. Payments settle asynchronously. Weather lands hourly, after the hour it describes.

    The two demands. - The ETA and demand-forecasting squad needs an AI feature service: point-in-time-correct features, offline for training and online for inference at sub-50 ms, with offline and online provably identical. - The operations leadership reviews a BI dashboard every morning at 07:00 and makes dispatch and surge decisions from it. Numbers that disagree with the data-science team's numbers are worse than no numbers.

    Your mandate. Deliver a Mini-Lakehouse both teams can trust — because the platform proves its own correctness, freshness, and governance without a human vouching for it. Saudi PDPL is a first-class constraint, not a Day-5 afterthought: rider identifiers and precise GPS are personal data, and the platform must be able to classify them, minimise them, retain them lawfully, and erase them on request.

    The question every design decision must answer:

    "What happens to Masar's AI system if THIS part of the data platform fails?"

    Everything you built in Labs 1–8 is a component. The capstone is the integration, the proof, and your own extension.

    2. Mandatory requirements¶

    Eight requirements. Each maps to a learning outcome and to a rubric criterion. All eight are in scope; none is optional.

    # Requirement LO Rubric criterion Built in
    R1 Medallion architecture. Bronze (append-only, lineage columns on every table) → silver (conformed, one row per key) → gold (marts + feature table). Layer contracts documented and enforced in code. No layer bypassed — no gold table reads bronze. LO1, LO7 Architecture & medallion contracts (15) Labs 1, 3, 7
    R2 Compute–storage discipline. Data in an object-storage-style layout. A BENCHMARKS.md cost and scan analysis from your runs. Gold marts OPTIMIZEd and ZORDERed with a measured file-count reduction. LO2 Architecture (15) + Delta (15) Labs 1, 2, 4
    R3 ELT pipeline. dbt (or Spark) staging → silver, incremental merge on trip_id with a lookback window; source-freshness SLA plus ≥ 6 data tests; a demonstrated late-arrival backfill. LO3 ELT & incrementality (15) Lab 3
    R4 Delta Lake. silver.trips as constrained Delta (schema + CHECK constraints); idempotent MERGE upserts proven twice; a time-travel audit diff; OPTIMIZE/VACUUM with a justified retention; one demonstrated PDPL erasure. LO4 Delta Lake mastery (15) Lab 4
    R5 Streaming. Kafka GPS topic → Structured Streaming → bronze.gps_events, exactly-once with a dedicated checkpoint per query; a kill/restart proof of zero pipeline duplicates; event-time windowed demand with a watermark. LO5 Streaming (15) Lab 5
    R6 Quality, observability, governance. A Great Expectations suite that acts — fail-fast on integrity, quarantine on partial, always alert; freshness / volume / schema-drift / distribution-drift checks that each fire on an injected fault; PII classification and an automated retention job; rendered data docs as evidence. LO6 Quality, observability, governance (15) Lab 6
    R7 Orchestration. The full medallion as one dependency-ordered, idempotent DAG, runnable by one command, with a demonstrated idempotent recovery from an injected mid-DAG failure. LO7 Orchestration & recovery (15) Lab 7
    R8 Serving. A point-in-time-correct feature table served offline and materialised online with consistency proven across every key; a conformed star schema that reconciles with the demand mart; a PDPL-safe serving view. LO8 Serving (AI + BI) & demo (10) Lab 8

    Layer contracts you must state and enforce¶

    Layer Contract
    bronze.* Append-only; schema as received; carries _ingested_at, _source_file, _batch_id; never mutated; readable by platform engineers only
    silver.trips One row per trip_id; enforced schema plus positive_fare and valid_duration CHECK constraints; timezone and units conformed; completed valid trips only; rejects routed to silver.trips_quarantine; readable by data scientists
    silver.vehicle_positions One row per event_id; event-time semantics with a stated watermark; speed_kmh always in km/h (the 2.5.0 m/s incident normalised); accuracy_m ≤ 50
    gold.* Documented grain, enforced by an assertion in code; idempotent write strategy; readable by analysts and the model service; no raw personal identifiers

    3. Extension menu — choose at least one¶

    Extensions earn up to +5 bonus, capped at 100, and only when mandatory scope already scores ≥ 80. Choose depth over breadth: one extension done to production standard scores better than three sketched.

    # Extension What "done" looks like Effort
    E1 Offline/online consistency check wired into CI consistency_check.py runs in CI across all zone keys and all online columns, exits non-zero on divergence, and you show it failing on an injected mismatch Low — highest value per minute; most commonly chosen
    E2 A leakage-lint that fails the pipeline A DAG task that inspects feature window bounds and fails the run if any frame includes non-prior data; demonstrated failing on a deliberately widened bound Medium
    E3 Multi-city 7-day backfill make pipeline-backfill FROM=... TO=... over 7 days; prove the backfilled state is byte-identical (checksums) to incremental runs over the same window Medium
    E4 A second streaming consumer group An independent consumer group off masar.gps.pings driving a live console demand view, with its own checkpoint, running simultaneously with the bronze ingest Medium
    E5 Direct-serve endpoint benchmarked A Trino / Databricks-SQL direct-serve path against gold, benchmarked head-to-head with the materialised mart (p50/p95, bytes scanned) and a written recommendation High

    5. Deliverables¶

    Submit a single git repository URL containing all six deliverables. Everything is graded from the repository and a live run; nothing is graded from slides.

    # Deliverable Must contain
    D1 Git repository with full commit history Checkpoint commits from each lab; a readable history (not one "final" commit); make pipeline as the one-command interface
    D2 A runnable Mini-Lakehouse All bronze / silver / gold tables produced by a clean run, with documented grains and contracts in MINI_LAKEHOUSE.md
    D3 BENCHMARKS.md The course benchmark tables filled from your runs: scan/cost (M2), small-files before/after OPTIMIZE (M4), streaming kill/restart dedup (M5), gold task rows and idempotency (M7), serving latency and consistency (M8). See BENCHMARKS_MASTER.md for target ranges
    D4 GOVERNANCE.md PII classification table (column → classification → access level → retention → PDPL basis); the retention policy with its justification; the erasure runbook naming every table holding a rider's data; a demonstrated erasure with its transcript
    D5 The 6-minute demo One-command pipeline run · streaming kill/restart with zero duplicates · quality-gate quarantine · time-travel audit · AI and BI consumers reading gold
    D6 DECISIONS.md Five one-paragraph engineering decisions with rationale and what would change them. Suggested set: partition-key choice · VACUUM retention · stream/batch boundary · online store yes/no · feature window and lookback

    Also expected in the repository (evidence the rubric looks for, not separately graded): LAB1_NOTES.md … LAB8_NOTES.md, LAB8_LEAKAGE.md, rendered GX data docs, and the pipeline run logs under logs/.

    6. Milestones¶

    Milestones are gates, not suggestions. A missed gate is recoverable; an unnoticed missed gate is not — the Day-5 assembly assumes M-A through M-C are green.

    Milestone Content Due Gate — how it is checked
    M-A Bronze landing (batch + stream) + profiling End Day 2 Instructor spot-check: three bronze tables exist with all three lineage columns; a re-run grows history rather than replacing it; ≥ 3 swamp risks documented in LAB1_NOTES.md
    M-B ELT silver + Delta constraints + tests End Day 3 dbt test green with ≥ 6 tests; silver.trips is constrained Delta (SHOW TBLPROPERTIES lists positive_fare and valid_duration); MERGE run twice shows updated=5 then updated=0; late-arrival backfill demonstrated
    M-C Streaming exactly-once + quality gate + governance End Day 4 Kill/restart demo with 0 pipeline duplicates; a dirty batch shown blocked (null keys) and another quarantined (bad fares) with rows visible in silver.trips_quarantine; PII classification table drafted
    M-D Orchestrated DAG + gold + serving Day 5, H2–H3 make pipeline runs end to end from a clean state; an injected mid-DAG failure leaves gold unchanged and a full re-run reproduces the identical state (checksums); gold.eta_features has exactly the 13 CONVENTIONS columns
    M-E Demo + submission Day 5, H5 Repository URL submitted before Hour 5 begins; live 6-minute demo; rubric scored

    If you are behind at a gate, say so at the gate. Fast-forward tags (lab5-start, lab7-start, …) exist for exactly this and using one costs you nothing on the rubric — the capstone is graded on the platform you submit, not on how you got there. Silently arriving at Day 5 without a working silver table is the only unrecoverable path.

    8. Demo script — 6 minutes, minute by minute¶

    You will be stopped at 6:00. Rehearse it once; the difference between a 6-minute demo and a 9-minute demo is entirely rehearsal.

    Before you start: terminals open and cd'd, Kafka and Redis up, the pipeline already run once so tables exist, and a second terminal pre-loaded with the kill/restart producer. Do not run a cold build live.

    Time Beat What you show What you say
    0:00–0:45 The platform in one command make pipeline starting; the task log scrolling: land_bronze → quality_gate → build_silver → observability → three gold tasks → optimize_vacuum "One command, eight tasks, dependency-ordered. Every task is idempotent, so this is also my recovery procedure." Name the one architectural decision you are proudest of
    0:45–1:45 Streaming: kill and restart Pre-kill count → Ctrl-C mid-batch → post-kill count → restart → drained count. Point at the checkpoint path "110,000 pings in, 110,000 rows in bronze, zero pipeline duplicates. Exactly-once is the composition of Kafka's replayable offsets and Delta's atomic commit, and it lives in this directory." Distinguish source duplicates (0.4%, a data problem) from pipeline duplicates (0, an engineering problem)
    1:45–2:45 The gate acts Run the null-key dirty batch → GateBlocked, 0 promoted. Run the bad-fares batch → 120 quarantined, 41,976 promoted. SELECT * FROM silver.trips_quarantine LIMIT 3 showing the reason column "Integrity failure blocks, because the MERGE key is null and quarantining would relocate the damage without containing it. A 0.25% value failure quarantines, because blocking would take the dashboard down over 120 rows."
    2:45–3:30 Time travel and PDPL DESCRIBE HISTORY → the version diff showing five corrected fares → the erasure: rider present at version n−1, absent at n → the retention number "A regulator asks what this table held three weeks ago. It can answer, because VACUUM retains 168 hours and I can justify that number three ways."
    3:30–4:30 Two consumers, one truth The BI dashboard query returning the star; the demand mart returning the same revenue at rel_diff = 0; redis-cli GET masar:features:zone:ZN-RUH-014; consistency_check PASS across all keys "Same silver, two contracts. The star and the demand mart reconcile because they share conformed dimensions. Online equals offline because the online path copies, never recomputes — and here is the check that proves it for every key."
    4:30–5:15 The leakage story The pre-fix metric, the diagnostic that exposed the leak, the post-fix metric "R² 0.943 became 0.712 and that is the correct outcome. The first number was the model reading the answer — distance_km is metered at dropoff and correlates 0.947 with the label. The second number is what the platform can deliver at 07:14:33."
    5:15–5:45 Recovery, and your extension The injected-failure snapshot diff (before == during, before == after), then 20 seconds on your extension "Gold never moved while silver was broken, and a full re-run reproduced the exact state including checksums."
    5:45–6:00 Close One sentence Answer the course question for your own platform: what happens to Masar's AI system if this part fails — and what your build does about it

    Demo rules - Pre-run everything that takes more than 20 seconds; show artefacts, not progress bars. - If something fails live, say what you expected, what you see, and what you would check first. A calm diagnosis scores better than a lucky rerun. - The assessor may interrupt once to ask you to verify a claim live — most often the kill/restart or the leakage explanation. Expect it.

    9. Academic integrity¶

    The capstone is graded on your platform producing your numbers. The rules are short and they are enforced.

    What is expected and encouraged - Using the course starter code, the lab reference solutions, and the checkpoint tags. This is a build course; reusing the labs is the design. - Pair work during labs, discussion at any time, and helping a colleague debug. - External documentation, blog posts and AI assistants for understanding and for writing code you then run and verify.

    What is required - Every number in BENCHMARKS.md comes from a run on your machine, with your hardware and Spark version named. Benchmark numbers are hardware-dependent; identical figures across repositories are the primary integrity signal and are checked automatically. - Every quarantine artefact, run log and data-docs render is from your own run. Identical quarantine artefacts across repositories are checked. - DECISIONS.md is in your own words and defends your choices; a decision you cannot defend when asked scores zero for that entry regardless of how it reads. - Any code you did not write and did not adapt is attributed in the file header, including code from an AI assistant. - If you fast-forward to a checkpoint tag, say so in your README. It costs no marks.

    What is prohibited - Submitting another participant's repository, or a repository with commits authored by another participant, in whole or in part. - Copying benchmark, quarantine or metric numbers you did not produce. - Fabricating a transcript — a pasted output that your code could not have produced. - Presenting a demo of a platform you cannot explain. The live-verification question exists to test this.

    Consequences. A confirmed integrity flag means no badge, irrespective of score (see EVALUATION_PLAN.md). A first, minor, self-declared issue — an uncited price, an unattributed helper function — is corrected and noted, not penalised. The line is between unattributed help and unearned evidence; only the second is an integrity matter.

    PDPL is also an integrity matter. Raw personal data served to BI, or personal data retained past your own stated policy, is a PDPL red flag and blocks badge issuance on its own. Check your serving boundary before you submit, not during the demo.

    10. Where this goes next¶

    Every mandatory requirement maps back to the module that taught it: medallion (M1), economics (M2), ELT (M3), Delta (M4), streaming (M5), quality and governance (M6), orchestration (M7), serving (M8). The platform you submit is the same shape of platform you will scale in SDA-DSC-215 — Big Data Analytics with Spark (the same Lakehouse at enterprise volume, with distributed execution, partitioning strategy and MLlib) and build on in SDA-DSC-313. Keep the repository.

    SDAIA Academy — internal use only. Do not distribute.
