    CS-11 — The Masar National Mobility Data Incident¶

    The signature case study of SDA-DSC-214¶

    Field Value
    Case ID CS-11
    Modules M5 (streaming ingest) · M6 (quality, observability, governance) · M8 (serving AI and BI) — the course's integrating case

    Facilitator note — why this case exists¶

    Every previous case in this course had a component that failed. Here nothing fails. The producer works. The stream is exactly-once. The schema validates. Great Expectations is green. The DAG is green. The dashboards are correct. Freshness, volume and row counts are all normal. Every engineer did their job correctly.

    And the AI system degrades by 18%.

    This is the gap between "everything is healthy" and "the system is working", and closing that gap is what separates a data engineer from a data engineer who can be trusted with an AI platform. Do not let teams find the answer quickly. The value of this case is in the forty minutes where they cannot see it.

    1. Scenario¶

    Masar (مسار) is the national smart-mobility platform of a Saudi ride-hailing and public-transit operator. It serves 14,000 vehicles across Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, Mecca and Medina, and its Lakehouse feeds two consumer families:

    • BI — gold.fact_trip and its conformed dimensions, driving the executive dashboard, city-level operations reporting and the monthly regulatory submission.
    • AI — gold.eta_features and gold.zone_hourly_demand, driving the rider-facing ETA quote and the demand-forecasting model that positions vehicles ahead of demand.

    It is 08:00 on Thursday 9 June 2026. The model-monitoring job has raised its first alert in four months:

    ALERT  demand_forecast_v4  |  severity: HIGH  |  fired 2026-06-09 08:00:04 +03
  7-day rolling MAPE:  11.4%  ->  13.5%   (target <= 12.0%)
  accuracy deterioration vs 30-day baseline:  -18.2%
  affected: all zones, RUH/JED/DMM;  MAK/MED within tolerance
  last model deploy: 2026-04-19 (51 days ago)
  last feature-pipeline change: none in 30 days

    The on-call data engineer opens the executive dashboard first, as everyone does.

    It looks completely normal. Trip counts are on trend. Revenue is on trend. Completion rate, cancellation rate, mean fare, driver utilisation, city mix — every tile is within its usual band. There is no red anywhere in the platform.

    The Chief Data Officer will ask, in ninety minutes, one question: "Is our data broken, or is our model broken?"

    Your teams must answer it, and prove it.

    2. Business context¶

    Item Figure
    Vehicles on the platform 14,000
    Vehicles affected by the firmware rollout 1,004 (34% of the fleet cohort; 7.2% of all vehicles)
    GPS events/day ~15,000,000
    Events in the incident batch 47,583
    Demand-forecast MAPE before 11.4%
    Demand-forecast MAPE after 13.5%

    ETA quotes affected per day ~412,000
    Mean ETA over-prediction on affected zones +3.8 minutes
    Rider cancellations attributable to ETA drift, per day ~5,900
    Revenue impact, 4 days undetected SAR 1,180,000
    Vehicle-positioning misallocation cost, 4 days SAR 740,000
    Model re-baselining and retraining SAR 190,000

    Total incident cost SAR 2,400,000

    The final two rows are the case. A control costing about two engineer-days would have prevented a SAR 2.4M incident, and it was not built because nothing in the platform was ever red.

    3. Technical challenge¶

    Teams must uncover, from evidence alone, a seven-link causal chain in which every link behaved correctly:

    1. GPS producer firmware rolled from producer_version 2.4.0 -> 2.5.0 on 34% of the fleet
                                  |
2. speed_kmh values silently became METRES PER SECOND -- same field name, same double type
                                  |
3. Schema validation ACCEPTED it, because the TYPE was unchanged and the values were plausible
                                  |
4. Silver transformations continued normally: dedup passed, ranges passed, joins passed
                                  |
5. gold.eta_features.rolling_avg_speed_10m was corrupted -- averaging km/h and m/s together
                                  |
6. The ETA feature distribution shifted; the model read "this zone is crawling" for a third
   of the fleet and began over-predicting journey times
                                  |
7. AI predictions degraded by 18%, while BI -- which reports counts, revenue and completion,
   not speed -- showed absolutely nothing

    The intellectual difficulty is link 3. Everyone accepts that a schema check catches a renamed or retyped field. Almost nobody has internalised that a schema is a statement about structure and says nothing whatsoever about meaning — and that a unit is meaning, not structure.

    4. Constraints¶

    • Bronze is immutable. Do not permit a team to "fix the data at source" — the raw record is what the device sent and it is the evidence.
    • The firmware cannot be rolled back within the incident window. 1,004 vehicles are in the field; an OTA rollback takes 6 days. The fix must be on the platform side.
    • The CDO's question must be answered in 90 minutes, with evidence.
    • Riders are being quoted wrong ETAs right now. There is a live-mitigation decision to make before the root cause is fully proven — teams must handle acting under uncertainty.
    • PDPL applies throughout: raw lat/lon is sensitive-personal; nothing in the response may re-materialise personal data into BI or the online store.

    5. The incident timeline¶

    Give teams the left two columns only at the start (as "what we know so far"). The right column is the facilitator's key and is revealed during the debrief. The asymmetry between what was happening and what was observed is the entire case.

    When What was observed at the time What was actually happening
    Day 1 — Sun 2026-06-05, 09:00 UTC (12:00 +03) Nothing. No alert, no ticket, no anomaly. Device firmware 2.5.0 begins rolling to 1,004 vehicles. It keeps the field name speed_kmh and its double type, and starts writing metres per second.
    Day 1, 09:00–23:59 Ingest normal: 15.02M events. Consumer lag flat. Zero parse errors. GX suite 7/7 PASSED. 47,583 events land in bronze.gps_events with speed_kmh values 3.6× too small. Bronze faithfully records exactly what the device sent — including producer_version = "2.5.0".
    Day 2 — Mon 2026-06-06 DAG green. dbt test green. Freshness green. Volume green. Dashboard normal. silver.vehicle_positions promotes every affected row. Dedup passes. Range check 0 <= speed_kmh <= 200 passes — 9.85 is deeper inside the range than 35.45 was. gold.eta_features.rolling_avg_speed_10m begins averaging km/h and m/s in the same window.
    Day 3 — Tue 2026-06-07 ETA quotes drift long on some routes. 3 rider complaints reach support; all closed as "traffic". The model, reading a collapsed speed feature, infers heavy congestion in affected zones and over-predicts durations by ~3.8 min. Demand forecasting begins under-serving those zones.
    Day 4 — Wed 2026-06-08 Cancellation rate up 0.3 pts — within its normal daily band. Nobody investigates. ~5,900 additional cancellations. Vehicles positioned away from zones the model believes are congested. Revenue impact accumulating at ~SAR 295k/day.
    Day 5 — Thu 2026-06-09, 08:00 +03 Model-monitoring alert: 7-day MAPE 11.4% → 13.5%, −18.2%. BI dashboard entirely normal. Every platform health signal green. Four days of corrupted features have moved the rolling metric past its threshold. The first and only signal the platform ever produced came from the model, not from the data platform.
    Day 5, 08:00–09:30 (this session) Teams investigate.

    Note for the debrief: the platform never detected this. The model did. And the model detected it on a 7-day rolling metric, which is why it took four days. Nothing in the data platform — not schema validation, not Great Expectations, not freshness, not volume, not lineage, not the DAG — produced a single signal at any point.

    6. Evidence pack — released in six stages¶

    How to run the staging. Six physical stacks. Release one at a time on the clock in §8. Never release early, even if a team asks. Never hint. A team that reaches the answer at P3 has been given the case by the facilitator, not by the evidence.

    After each release, require every team to write one sentence in the worksheet's "What this rules in / rules out" box before they discuss. This forces evidence to be interpreted rather than accumulated.

    P5 — Producer release notes (released at minute 46)¶

    Retrieved from the device vendor's firmware portal, on request, after the investigation reached the producer:

    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 Masar Telematics SDK — release 2.5.0                        2026-06-05
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 Rollout: staged, cohort A (1,004 vehicles), from 09:00 UTC
 Rollback: OTA, estimated 6 days for full fleet

 CHANGES
  * Reduced GPS fix interval jitter (median -180 ms)
  * Battery: sleep-state tuning, ~4% improvement on idle
  * Harmonised telemetry units to SI across the payload for consistency
    with the vehicle CAN-bus integration.
  * Fixed a rare crash in the offline buffer flush path
  * Dependency bump: openssl 3.0.13 -> 3.0.14

 BREAKING CHANGES
  none
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

    Bullet three. "Harmonised telemetry units to SI across the payload." Ten words. Filed under CHANGES, not under BREAKING CHANGES, because the field name did not change and the type did not change — and by the vendor's definition of "breaking", which is a structural definition, that is entirely defensible.

    SI unit for speed is metres per second. The field is still called speed_kmh.

    What this rules in / rules out. This is the root cause. But do not let the room stop here — the causal chain is not "a vendor made a mistake". It is: the vendor made a change that was breaking in every sense except the one anybody was checking.

    9. The five conclusions teams must reach¶

    These are the assessable outcomes. A team has not completed CS-11 until it can state all five in its own words. Take them in order in the debrief; each one sets up the next.

    Conclusion 1 — Why schema validation missed a semantic change¶

    A schema is a contract about structure: field names, types, nullability, nesting. It says nothing about meaning: units, scale, currency, timezone, encoding, coordinate reference system, or the definition of an enum value.

    speed_kmh was a non-null double before the change and a non-null double after it. Every structural validator — a Spark StructType check, an Avro or Protobuf compatibility check, Delta's mergeSchema, a JSON Schema validator, a dbt contract on types — reports FULL_COMPATIBLE, and each is correct in reporting so.

    The general form: semantic changes are always type-compatible, because the type is not what changed. This makes them structurally invisible to the entire class of validation most platforms rely on. The same defect class produced CS-06's currency change (halalas → riyals, double → double) and would produce identical results for a timezone change (timestamp → timestamp), a coordinate-system change (double → double) or a redefinition of status = 'completed' (string → string).

    The corollary teams must state: a green schema check is evidence of nothing except that the shape is unchanged. Validation that stops at structure is validating the envelope and not the letter.

    Conclusion 2 — Why BI did not expose the failure¶

    BI measures what the business transacts: trips completed, revenue earned, fares charged, cancellations, driver utilisation, city mix. Every one of those is derived from silver.trips and gold.fact_trip. speed_kmh appears in none of them. The dashboard was not fooled and did not fail — it was measuring a different part of the world, correctly, the entire time.

    The deeper structural point, and the one that transfers: BI aggregates are counts and sums of discrete business events. Corrupting a continuous physical measurement does not move a count. Meanwhile AI features are transformations of continuous measurements, and a scale error passes through them directly and proportionally.

    Say the consequence plainly: a healthy BI layer is not evidence of a healthy data platform. For four days, a BI-centric view of platform health was not merely unhelpful — it was actively reassuring, and that reassurance is what allowed a 0.3-point cancellation rise on Day 4 to be dismissed. Most organisations' entire data-observability practice is BI-shaped, which means it is blind to precisely the failures that damage AI systems.

    Conclusion 3 — Why AI was disproportionately affected¶

    Four mechanisms compound, and teams should name at least three:

    1. Direct functional dependence. rolling_avg_speed_10m is computed from payload.speed_kmh. A 3.6× scale error propagates into the feature at full strength. No aggregation dilutes it; averaging km/h and m/s together produces a value that is neither.
    2. Models weight features, they do not sanity-check them. A dashboard is read by a human with priors — an analyst seeing a mean speed of 9 km/h across Riyadh would question it. A gradient-boosted model has no priors and no concept of "km/h". It has a learned weight, and it applies it faithfully to a number from a distribution it has never seen.
    3. Feature semantics amplify the error into a decision. Low average speed means congestion to this model. The corrupted feature did not merely add noise; it delivered a confident, coherent, entirely false narrative — "a third of the fleet is crawling" — that the model acted on correctly given its inputs. Corrupted features do not make models uncertain; they make models confidently wrong.
    4. The feedback loop closes. The demand model, believing affected zones congested, positioned vehicles away from them. Fewer vehicles produced longer waits and more cancellations, which the model then observed as reduced demand — confirming its own error. The data defect became a self-reinforcing operational failure, which is why the cost grew each day rather than staying constant.

    The transferable statement: BI degrades gracefully and visibly; AI degrades silently and compounds. A wrong number on a dashboard is a wrong number. A wrong number in a feature is a wrong decision, repeated 412,000 times a day, that changes the world the next batch of data describes.

    Conclusion 4 — Why observability must include feature distributions¶

    Masar's observability, before the incident, covered: freshness, volume, row counts, schema conformance, null rates, referential integrity, range checks, pipeline success, and consumer lag. Every one of them was green throughout. They are the standard, respectable set, and they are all operational signals — they answer "did the pipeline run?" rather than "does the data mean what it meant yesterday?"

    What was missing is a distribution monitor on the features the model actually consumes, segmented by the dimension along which change is deployed:

    monitor: gold.eta_features.rolling_avg_speed_10m
  statistic: PSI (10 deciles) vs frozen baseline 2026-05-01..2026-05-31
  segment by: producer_version, city, hour_of_day
  threshold: PSI > 0.20 warn, > 0.30 page
  observed on 2026-06-06: PSI 1.87   -> would have paged on DAY 2

    Four properties make this the right control, and each is a design decision teams should articulate:

    • It monitors the feature, not the source. A source monitor on speed_kmh also fires here, but the feature is what the model consumes and is where every upstream defect eventually converges. Monitoring the last mile catches defects you did not anticipate.
    • It uses a frozen, versioned baseline with a named owner. A rolling baseline would have absorbed the corruption within a fortnight (CS-06's "baseline-the-bug" trap) and then flagged the correction as the anomaly.
    • It segments by producer_version. Aggregated over the whole fleet the mean moved 25.7% — large, but a 34%-of-a-cohort effect is exactly the kind that a global threshold can be tuned to ignore. Segmented, the v2.5.0 cohort's mean is 9.85 against 35.45, a 3.6× separation that no threshold survives. Always segment drift by the dimension along which change is deployed.
    • It watches for bimodality, not only for mean shift. Two humps in one column means two populations — which is the signature of a semantic split rather than a real-world change, and it is the fastest thing to eyeball once a monitor exists to look at.

    State the principle: "the pipeline ran" and "the data is still what it was" are different questions, and only the second one protects a model.

    Conclusion 5 — Why data contracts must specify semantics and units, not merely types¶

    The contract in force on 5 June:

    # contracts/gps_events.yml  (v1.2 — the contract that PASSED on the day of the incident)
fields:
  - name: payload.speed_kmh
    type: double
    nullable: false

    That contract was fully satisfied by the corrupted data. It is a type declaration wearing the word "contract", and it cannot express the thing that changed.

    The contract that makes this class impossible:

    # contracts/gps_events.yml  (v2.0 — unit-aware, semantic)
fields:
  - name: payload.speed_kmh
    type: double
    nullable: false
    unit: "km/h"                     # SEMANTIC. A change here is a BREAKING change.
    unit_field: "payload.speed_unit" # producer MUST declare its unit in the payload
    valid_range: [0, 200]
    expected_distribution:
      mean:   {min: 25.0, max: 45.0}   # a scale error breaks this; a traffic change does not
      p05:    {min:  8.0, max: 20.0}
      p95:    {min: 40.0, max: 60.0}
    reconciles_with:
      expression: "silver.trips.distance_km / (silver.trips.duration_min / 60)"
      tolerance_pct: 25                # independent measurement of the same physical fact
    segment_monitoring_by: ["producer_version"]

producer_contract:
  version_field: producer_version
  breaking_change_policy: |
    ANY change to a field's unit, scale, currency, timezone, coordinate reference
    system, or enum meaning is a BREAKING change, EVEN IF the name and the type
    are unchanged. A breaking change REQUIRES a new field name or a major
    contract version bump, and dual emission for one overlap period.
  allowed_producer_versions: ["2.4.0"]   # new versions are GATED, not auto-accepted

    Four clauses do the work: unit makes the meaning explicit and therefore checkable; expected_distribution makes a scale error a contract violation on the first batch; reconciles_with anchors the field to an independent measurement (distance_km / duration_min from silver.trips) so that no baseline poisoning can hide it; and allowed_producer_versions turns an unannounced producer change into a hard failure at the boundary rather than a silent acceptance.

    And the organisational half, which is the harder half: the vendor's release note said "BREAKING CHANGES: none", and by a structural definition of "breaking" that was true. The contract must therefore define what breaking means, in writing, jointly, and that definition must include semantics. Otherwise two parties will each act correctly under two different definitions, which is exactly what happened.

    The sentence to write down: a type is a promise about shape; a unit is a promise about meaning; and models consume meaning.

    Q3 — Drift statistics: PSI and KS on the feature¶

    # quality/drift/psi.py — the check that would have paged on Day 2
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import ks_2samp

def psi(expected, actual, buckets=10):
    """Population Stability Index. <0.10 stable, 0.10-0.20 moderate, >0.20 breach."""
    edges = np.percentile(expected, np.linspace(0, 100, buckets + 1))
    edges[0], edges[-1] = -np.inf, np.inf
    e = np.histogram(expected, bins=edges)[0] / len(expected)
    a = np.histogram(actual,   bins=edges)[0] / len(actual)
    e, a = np.clip(e, 1e-6, None), np.clip(a, 1e-6, None)
    return float(np.sum((a - e) * np.log(a / e)))

baseline = spark.sql("""
    SELECT rolling_avg_speed_10m FROM gold.eta_features
    WHERE feature_ts BETWEEN '2026-05-01' AND '2026-05-31'
""").toPandas()["rolling_avg_speed_10m"].values          # FROZEN, versioned, owned

current = spark.sql("""
    SELECT rolling_avg_speed_10m FROM gold.eta_features
    WHERE DATE(feature_ts) = '2026-06-06'
""").toPandas()["rolling_avg_speed_10m"].values

print("PSI :", round(psi(baseline, current), 3))        # 1.870   (threshold 0.20)
ks = ks_2samp(baseline, current)
print("KS  :", round(ks.statistic, 3), "p =", ks.pvalue) # 0.310   p < 1e-300

# AND — the segmentation that makes it unmissable:
for pv in ("2.4.0", "2.5.0"):
    cur = spark.sql(f"""
        SELECT f.rolling_avg_speed_10m
        FROM   gold.eta_features f
        JOIN   (SELECT DISTINCT trip_id, producer_version FROM bronze.gps_events
                WHERE DATE(ts)='2026-06-06') g USING (trip_id)
        WHERE  g.producer_version = '{pv}'
    """).toPandas()["rolling_avg_speed_10m"].values
    print(pv, "PSI =", round(psi(baseline, cur), 3))
# 2.4.0 PSI = 0.021    <- stable, as expected
# 2.5.0 PSI = 6.940    <- catastrophic, unmissable at ANY threshold

    Point out the contrast for the class: aggregated PSI 1.87 (a breach that a lenient threshold might survive); segmented PSI 6.94 (a breach nothing survives). Segmentation is not a refinement — it is the difference between a signal that can be argued with and one that cannot.

    11. Remediation exercise¶

    Four controls. Each needs an owner, an implementation, and a done-test — something you can run that proves the control works.

    R1 — Immediate: roll back the corrupted feature via Delta time travel¶

DESCRIBE HISTORY gold.eta_features;

-- 4. Restore
RESTORE TABLE gold.eta_features TO VERSION AS OF 808;

-- 5. Rebuild forward with the unit normalisation applied (R2 below), then re-score.

    Done-test: SELECT AVG(rolling_avg_speed_10m) FROM gold.eta_features WHERE DATE(feature_ts) = '2026-06-08' returns a value in [25, 45], and the model's MAPE on a re-scored backtest returns to ≤ 12.0%.

    The live-mitigation decision (worksheet Q6), made at 08:40 and not at 09:30: exclude v2.5.0 vehicles from feature computation immediately — a one-line filter — accepting a 7.2% reduction in feature coverage rather than a 3.6× error on 34% of a cohort. Cost: slightly noisier features for four days. Benefit: correct ETAs restored within the hour. Acting on a well-founded hypothesis before proof is complete is correct incident response, provided the action is reversible and its cost is bounded. Teams that wait for certainty lose another SAR 295k.

    R2 — Contract: a unit-aware data contract¶

    Implement contracts/gps_events.yml v2.0 as specified in Conclusion 5. Plus the normalisation that should have existed in Silver from day one:

    # src/masar/transform/normalise.py
UNIT_FACTOR = {"2.4.0": 1.0, "2.5.0": 3.6}   # v2.5.0 emits m/s; multiply to reach km/h

def normalise_speed_by_producer_version(df):
    """Make speed_kmh actually mean km/h for every row, whatever the producer sent.
    An UNKNOWN producer_version FAILS. It is never silently passed through."""
    known = F.create_map([F.lit(x) for kv in UNIT_FACTOR.items() for x in kv])
    return (df
        .withColumn("_speed_factor", known[F.col("producer_version")])
        .withColumn("_unit_known",   F.col("_speed_factor").isNotNull())
        .withColumn("speed_kmh_raw", F.col("payload.speed_kmh"))        # keep the original
        .withColumn("speed_kmh",     F.col("payload.speed_kmh") * F.col("_speed_factor")))
    # rows where _unit_known is false are routed to quarantine by the caller, never promoted

    Done-test: inject a synthetic batch with producer_version = "2.6.0". The pipeline must quarantine it and fail the gate, not promote it. A contract that passes unknown versions has learned nothing from the incident.

    Done-test: replay data/fixtures/incident_speed_unit/gps_2026-06-05_v250.ndjson.gz through the pipeline. The monitor must page within one build cycle with segmented PSI ≈ 6.9 and must block serving. This is the single most important regression test in the platform, and it belongs in CI.

    R4 — Gate: a producer-version gate¶

    # quality/gates/producer_version_gate.py — runs at the Bronze -> Silver boundary
ALLOWED = {"2.4.0"}    # explicit allow-list; new versions are ADMITTED, never assumed

def gate(batch_df, batch_id):
    seen = {r.producer_version for r in
            batch_df.select("producer_version").distinct().collect()}
    unknown = seen - ALLOWED
    if unknown:
        quarantine(batch_df.filter(F.col("producer_version").isin(list(unknown))), batch_id)
        alert(severity="HIGH",
              title=f"Unknown producer_version in {batch_id}: {sorted(unknown)}",
              body=("Rows quarantined, NOT promoted to silver. A new producer version "
                    "requires: (1) vendor release notes reviewed for semantic changes, "
                    "(2) contract v-bump if any unit/scale/timezone/enum meaning changed, "
                    "(3) UNIT_FACTOR entry added, (4) 24h shadow comparison of the new "
                    "cohort's distributions against the incumbent, (5) explicit "
                    "allow-list addition by a named approver."))
        return "QUARANTINE"
    return "PROMOTE"

    Done-test: the same fixture replay must quarantine all 47,583 v2.5.0 events at the Bronze→Silver boundary on Day 1, and must page. Nothing reaches silver.vehicle_positions until a human adds "2.5.0" to the allow-list with a UNIT_FACTOR entry.

    Make the room notice what R4 costs: it converts an unannounced producer change from a silent semantic corruption into a loud, bounded, visible operational gap — 34% of a cohort's positions missing until someone acts. That is exactly the conversion CS-06 argued for, and it is the trade the platform should always make. Missing data asks a question; wrong data makes a decision.

    14. Debrief (4 minutes — deliver exactly these six beats)¶

    1. Nothing failed. Reveal the timeline's right-hand column. The producer worked. The stream was exactly-once. The schema validated. GX was 12/12 and 8/8. The DAG was 14/14. Freshness, volume, lag, nulls, referential integrity — all green, every day, throughout. Every component did precisely what it was told, and the AI system degraded by 18%. Type-compatibility is not semantic compatibility, and no amount of operational health tells you the data still means what it meant.

    2. The platform never detected it. The model did. For four days the only signal Masar produced came from a 7-day rolling model metric — the slowest, furthest-downstream, most lagging indicator in the entire stack. Everything designed to detect data problems was watching the wrong kind of thing. Observability that is BI-shaped is blind to the failures that damage AI, and worse than blind: it is reassuring, which is what allowed a real Day-4 signal (cancellations +0.3 pts) to be dismissed as normal.

    3. One GROUP BY cracked it — and a Day-1 design decision decided whether that was possible. Show Q2 again. GROUP BY producer_version, two rows, ratio 3.60. On Day 1 of this course, in CS-09, a perfectly sensible "efficient" ingestion design proposed dropping producer_version as "not needed downstream" and converting units at ingest. That design makes this incident permanently unsolvable. Whether you could solve today's problem was decided on Monday morning, in a job nobody thought was interesting.

    4. BI and AI fail differently, and the difference is structural. BI aggregates counts and sums of discrete business events; a corrupted continuous measurement does not move them. AI transforms continuous measurements; a scale error passes straight through, and the model does not become uncertain — it becomes confidently wrong, and then acts, and its actions change the world that the next batch of data describes. BI degrades visibly and stops. AI degrades silently and compounds.

    5. Deliver the two findings from the blast radius last. driver_safety_score has been flattering 1,004 vehicles' drivers for four days — an HR and fairness problem. And ops.alerts_speeding has fired for none of them since Day 1, because every reported speed was 3.6× too low. A safety control was silently disabled for four days on a national mobility platform, and the executive dashboard was green the entire time. Let that sit before you move on.

    6. The ratio. SAR 2,400,000 for the incident. Approximately SAR 12,000 — six engineer-days — for the four controls that would have prevented and detected it. The controls were not built because nothing was ever red. The whole discipline of this course is the practice of building controls for failures that will never announce themselves, and the course question in its final form:

    "What happens to Masar's AI system if THIS part of the data platform fails?"

    Here, nothing failed. And that is the most dangerous case of all.

    15. The one sentence participants should leave with¶

    A data platform can be entirely healthy — exactly-once, schema-valid, tests green, dashboards correct — while silently feeding an AI system data that no longer means what it used to mean; so contracts must specify units and semantics rather than only types, observability must watch the distributions of the features models actually consume, segmented by the dimension along which change is deployed, and the raw layer must retain the producer version that makes the one decisive query possible.
