    CS-05 — Live Dispatch at "Sareea" Ride-Hailing¶

    Field Value
    Case ID CS-05
    Module M5 — Streaming Data and Event-Driven Architectures
    Day / Hour Day 4 · Hour 1 (before LAB-05, as the design brief that LAB-05 then implements)
    Duration 45 minutes
    Format Design exercise
    Group size 4 per team
    Deliverable A one-page streaming design: topic + partition key, delivery semantics, watermark with a defended number, what stays batch, the single metric that tells you the stream is healthy, and the failure mode you accept
    Materials Evidence pack §5 per team (5 pages); A3 blank sheets for the pipeline drawing; the LAB-05 ASCII architecture on the projector
    Facilitator prep 15 min. Have data/fixtures/late_pings/README.md open (520 rows: 284 within, 216 beyond, 20 duplicates) — the beyond/within split is the watermark argument in real numbers

    1. Scenario¶

    "Sareea" (سريع) operates ride-hailing in Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam. Dispatch decides, minute by minute, where to send idle drivers. Today that decision is made from a demand map built overnight: a batch job ingests the day's GPS and trip events at 01:00 and produces a zone-demand table used by dispatch for the following day.

    Dispatch is therefore always reacting to yesterday's demand. When a concert, a match at the stadium, a flight bank at the airport, or a sandstorm shifts demand within the hour, drivers arrive where demand was.

    The COO wants zone demand within one minute of the events that create it. The head of engineering has one non-negotiable condition: "whatever you build must not double-count. If it double-counts, surge goes up, riders are charged more for demand that does not exist, and we are on the news."

    2. Business context¶

    Item Figure
    Vehicles emitting GPS 14,000
    GPS ping interval 5 seconds
    GPS events per day ~15,000,000
    Trips per day 48,200
    Current demand-map latency ~26 hours
    Target demand-map latency ≤ 60 seconds
    Mean rider wait, current 6.4 min
    Mean rider wait, modelled with 60-second rebalancing 4.9 min
    Modelled uplift in trip acceptance +3.1 pts
    Modelled annual revenue impact SAR 18,600,000
    Cost ceiling for the streaming platform SAR 65,000 / month
    Cost of a 10% spurious surge event (refunds + regulatory attention + churn) SAR 400,000+, plus unquantified reputational cost

    The asymmetry is the whole case: the upside of speed is large and modelled; the downside of double-counting is smaller in expectation but unbounded in consequence. A design that is fast and occasionally wrong is worse than a design that is slightly slower and always right.

    3. Technical challenge¶

    Move from a nightly batch demand map to a streaming pipeline computing zone demand within a minute, at 15M events/day, without losing or double-counting events, in a system that is restarted for deploys twice a week.

    The specific hard parts: - Duplicates inflate surge. At-least-once delivery is the default of every message broker. Left uncorrected, a redelivery after a restart counts the same vehicle twice in the same zone and the surge multiplier rises on phantom demand. - Late pings are structural, not exceptional. Riyadh has tunnels, multi-storey car parks and underground malls. A device buffers and flushes; pings arrive minutes after the moment they describe. - Restart safety. Deploys, autoscaling events and broker rebalances all restart consumers. Correctness must survive all three without human intervention. - Bounded cost. Unbounded state is the classic streaming cost failure: a window kept open too long grows state until the job dies at the worst moment.

    4. Constraints¶

    • Devices in tunnels/basements deliver pings minutes late (P3 quantifies it).
    • The platform is redeployed twice a week; it must be restart-safe with no manual reconciliation.
    • SAR 65,000/month ceiling.
    • Cannot change the device firmware or the 5-second emission interval this year.
    • The historical feature build for the ETA model stays as it is — do not propose rewriting everything as a stream.
    • Ops needs an alert path off the same data; it must not compete with the AI path for the same consumer offsets.

    5. Evidence pack¶

    P1 — The event, as it arrives (one line of gps_YYYY-MM-DD.ndjson)¶

    {"event_id":"ev_00009f025f480001","vehicle_id":"VEH-00884","driver_id":"DRV-02492",
 "trip_id":"TRP-20260601-0000000","ts":"2026-06-01T19:38:56Z",
 "payload":{"lat":26.41803,"lon":50.10770,"speed_kmh":15.3,"heading_deg":211,"accuracy_m":17.3},
 "producer_version":"2.4.0"}

    Five fields carry the entire design: event_id (the dedup key), vehicle_id and trip_id (candidate partition keys), ts (event time, not arrival time), and producer_version (which will matter more than anyone expects — see CS-11).

    P2 — Volume and shape¶

    Value
    Events/day 15,000,000
    Mean events/second 174
    Peak events/second (17:00–19:00 +03) 620
    Peak/mean ratio 3.6×
    Mean event size 268 bytes
    Peak ingress ~166 KB/s (~14 GB/day)
    Duplicate event_id rate at source 0.4% (~60,000/day)
    Out-of-order ts rate ~1%
    Vehicles 14,000
    Zones 63

    Note 0.4% duplicates arrive from the producer itself, before any broker redelivery. Deduplication is required by the data, not only by the delivery semantics. Teams that plan to rely on exactly-once delivery to avoid a dedup step have already failed.

    P3 — Ping arrival-lag distribution (measured over 7 days)¶

    Lag (event time → arrival at the broker) Share Cumulative
    ≤ 5 s 96.20% 96.20%
    5–30 s 2.10% 98.30%
    30 s – 2 min 0.90% 99.20%
    2–5 min 0.55% 99.75%
    5–10 min 0.18% 99.93%
    10–30 min 0.06% 99.99%
    > 30 min (device offline for a shift) 0.01% 100.00%

    This table is the watermark argument. Teams must select a watermark from this distribution and state the residual they are accepting. Do not let anyone answer "10 minutes because that seems safe."

    The Masar fixture makes it concrete: data/fixtures/late_pings/gps_late_2026-06-04.ndjson holds 520 late pings — 284 arriving within a 5-minute watermark and 216 beyond it, plus 20 duplicates. That is a 45% drop rate for events that were late by more than five minutes; whether that is acceptable depends entirely on what the number is used for, which is §7 Q2.

    P4 — Two failure post-mortems from Sareea's first streaming attempt (2025, abandoned)¶

    INC-2025-0412  "Surge spike, Olaya, 18:40-19:05"
  Consumer restarted during a deploy. Offsets were committed BEFORE the sink write.
  7,300 pings reprocessed and re-counted. Zone Z-RUH-01 demand read 1.41x actual.
  Surge rose to 2.3x on demand that did not exist. 412 rider complaints, SAR 61,000
  in goodwill refunds. Root cause recorded as "restart timing".

INC-2025-0517  "Demand map frozen, all cities, 03:12-07:40"
  Windowed aggregation used a 60-minute watermark to 'be safe about tunnels'.
  State store grew to 41 GB, executor OOM, job died, no alert until the morning
  standup. Demand map served stale data for 4.5 hours during the morning peak.

    These two incidents are the two failure modes of the same decision, in opposite directions. Give them to the teams together; the design must survive both.

    6. Facilitation script (minute-by-minute)¶

    Min Activity Facilitator does Watch for
    0–4 Frame. Give the COO's requirement and the head of engineering's condition, verbatim. Put the two numbers on the board: SAR 18.6M upside, unbounded downside. Ask: "Which of those two numbers should drive your design?" Teams that optimise for the 60 seconds. The condition, not the target, is the binding constraint.
    4–6 Distribute P1–P4 (hold P5 back). State the one-page deliverable. — —
    6–18 Round 1 — Draw the pipeline. A3 sheet: sources → broker → processing → storage → consumers. Every arrow must be labelled with its delivery guarantee. Circulate. One question per table: "Draw me the arrow where a duplicate can be created." Most teams have not thought about where. Teams that draw one consumer serving both AI and Ops. Ask what happens when the alerting consumer falls behind.
    18–25 Round 2 — The five decisions. Partition key · delivery semantics · watermark (with a number from P3) · what stays batch · the health metric. Hand out P4's two post-mortems now, if you have not already, to any team whose watermark is either 30 seconds or an hour. "Exactly-once" asserted without a mechanism. Ask: "exactly-once between which two points?"
    25–28 Reveal P5. Teams compare their design against the reference and mark the differences. Be explicit: differences are for defending, not for correcting. —
    28–38 Design review. Two teams present in 4 minutes; the room asks one question each. You ask the killer question of §7 Q2 and Q4. Play the head of engineering: "Convince me this cannot double-count during Thursday's deploy." Teams whose answer to restart safety is "we'll be careful during deploys."
    38–45 Debrief. §10. Show the exactly-once equation. Close on §11 and hand straight into LAB-05. The bridge line: "You have one hour to build what you just designed." —

    7. Discussion questions with model answers¶

    Q1. Why would at-least-once (duplicates) directly harm the business here?

    Model answer. Because the downstream consumer of a ping count is a pricing decision. Zone demand is a count of distinct vehicles and requests in a window; the surge multiplier is a monotonic function of that count. A duplicate does not add noise to a report — it adds a rider that does not exist to a market that then prices itself accordingly.

    The harm chain, which teams should be able to recite: duplicate ping → inflated zone demand → surge multiplier rises → riders charged more for demand that is not there → complaints, refunds (SAR 61,000 in INC-2025-0412 alone), regulatory attention on pricing fairness, and a rebalancing decision that sends drivers to an empty zone, which reduces supply where demand actually is. The duplicate causes a real physical misallocation of 14,000 vehicles.

    Contrast with a domain where at-least-once is fine: a page-view counter, a log aggregation, an ML training set of 15M rows where 0.4% duplicates barely move a gradient. The lesson is not that duplicates are always fatal; it is that the acceptable delivery semantic is a property of the consumer, not of the pipeline, and here one consumer sets prices.

    Then close the loop on P2: even with perfect exactly-once delivery, the producer emits 0.4% duplicate event_ids. Exactly-once transport does not give you exactly-once data. You need a dedup on event_id regardless.

    Q2. How do you pick the watermark given tunnel-delayed pings vs the cost of state?

    Model answer. Derive it from P3, state the residual, and — crucially — recognise that different consumers need different watermarks off the same stream.

    From P3: a 2-minute watermark admits 99.20% of events; 5 minutes admits 99.75%; 10 minutes admits 99.93%. Each additional minute of watermark holds more window state open: state ≈ (events/second) × (watermark seconds) × (per-key state size), so at 620 events/s peak, going from 5 to 60 minutes is a 12× state increase — which is exactly how INC-2025-0517 reached 41 GB and died.

    The correct design is two watermarks, not one:

    Consumer Watermark Residual dropped Why
    Dispatch / surge (real-time) 2 minutes 0.80% A demand map is a decision aid with a 60-second SLA. An event 5 minutes late describes a world that no longer exists; admitting it late is worth less than emitting the window on time. Under-counting by 0.8% is safe (it under-surges, never over-surges).
    silver.vehicle_positions → gold.eta_features (AI) 10 minutes 0.07% Feature correctness matters more than latency; the batch feature build tolerates minutes.
    Historical / training none — batch reprocess from bronze.gps_events 0% Bronze is append-only and complete. Anything dropped by any watermark is still in bronze and can be recomputed.

    That last row is the key insight and most teams miss it: the watermark only decides what the streaming aggregate sees. It never loses data, because bronze is append-only and holds every event including the very late ones. A watermark is a latency/completeness trade on a derived view, not a retention policy. Once teams see that, the anxiety about dropping 0.8% evaporates and the decision becomes tractable.

    Defend the number, never the feeling: "2 minutes, admitting 99.2% of events, dropping ~120,000 of 15M per day from the real-time view only, all of which remain in bronze and are absorbed by the next batch rebuild."

    Q3. What single metric tells you the stream is keeping up?

    Model answer. Consumer lag — the difference between the latest offset produced to a partition and the latest offset committed by the consumer group, measured per partition.

    Why it beats the alternatives: - Throughput looks healthy right up until it does not; a consumer processing 174 events/s looks fine while 620/s arrive, and only lag reveals the deficit. - Latency of the last processed record is a lagging indicator; it tells you about the record you finished, not the queue you have not started. - CPU / memory tells you the machine is busy, not that the work is being completed.

    Lag is the integral of the deficit: it is zero when you are keeping up, and it grows monotonically the moment you are not, long before anything fails. The alert condition should be on the derivative as well as the level — "lag increasing for 5 consecutive minutes" fires earlier than any absolute threshold and is robust to a legitimate burst.

    Watch for the per-partition subtlety: total lag can look flat while one partition — the one holding the hot key — grows without bound. Sareea's vehicle_id partitioning avoids the hottest-key problem, but a design keyed by city would put a third of 15M events on one partition and total lag would hide it. Alert per partition.

    Second-place answers worth acknowledging: watermark progression (if the watermark stops advancing, a partition has gone silent and windows will never close — INC-2025-0517's silent precursor) and checkpoint commit interval. A mature design alerts on all three, but if you get one number on a wall, it is lag.

    Q4. What stays batch, and why not stream everything?

    Model answer. Stay batch for anything that is (a) not latency-sensitive, (b) needs a wide historical window, or (c) needs to be reprocessed when logic changes.

    Workload Stream or batch Reason
    Ping → bronze.gps_events Stream It is the ingestion path; it must be continuous and exactly-once
    Dedup + unit normalisation → silver.vehicle_positions Stream Cheap, stateless-ish, needed by both consumers
    1-minute zone demand for dispatch Stream This is the 60-second business requirement
    gold.eta_features historical build (rolling windows, joins to weather and zones) Batch Needs days of history, joins several dimensions, and is rebuilt whenever feature logic changes
    Model training sets Batch Point-in-time correctness over months
    Daily/monthly BI marts Batch Nobody dispatches from a monthly mart
    Backfills and corrections Batch By definition

    The architectural name for this is Kappa-lite: stream into the lakehouse once, exactly once, and refine downstream in whichever mode fits each consumer. It beats a full Lambda architecture (two parallel implementations of the same logic, batch and streaming, kept in sync by hope) because it has one ingestion path and one copy of the raw truth — which is CS-01's lesson arriving from a different direction.

    The cost argument matters too and lands well with a COO: streaming everything means every historical recomputation becomes a replay of 15M events/day across the retention window, at streaming infrastructure prices, inside the SAR 65,000/month ceiling. Batch reprocessing from bronze is dramatically cheaper for exactly the workloads that need reprocessing most often.

    Q5. Exactly-once is often called impossible. What exactly are you claiming when you claim it?

    Model answer. The claim is not that a message is delivered exactly once — over an unreliable network that is genuinely impossible. The claim is about the observable end state:

    exactly-once effect = at-least-once delivery + idempotent, transactional sink + checkpointed offsets committed atomically with the write

    Each term does specific work: - At-least-once delivery — Kafka retains events and replays from an offset; nothing is lost. - Checkpointed offsets — Structured Streaming records which offsets a micro-batch covers, in the checkpoint, as part of committing the batch. - Atomic sink — the Delta commit for that micro-batch either lands entirely or not at all. - Committed together — the offset advance and the data commit are one atomic fact. This is precisely what INC-2025-0412 got wrong: offsets were committed before the sink write, so a restart in between skipped nothing but replayed a batch that had already partly landed.

    The order is the whole thing. Write, then commit offsets, atomically. And place the checkpoint next to its sink — one checkpoint per streaming query, never shared, never inside the table directory.

    8. Solution approach¶

    8.1 The five decisions, with justification¶

    Decision Choice Justification
    Topic + partitioning masar.gps.pings, keyed by vehicle_id, 24 partitions Ordering is only required per vehicle (a vehicle's position sequence must not reorder). 14,000 vehicles across 24 partitions gives even distribution and no hot key. Keying by city would create a 3-way skew; keying by trip_id orders per-trip but fragments a vehicle's idle-time pings across partitions. Choose the key by asking: what must not be reordered?
    Retention 7 days on the topic Long enough to replay a full week after a bad deploy or a logic bug; bronze is the permanent record so the topic does not need to be one.
    Delivery semantics Exactly-once effect, per §7 Q5 The business condition. Achieved by construction, not by care.
    Consumer groups Separate groups for the AI path and the Ops alert path Independent offsets mean a slow or crashed alerting consumer cannot delay ingestion, and a replay of one does not replay the other. This is the fan-out in P5 and the reason it is drawn as a fan-out.
    Watermark 2 min for the dispatch aggregate; 10 min for the silver→gold feature path; batch rebuild from bronze for training Derived from P3, with stated residuals; bronze retains everything regardless.
    Dedup dropDuplicates(["event_id"]) within the watermark, in silver — not in bronze Bronze is the immutable record and must keep the duplicates (they are evidence). Silver is where the data becomes trustworthy.
    Checkpoint One per query, on durable object storage, outside the table path Shared checkpoints are the second-most-common streaming outage after unbounded state.
    Trigger 30 seconds Meets the 60-second end-to-end SLA with margin; larger micro-batches are far more file-efficient than a continuous trigger.
    Health metric Consumer lag per partition; alert on 5 minutes of monotonic increase §7 Q3
    Maintenance Scheduled OPTIMIZE on bronze.gps_events and silver.vehicle_positions A 30-second trigger writes 2,880 commits/day; without compaction, read latency degrades within weeks (this is CS-02's small-file tax arriving in a streaming sink)

    8.2 The accepted failure mode (make every team write this line)¶

    "Under a 2-minute watermark, approximately 0.8% of pings (~120,000/day) arrive too late to be counted in the real-time dispatch view. They are retained in full in bronze.gps_events and are absorbed by the next batch rebuild of gold.eta_features. The real-time view therefore under-counts demand slightly and never over-counts it, which is the safe direction for surge."

    A design that does not name its accepted failure mode has not been designed; it has been hoped for. The direction of the error — under, never over — is the part that makes it acceptable to the head of engineering.

    8.3 Cost sanity check against the SAR 65,000 ceiling¶

    Component Sizing SAR/month
    Kafka (3 brokers, 14 GB/day ingress, 7-day retention ≈ 100 GB + replication) modest 14,000
    Structured Streaming cluster (2–6 workers, 30 s trigger, 620 ev/s peak) autoscaled 31,000
    Object storage (bronze GPS, ~14 GB/day compressed) 5 TB/year 4,000
    OPTIMIZE/VACUUM maintenance job (nightly, spot) 20 min/night 3,000
    Monitoring, lag metrics, alerting — 4,000
    Total 56,000

    Within ceiling with ~14% headroom. Note for the class: the 60-minute-watermark design from INC-2025-0517 does not fit — its state store alone forces a larger cluster class. The watermark decision is a cost decision as well as a correctness one.

    9. What good looks like vs common wrong turns¶

    What good looks like Common wrong turn
    Picks the partition key by asking "what must not be reordered?" and answers vehicle_id Partitions by city, creating a 3-way hot-key skew that per-partition lag will expose in week one
    Derives the watermark from P3 and states the residual and its direction "10 minutes, to be safe" — the exact reasoning that produced INC-2025-0517
    Uses different watermarks for different consumers off one stream One watermark for the whole platform, forcing dispatch latency and AI completeness to trade against each other
    Understands the watermark never loses data because bronze is append-only Treats watermark drop as permanent data loss and inflates it to compensate
    Separate consumer groups for AI and Ops One consumer serving both, so a stuck alerting sink stalls feature ingestion
    States exactly-once as at-least-once + idempotent sink + atomically committed offsets Asserts "we'll use exactly-once" as a configuration flag
    Dedups on event_id in silver, keeping duplicates in bronze as evidence Dedups at ingest, destroying the ability to measure the 0.4% producer duplicate rate
    Keeps the historical feature build in batch (Kappa-lite) Proposes streaming everything, then cannot reprocess when feature logic changes
    Names consumer lag per partition as the health metric, alerting on the derivative Monitors CPU and throughput, both of which look fine while falling behind
    Schedules OPTIMIZE on the streaming sinks from day one Ignores small files; read latency degrades over weeks and is diagnosed as "Delta is slow"
    Writes the accepted failure mode as a sentence Claims no data is ever lost, which is either false or expensive

    10. Debrief (5 minutes)¶

    1. The condition beat the target. The COO asked for 60 seconds; the head of engineering said "must not double-count." Every good design in the room was shaped by the second sentence. Latency targets are negotiable; correctness conditions are the architecture.
    2. Put the equation on the board and leave it there for the rest of the day: exactly-once effect = at-least-once delivery + idempotent transactional sink + offsets committed atomically with the write INC-2025-0412 had all three components and got the ordering wrong. Streaming correctness is mostly about what is committed together with what.
    3. The two post-mortems are the same decision in opposite directions. Watermark too short: lose late data. Watermark too long: unbounded state, OOM, and a frozen demand map during the morning peak — a worse outcome than losing 0.8% of pings. The watermark is not a safety setting where more is safer; it is a trade with a cost on both sides.
    4. The insight most teams miss: a watermark decides what the stream sees, not what you keep. Bronze is append-only and holds everything. This is why the medallion architecture and streaming fit together so well — you can be aggressive about latency in the derived view precisely because the raw layer forgives you.
    5. Hand into LAB-05. "You have one hour to build this: Kafka → Structured Streaming → bronze.gps_events → silver.vehicle_positions → two sinks. You will kill the job mid-batch and restart it, and you will prove zero duplicates."
    6. The course question. "What happens to Masar's AI system if the GPS stream duplicates or drops events?" Duplicates inflate rolling_zone_demand_15m and the model learns phantom peaks; drops deflate it and the model learns a flattened curve. And — foreshadow CS-11 deliberately — "a stream that is perfectly exactly-once can still deliver perfectly corrupted data; on Day 5 you will meet the incident where every event arrived exactly once and the model still broke."

    11. The one sentence participants should leave with¶

    Exactly-once is not a delivery guarantee you switch on, it is an end state you construct — at-least-once delivery plus an idempotent sink plus offsets committed atomically with the write — and every remaining design choice, above all the watermark, is a trade between latency, completeness and state that you must make with a number and name as an accepted failure mode.
