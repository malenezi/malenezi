    CS-02 — The Surprise Cloud Bill at "Tabadul" Analytics¶

    Field Value
    Case ID CS-02
    Module M2 — Compute–Storage Separation and Cloud Economics
    Day / Hour Day 1 · Hour 6 (after the cost-model walkthrough, before or immediately after LAB-02)
    Duration 50 minutes
    Format Investigation (cost autopsy) with a written recommendation
    Group size 3–4 per team; each team is "the FinOps tiger team"
    Deliverable A one-page memo to the CFO: the four largest cost drivers ranked by SAR, the fix for each, the SAR/month saved, the engineering days required, and the guardrail that stops recurrence
    Materials Evidence pack §5 printed per team (6 pages); calculators or laptops; blank memo template (§8.4)
    Facilitator prep 15 min. Work the arithmetic in §7 yourself once — participants will challenge your numbers and you must be able to derive them live

    1. Scenario¶

    "Tabadul" (تبادل) is the analytics unit of a Saudi holding company. In April 2026 it completed a migration from an on-premises appliance warehouse to a cloud Lakehouse. The business case, approved by the board, promised 40% cost savings within two quarters.

    The first full month on the new platform is May 2026. The bill arrived on 3 June. It is 20% higher than the on-premises run-rate it replaced.

    The CFO has scheduled a review for Thursday. The platform lead has asked your team to find out what happened and what to do about it, in that order.

    2. Business context¶

    Item Figure
    On-premises run-rate being replaced (amortised appliance + support + DC) SAR 385,000 / month
    Business case target after migration SAR 231,000 / month (−40%)
    Actual May 2026 cloud bill SAR 462,000 / month (+20% vs on-prem, +100% vs target)
    Gap to business case SAR 231,000 / month
    BI dashboards in production 40, refreshing every 15 minutes, 06:00–22:00 +03
    Nightly ML feature build 1 job, ~90 minutes
    Data growth 8% / month
    Analyst headcount served 55
    Deadline to show savings 60 days

    Two things are true simultaneously and teams must hold both: the migration was not a mistake, and the current bill is not defensible. Decoupling compute from storage bought elasticity — the option to pay only for what you use. Tabadul exercised none of it.

    3. Technical challenge¶

    The platform is architecturally correct and economically catastrophic. Specifically:

    • The gold marts are one unpartitioned Delta table per subject area, written by an append job that produces thousands of small files and has never been compacted. Every dashboard query full-scans.
    • Autoscaling is enabled, minimum worker count is set to the peak requirement, and idle termination is disabled because an engineer disabled it in March to stop a demo cluster dying mid-presentation.
    • All jobs run on on-demand instances. The nightly feature build is fully restartable and idempotent — a textbook spot workload — and pays on-demand rates.
    • There is no cost attribution: no tags, no per-dashboard or per-team chargeback. Nobody can name the most expensive dashboard.

    4. Constraints¶

    • Dashboard refresh cadence cannot change. The business has refused; treat it as fixed. (This is deliberate: it removes the easy answer and forces teams to attack cost-per-query instead of query count.)
    • Savings must be demonstrable within 60 days — so fixes with a long lead time score poorly.
    • Data volume grows 8% / month; any fix that does not scale is a deferral, not a solution.
    • No reduction in analyst headcount or dashboard count.
    • The team has two engineers, part-time: budget roughly 12 engineering days for the whole programme.

    5. Evidence pack¶

    P1 — May 2026 bill, decomposed¶

    Line item SAR / month Share
    Interactive/BI compute (the dashboard cluster) 268,000 58.0%
    Job compute (nightly feature build + 6 small ETL jobs) 121,000 26.2%
    Object storage (data at rest, 340 TB) 27,000 5.8%
    Storage API requests (GET/LIST) 31,000 6.7%
    Egress + networking 9,000 1.9%
    Other (logging, metastore, monitoring) 6,000 1.3%
    Total 462,000 100%

    The headline for the debrief: storage is 5.8% of the bill. Every instinct from the on-premises era says "data is expensive." In a decoupled architecture, data at rest is nearly free and reading it badly is what costs money.

    P2 — Dashboard cluster utilisation, one representative week (hours)¶

    Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat Sun
    Cluster running (h) 24 24 24 24 24 24 24
    Hours with ≥1 query 16 16 16 15 7 6 16
    Hours fully idle 8 8 8 9 17 18 8

    Idle hours per week: 76 of 168 = 45.2%. Applied to the interactive line: 268,000 × 0.452 ≈ SAR 121,000 / month spent on a cluster with zero queries running.

    Note the Friday/Saturday shape — the Saudi weekend. A naive "9-to-5 weekday" schedule imported from a foreign template would be wrong here; the Sunday column is a full working day.

    6. Facilitation script (minute-by-minute)¶

    Min Activity Facilitator does Watch for
    0–3 Frame. "The migration promised 40% savings. Month one is 20% over the old bill. The CFO meets you Thursday. What do you tell her?" Deliberately do not allow an answer yet. Write SAR 385k → 462k (target 231k) on the board and leave it there all session. Teams that immediately say "the migration failed." Note who; you will use them in the debrief.
    3–6 Distribute the pack. State the memo deliverable and the 12-engineering-day budget. Emphasise the budget aloud — it is what turns this from a list of ideas into a ranked plan. —
    6–14 Round 1 — Where does the money go? Teams decompose P1 and must produce a SAR figure for each of: idle compute, over-scanning, on-demand premium, small-file overhead. Circulate. To any team without a number: "Give me a number. Wrong is fine; absent is not." Teams that discuss without arithmetic. FinOps is arithmetic.
    14–17 Anchor round. Each team calls out its estimate for idle compute only. Write all estimates on the board. Do not correct yet. Spread of estimates is itself the lesson: nobody at Tabadul had a number either. Estimates should cluster near SAR 120k.
    17–33 Round 2 — Build the memo. Rank the four drivers, name a fix each, estimate SAR saved and days spent, and specify one guardrail. Push on savings per engineering day — that is the ranking criterion, not absolute savings. Ask each team: "If you only get 3 of your 12 days, which fix do you do?" Teams that put "migrate back on-prem" in the memo. Take it seriously for 30 seconds, then price it.
    33–43 CFO panel. You play the CFO. Two teams present their memo in 3 minutes. Interrupt with §7 Q3-style challenges. Be a sceptical but fair CFO: "You're telling me the same team that promised 40% now promises 55%. Why should I believe the second number?" Teams that promise savings without a measurement plan. That is the exact failure that got Tabadul here.
    43–50 Debrief. §10. Show the reference answer table in §8.2. Close on §11. Reveal that the four fixes total ~SAR 267k/month for ~9 engineering days, i.e. the business case was achievable all along. —

    7. Discussion questions with model answers¶

    Q1. Why did decoupling alone not deliver savings?

    Model answer. Because decoupling does not reduce cost — it converts cost from fixed to variable and hands you the controls. On the appliance, storage and compute were fused and pre-paid: reading a table badly was already paid for, so nobody optimised it. In the cloud, every bad read is a fresh invoice line. Tabadul migrated the architecture and kept the appliance-era operating habits: a permanently-on cluster sized for peak (the appliance was always on and sized for peak, and that was correct there), unpartitioned tables (the appliance's own indexes hid this), and no attribution (there was nothing to attribute — the appliance was one number).

    The precise formulation to write on the board: elasticity is an option, and an unexercised option has a cost but no benefit. Tabadul is paying the premium for elasticity (cloud unit prices exceed amortised appliance unit prices) while consuming a fixed-capacity workload profile. That is the worst square of the matrix, and it is exactly where naive migrations land.

    Q2. Which fix yields the most savings per engineering hour — compaction, caching, or idle-termination?

    Model answer. Rank by SAR saved ÷ engineering days:

    Fix SAR/month saved Eng. days SAR per eng. day
    Idle termination + right-sized min_workers + weekend schedule ~121,000 0.5 242,000
    Spot for the nightly feature build ~42,000 0.5 84,000
    OPTIMIZE + ZORDER + partitioning of the four gold marts ~76,000 3 25,300
    Result caching / hot-mart materialisation for BI ~28,000 5 5,600

    Idle termination wins by two orders of magnitude and it is a configuration change: autotermination_minutes: 20, min_workers: 3, plus a schedule that respects the Saudi weekend. It is also the least intellectually interesting fix, which is precisely why engineers under-propose it and why you must make the class rank by ratio rather than by absolute saving.

    Compaction is second on absolute savings and third on ratio, but it is the only fix that scales with the 8%/month growth, so it must not be dropped — frame it as "do the config change this week, the layout change this month."

    Caching is last: highest effort, most new moving parts, and it optimises a cost that partitioning has already largely removed. A team that proposes caching first has optimised the interesting problem rather than the expensive one.

    Q3. How would you present month-two numbers to rebuild leadership trust?

    Model answer. Trust was destroyed by a single unfalsifiable promise ("40% savings"). It is rebuilt by many small falsifiable ones. The memo must contain:

    • A baseline stated before the fix, in the same units as the bill (SAR/month by line item), so the claim can be checked against the invoice rather than against a dashboard the team controls.
    • Per-fix attribution: after tagging, the CFO sees bi-interactive fall from 268k to 147k as a line item, not a slide. Attribution is the deliverable; savings is the consequence.
    • A committed cadence: a weekly one-page cost report with forecast vs actual, published whether the news is good or bad. Publishing a bad week on time buys more credibility than a good week published on request.
    • Unit economics, not totals: SAR per dashboard-refresh and SAR per TB scanned. Totals will rise again with 8%/month growth and a growing business should cost more; unit cost falling while totals rise is the honest story, and it survives growth.
    • An explicit statement of what would falsify the plan and what happens then.

    Q4. What guardrail prevents this recurring as data grows 8%/month?

    Model answer. Guardrails must be automatic and preventive, not a policy document. Four, in order of value:

    1. Mandatory cost tags enforced at cluster/job creation — an untagged resource cannot be created. Without attribution, no other guardrail can be targeted.
    2. A budget alert with a unit-cost trigger, not only a total — page when SAR per TB scanned rises, because the total is supposed to rise at 8%/month and a total-only alert will be either permanently firing or permanently muted.
    3. OPTIMIZE/VACUUM as scheduled DAG tasks, not a runbook step (this is exactly what LAB-07 builds — say so). Small-file decay is not an event, it is a rate; the counter-measure must also be a rate.
    4. Idle termination and bounded autoscale as policy defaults in the cluster template, so that the only way to get an always-on cluster is an explicit, reviewed exception with a name attached to it.

    Note the 8% figure explicitly: at 8%/month, volume doubles in 9 months. A one-off cleanup delivers a saving that is fully consumed within a year. Only the scheduled, automatic fixes survive.

    Q5 (reserve). Should Tabadul migrate back to the appliance?

    Model answer. Price it, do not dismiss it. Migrating back costs the migration budget again (sunk cost already spent, plus new spend), reinstates a fixed peak-sized capital commitment against a workload growing 8%/month (the appliance must be sized for month 24, and paid for from month 1), and forfeits the elasticity option permanently. Meanwhile the four fixes above reach ~SAR 195,000/month for ~9 engineering days — below the business-case target of 231k. The correct answer is that the cloud architecture is fine and the operating model is not, and operating models are cheap to change.

    8. Solution approach¶

    8.1 The four fixes, in execution order¶

    Week 1 — configuration (0.5 days, ~SAR 121,000/month). 

    autotermination_minutes: 20     # was 0
autoscale: {min_workers: 3, max_workers: 12}   # was min=max=12
# plus: scheduled scale-to-zero outside 06:00-22:00 +03, with Friday/Saturday reduced
    Right-sizing the floor and terminating on idle recovers essentially all of the 45.2% idle. Keep max at 12 so peak behaviour is unchanged — the business constraint (refresh cadence, dashboard responsiveness) is respected.

    Week 1 — spot for the nightly build (0.5 days, ~SAR 42,000/month). The feature build is idempotent and restartable, which is the only precondition for spot. State this explicitly to the class: idempotency is a cost lever, not just a correctness property. It is the same design decision M3, M4 and M7 keep making, cashed out in SAR.

    Weeks 2–3 — layout (3 days, ~SAR 76,000/month, and it scales). 

    -- one-off remediation
OPTIMIZE gold.trip_revenue_hourly ZORDER BY (city, hour_ts);
OPTIMIZE gold.fact_trip           ZORDER BY (city, pickup_ts);
-- and, going forward, partition on the two universal dashboard filters
ALTER TABLE gold.fact_trip SET TBLPROPERTIES (
  delta.autoOptimize.optimizeWrite = true,
  delta.autoOptimize.autoCompact   = true
);
    Plus partitioning the marts by city and date, and rewriting the dashboard queries to select only the columns they use. Expected effect on P3's query: numFiles 41,882 → ~300; bytes scanned 74 GB → ~4 GB; PartitionFilters and PushedFilters populated.

    Week 4+ — attribution and guardrails (2 days, savings enabling). Tags on every cluster and job; a weekly unit-cost report; OPTIMIZE/VACUUM promoted to DAG tasks; cluster-policy defaults.

    8.2 Reference answer — the memo's numbers¶

    Rank Driver SAR/month Fix Saved Days SAR/day
    1 Idle interactive compute (45.2% of 268k) 121,000 Idle termination + min_workers=3 + weekend schedule 121,000 0.5 242,000
    2 Full-scan tax (unpartitioned, uncompacted, 14 cols read for 3) ~104,000 Partition + ZORDER + OPTIMIZE + column pruning 76,000 3 25,300
    3 On-demand premium on a restartable job 121,000 job line Spot with on-demand fallback 42,000 0.5 84,000
    4 Storage API request volume (41,882 files/scan × 460,800 queries) 31,000 Falls out of fix 2 28,000 0 ∞
    Total ~267,000 ~4 (+2 guardrails)

    Resulting run-rate: 462,000 − 267,000 ≈ SAR 195,000 / month, against a business-case target of 231,000 — the case was beatable, and it was lost to configuration, not to architecture. Deliver this line slowly.

    8.3 What must NOT be in the memo¶

    • Changing the dashboard refresh cadence (constraint).
    • Reducing dashboard count or headcount (constraint).
    • Deleting history to shrink storage — storage is 5.8% of the bill; deleting it destroys audit and PDPL evidence to save ~2%.
    • Migrating back.

    8.4 Memo template (hand out blank)¶

    TO:      CFO
FROM:    Platform FinOps tiger team
DATE:    2026-06-__
RE:      May 2026 platform cost — diagnosis and 60-day corrective plan

1. WHAT HAPPENED  (3 sentences, no blame, no jargon)

2. WHERE THE MONEY WENT  (four ranked drivers, SAR/month each, summing to ≥ 90% of the gap)

3. WHAT WE WILL DO  (per driver: fix, SAR/month saved, engineering days, week delivered)

4. WHAT YOU WILL SEE AND WHEN  (baseline, measurement, cadence, the number that falsifies us)

5. WHY IT WILL NOT RECUR  (the guardrail, and why it is automatic rather than a policy)

    9. What good looks like vs common wrong turns¶

    What good looks like Common wrong turn
    Every claim carries a SAR figure derived from the pack Qualitative list of "best practices" with no arithmetic
    Ranks fixes by savings per engineering day Ranks by absolute savings, so compaction beats a 30-minute config change
    Notices storage is only 5.8% and stops trying to delete data Proposes an aggressive retention cut, saving ~SAR 8k and destroying the audit trail
    Ties spot eligibility to the job being idempotent Proposes spot for the interactive BI cluster, then discovers dashboards die mid-refresh
    Reads P2's Friday/Saturday columns and schedules to the Saudi week Imports a Mon–Fri schedule and turns off the cluster on Sunday, a full working day
    Proposes attribution first because nothing else can be targeted without it Leaves tagging as a "phase 3 nice-to-have"
    Distinguishes total cost (which must rise with growth) from unit cost (which must fall) Promises the total bill will fall forever at 8%/month growth
    Respects the fixed refresh cadence and attacks cost-per-query Recommends "refresh hourly instead of every 15 minutes" — the one thing the business excluded
    Says out loud that the architecture is fine and the operating model is not Concludes the migration was a mistake

    10. Debrief (5 minutes)¶

    1. Read P1 line 3 aloud: storage is 5.8%. In the appliance era, data volume was the cost. In a decoupled architecture, data at rest is nearly free and reading it badly is the entire bill. Every participant carries the old intuition; this line is where it dies.
    2. Read P3's four lines aloud. PartitionFilters: [], PushedFilters: [], numFiles: 41,882, 96.4 GB scanned to return 720 rows. Then say: nobody wrote a bad query. They wrote a reasonable query against a badly laid-out table. Physical layout is a cost control, and it is set by the writer, not the reader.
    3. The ratio table (§7 Q2). The most valuable fix in the case is a two-line YAML change worth SAR 242,000 per engineering day, and it is the one every engineer proposes last. Name the bias: we optimise the interesting problem, not the expensive one.
    4. Elasticity is an option. Tabadul paid the premium and never exercised it. Say the sentence: you do not save money by moving to the cloud; you gain the ability to stop paying for what you are not using, and you must then actually stop.
    5. Bridge to LAB-02 and to the course question. In the lab, participants will run the naive / pruned / partition-filtered query on Masar's own silver.trips and watch ReadSchema and bytes-scanned collapse. Then ask: "What happens to Masar's AI system if the feature build's cost is not controlled?" Answer: it gets run less often. Feature freshness is a budget decision before it is an engineering one, and a stale feature is a degraded model — which is exactly the failure CS-11 will show you on Day 5.

    11. The one sentence participants should leave with¶

    Separating compute from storage does not make anything cheaper; it converts a fixed bill into a variable one and hands you the controls — and until you set idle termination, lay the data out for the queries you actually run, and attribute every riyal to something a person owns, you are paying a premium for an elasticity you never exercise.
