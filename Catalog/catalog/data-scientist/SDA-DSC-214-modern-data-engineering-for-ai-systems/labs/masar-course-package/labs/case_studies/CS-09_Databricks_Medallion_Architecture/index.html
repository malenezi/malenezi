    CS-09 — The Medallion Architecture as an Industry Benchmark (Databricks)¶

    Field Value
    Case ID CS-09
    Module M1 — Evolution of Data Architecture
    Day / Hour Day 1 · Hour 4 (immediately after CS-01, before LAB-01)
    Duration 60 minutes
    Format Design exercise (classification + written architecture decision)
    Group size 3–4 per team
    Deliverable A one-page Architecture Decision Record (ADR) answering: which transformations belong in Bronze, Silver and Gold for Masar's 15M daily GPS events — and which must NEVER occur before Bronze persistence
    Materials Blank ADR template (§8.4) one per participant; the transformation deck (§5 P3) printed and cut, one set per team; the exemplar ADR (§8.3) held back until minute 45
    Facilitator prep 20 min. Read §8.2's classification table until you can defend any single row unprompted. Participants will challenge rows 3, 9, 13 and 18 — those four are where the learning is

    1. Scenario¶

    The medallion architecture — Bronze → Silver → Gold — is the reference pattern Databricks popularised for Lakehouse design, and it is now the de facto industry benchmark. Almost every team can recite the three names. Far fewer can answer the only question that matters in practice: for a specific transformation, which layer does it belong in, and why?

    Masar is about to land its first production feed: 15,000,000 GPS events per day from 14,000 vehicles, at one ping per vehicle every 5 seconds. The platform team has drafted a design in which the ingestion job performs deduplication, drops pings with poor GPS accuracy, converts speed to a canonical unit, hashes the driver identifier, and writes the result to bronze.gps_events.

    The design is efficient. It is also irreversible, and it will make the next twelve months of the platform's failures undiagnosable.

    Your teams must decide, transformation by transformation, what Bronze is for.

    2. Business context¶

    Item Figure
    GPS events per day 15,000,000
    Vehicles 14,000
    Emission interval 5 seconds
    Raw event size ~268 bytes
    Raw daily volume (uncompressed / compressed) ~4.0 GB / ~0.9 GB
    Annual bronze storage at full retention ~330 GB compressed
    Annual object-storage cost of retaining every raw event ≈ SAR 1,100
    Cost of the drafted "efficient" design's savings (dedup + accuracy filter at ingest) ~1.4% of events dropped → ≈ SAR 15/year saved
    Cost of one undiagnosable data incident (CS-11's actual cost) SAR 2,400,000

    Put those last two lines next to each other and the entire architectural argument is settled by arithmetic: the drafted design saves roughly fifteen riyals a year and forfeits the ability to investigate a multi-million-riyal incident. Bronze retention is not a cost decision. It is an insurance decision priced at approximately nothing.

    3. Technical challenge¶

    Classify every transformation in Masar's GPS pipeline into one of four buckets:

    Bucket Meaning
    Bronze Applied at landing. Must be additive and lossless: it may add metadata, never remove or reinterpret a source value
    Silver Applied when raw becomes trustworthy: validated, deduplicated, conformed, typed, unit-normalised, joined to reference data
    Gold Applied when trustworthy becomes useful: aggregated, windowed, modelled to a grain, shaped for one named consumer
    NEVER before Bronze Destroys information the raw record carried. Must not happen upstream of Bronze persistence under any circumstances

    The fourth bucket is the point of the case. Bronze/Silver/Gold is a progressive refinement; the boundary that actually protects the platform is the one before Bronze.

    4. Constraints¶

    • Bronze must satisfy CONVENTIONS.md: every bronze table carries _ingested_at, _source_file, _batch_id.
    • Bronze must be append-only. A re-run grows history; it never replaces it.
    • PDPL applies. Precise GPS (payload.lat, payload.lon) is sensitive-personal; driver_id is personal. Retention for raw GPS is 30 days (see data/DATA_DICTIONARY.md).
    • The AI consumer (gold.eta_features) and the BI consumer (gold.fact_trip) must both descend from the same Silver.
    • The design must survive a producer changing behaviour without telling anyone — this is not hypothetical; it is CS-11.

    5. Evidence pack¶

    P1 — The raw event, exactly as the producer emits it¶

    {"event_id":"ev_00009f025f480001","vehicle_id":"VEH-00884","driver_id":"DRV-02492",
 "trip_id":"TRP-20260601-0000000","ts":"2026-06-01T19:38:56Z",
 "payload":{"lat":26.41803,"lon":50.10770,"speed_kmh":15.3,"heading_deg":211,"accuracy_m":17.3},
 "producer_version":"2.4.0"}

    Every field on this line is a fact about the world as the device reported it. Bronze's job is to record that report faithfully, including the parts that are wrong.

    P2 — The drafted "efficient" ingestion design (the proposal under review)¶

    # ingest/land_gps.py  -- AS DRAFTED. Four of these five steps are architectural errors.
(spark.readStream.format("kafka").load()
   .select(from_json(col("value"), GPS_SCHEMA).alias("e")).select("e.*")
   .dropDuplicates(["event_id"])                                  # (1) dedup at ingest
   .filter(col("payload.accuracy_m") < 20)                        # (2) drop imprecise pings
   .withColumn("speed_mps", col("payload.speed_kmh") / 3.6)       # (3) unit conversion
   .drop("payload.speed_kmh")                                     #     ... and drop the original
   .withColumn("driver_id", sha2(col("driver_id"), 256))          # (4) hash the driver id
   .drop("producer_version")                                      # (5) "not needed downstream"
   .writeStream.format("delta").outputMode("append")
   .option("checkpointLocation", CKPT).table("bronze.gps_events"))

    Have teams mark each of the five steps before they see the classification table. Then, in the debrief, walk through what each one costs:

    Step What it destroys Which incident it makes undiagnosable
    (1) dedup at ingest The 0.4% duplicate rate — a producer health metric A firmware regression that triples duplicates becomes invisible
    (2) accuracy filter ~1% of pings and, with them, the accuracy distribution Tunnel/urban-canyon coverage analysis; a device fleet with degrading GPS
    (3) unit conversion + drop The value the producer actually sent CS-11 entirely. The 3.6× ratio is the evidence; converting by 3.6 at ingest silently corrects v2.5.0 and silently corrupts v2.4.0, and there is no way to tell them apart afterwards
    (4) hash driver_id Nothing, if done consistently — but at the wrong layer It couples ingestion to a key-management lifecycle; a rotated salt breaks bronze history irreversibly
    (5) drop producer_version The only column that correlates a data change with a deployment CS-11's root cause is found by GROUP BY producer_version. Without it, there is no investigation

    Step (3) plus step (5) together are precisely the configuration in which the course's signature incident is permanently unsolvable. That is the sentence to leave hanging in the room.

    6. Facilitation script (minute-by-minute)¶

    Min Activity Facilitator does Watch for

Ask the room to define them first, in one sentence each. Write what they say; correct it at minute 50. Definitions phrased by technology ("bronze is Parquet") rather than by question answered.
    5–9 P2 — mark the draft. Project the drafted ingestion job. "Five transformations. Mark each one keep or move." Individually, silently, 4 minutes. Silence. No discussion. Most people mark 1–2 of the five. The gap between that and the real answer is the case.

    7. Discussion questions with model answers¶

    Q1 — the centrepiece. Masar receives 15 million GPS events daily. Which transformations belong in Bronze, Silver and Gold, and which must NEVER occur before Bronze persistence?

    Model answer. The full classification is §8.2. The principle behind it, which is what participants must be able to apply to a card that was not in the deck:

    • Bronze answers "what did the source actually say?" Therefore the only admissible transformations at Bronze are additive: attaching lineage metadata (_ingested_at, _source_file, _batch_id), and parsing the wire format into typed columns without changing any value. Bronze may add columns. It may never remove a row, remove a column, or reinterpret a value. Bronze is an evidence store before it is a data store.
    • Silver answers "what is true about the world?" Therefore Silver is where judgement is applied: dedup, unit normalisation, timezone normalisation, validation, quarantine, conforming joins to reference data. Every Silver decision is reversible by re-running from Bronze, which is exactly what makes it safe to be opinionated there.
    • Gold answers "what does this specific consumer need?" Therefore Gold is aggregation, windowing, grain selection, feature engineering, star-schema modelling — and there are legitimately several Golds, one per consumer, because BI's needs and the ETA model's needs genuinely differ. gold.fact_trip and gold.eta_features are siblings, not layers.
    • NEVER-before-Bronze is any transformation that destroys information the source provided: dropping rows for any reason, dropping columns, converting units in place, repairing values, filtering outliers, or discarding provenance such as producer_version.

    The test to apply to any card, and the sentence worth memorising: "If this transformation is wrong, can I fix it by re-running?" If yes, it belongs in Silver or Gold. If no — if being wrong means the information is gone forever — it must not happen before Bronze.

    Then the economics, because the objection is always cost: at 15M events/day, retaining every raw event costs roughly SAR 1,100/year. The drafted "efficient" design saves about SAR 15/year and forfeits the investigation of an incident that cost SAR 2.4M. Bronze is the cheapest insurance in the entire platform.

    Q2. Card 3 — deduplication. Everyone agrees duplicates are bad. Why is dedup at Bronze wrong?

    Model answer. Because the duplicate rate is a signal about the producer, not noise in the data. Masar's GPS feed carries ~0.4% duplicate event_ids (~60,000/day). That number is a health metric: if it triples after a firmware rollout, something is wrong with the device fleet, and that is worth knowing.

    Dedup at Bronze destroys the metric permanently and silently. Nobody notices, because the deduplicated data looks fine — which is exactly why it is dangerous.

    The correct design: Bronze keeps duplicates; Silver removes them. silver.vehicle_positions deduplicates on event_id; bronze.gps_events retains everything and a monitor computes the duplicate rate per producer_version per day. If Silver's dedup logic is later found to be wrong (wrong key, wrong window), Silver is rebuilt from Bronze in an afternoon. If Bronze deduplicated, there is nothing to rebuild from.

    There is one narrow, legitimate exception worth naming so teams do not over-apply the rule: transport-level idempotency — an exactly-once sink that prevents the same micro-batch being written twice on restart — is not "dedup", it is correct write semantics, and it belongs at Bronze. The distinction: preventing your pipeline from writing a record twice is correctness; removing records the producer sent twice is interpretation.

    Q3. Card 9 — casting speed_kmh from string to double. Is that Bronze?

    Model answer. This is the subtlest card in the deck and it deserves the time.

    Parsing the wire format is Bronze; changing a value is not. JSON arrives as bytes; turning those bytes into a typed column so the data is queryable at all is part of reading the source, not of interpreting it. So a cast is admissible at Bronze — on one condition: the cast must be total and lossless.

    The failure mode is what happens to a value that does not cast. If speed_kmh is the string "n/a" and the pipeline casts to double, Spark yields null, and an unparseable value has silently become a missing value. The information "the producer sent us the literal string n/a" is destroyed at the one layer that was supposed to preserve it. That is a NEVER-before-Bronze act wearing the costume of a cast.

    The correct pattern, and the one to put on the board:

    # Bronze: parse permissively, keep the original, never lose a row
.withColumn("_raw", col("value").cast("string"))            # the bytes, always retained
.withColumn("e", from_json(col("_raw"), GPS_SCHEMA, {"mode": "PERMISSIVE",
                                        "columnNameOfCorruptRecord": "_corrupt_record"}))
# rows that fail to parse land WITH their _corrupt_record, they are not dropped

    So: cast at Bronze only with a permissive mode and a corrupt-record column, so that failure is recorded rather than erased. A strict cast that nulls or drops belongs in Silver, where it can quarantine.

    Note the connection to CS-11: the incident is invisible to type checks precisely because the type never changed. Casting is not validation. A double is a double whether it means km/h or m/s.

    Q4. Card 13 — rolling_avg_speed_10m. Silver or Gold?

    Model answer. Gold, and the reasoning generalises to every windowed computation.

    The distinguishing question is not "is it a calculation?" but "is it a fact about a record, or a fact about a consumer's question?" rolling_avg_speed_10m is not a property of any GPS ping — it is a 10-minute window chosen because the ETA model's feature engineering wanted 10 minutes. A different consumer wants 5, or 30. The window length is a modelling decision, not a truth about the world, and modelling decisions live in Gold.

    Silver's job on speed is different and more fundamental: make speed_kmh actually mean km/h for every row (unit normalisation, card 4), so that any Gold consumer computing any window gets a defensible answer. Silver produces a trustworthy quantity; Gold produces an aggregate for a purpose.

    Sharpen it with the test from §8.1: if two consumers would legitimately want different versions of this transformation, it is Gold. If both consumers need it done the same way or neither can be trusted, it is Silver. Nobody wants speed in m/s for some purposes and km/h for others — that is Silver. Everybody wants their own window length — that is Gold.

    Flag the forward reference explicitly: rolling_avg_speed_10m is the exact column CS-11 corrupts, and the reason the corruption is fatal is that Silver failed to normalise the unit, so Gold's window faithfully averaged two incompatible quantities.

    Q5. Card 18 — rejecting a row that fails the schema contract. Surely rejecting bad data is good?

    Model answer. Rejecting is good. Rejecting before Bronze is not, and the distinction is the same one as Q2.

    A row that violates the schema contract is evidence that the producer sent something unexpected. It is often the single most valuable row in the batch — it is the first sign of a producer change, a firmware bug, an encoding problem, an upstream outage. Discarding it at ingest destroys the earliest available warning, and destroys it most reliably in exactly the situations that matter most.

    The correct pattern: Bronze lands everything, including malformed records, with a _corrupt_record column and its lineage metadata. Silver applies the contract and routes failures to silver.trips_quarantine. The gate then has three properties Bronze-side rejection cannot have: the bad rows are counted (so a spike is detectable), inspectable (so a human can diagnose), and recoverable (so a contract fixed on Tuesday can reprocess Monday's quarantine).

    The one-line rule: quarantine, never delete. A rejected row is a measurement; deleting it is choosing not to measure.

    Q6. Card 5 — hashing PII. Doesn't PDPL require this as early as possible?

    Model answer. PDPL requires minimisation and appropriate protection, and it is satisfied by access control on Bronze plus hashing at the Silver boundary — not by destroying identifiers at ingest.

    Three reasons hashing at Bronze is the wrong layer: 1. Bronze is where the erasure obligation is discharged. PDPL Art. 18 erasure requires deleting a specific data subject's rows (MERGE DELETE + VACUUM, per CONVENTIONS.md). If Bronze holds only irreversible hashes with no key mapping, you cannot reliably identify a subject's rows to erase them — a privacy control has obstructed a privacy right. 2. Salt rotation breaks history irreversibly. Key management is an operational lifecycle. Rotate the salt and Bronze's historical hashes no longer join to anything, forever. 3. The protection Bronze needs is access, not transformation. Column-level access control, short retention (30 days for raw GPS), and audit logging protect the data while keeping it usable for erasure and investigation.

    The correct boundary: hash and minimise at the Silver→Gold boundary, where CONVENTIONS.md's rule bites — never emit raw rider_id into BI or the online store. That is one enforceable checkpoint, in one publish job, auditable in one place. Which is also, note, exactly the boundary CS-08 identified for offline/online serving.

    Card 7 (drop lat/lon after deriving the geohash) is the same argument at higher stakes: precise location is sensitive-personal, the geohash is the minimised form, and the minimisation happens on the way into Silver/Gold — with raw coordinates retained in Bronze under a 30-day retention and strict access, then aged out by the retention job (card 20), not by the ingest job.

    8. Solution approach¶

    8.1 The four questions that decide any card¶

    Apply in order; the first that answers decides the layer.

    1. "If this is wrong, can I fix it by re-running?" — No → it must not happen before Bronze.
    2. "Does this remove or reinterpret anything the source sent?" — Yes → not Bronze.
    3. "Would two legitimate consumers want it done differently?" — Yes → Gold. No → Silver.
    4. "Is this a fact about the record, or a fact about somebody's question?" — Record → Silver. Question → Gold.

    8.2 The classification (facilitator key)¶

    # Transformation Layer Rationale (one line)
    1 Add _ingested_at, _source_file, _batch_id BRONZE Purely additive lineage metadata; mandated by CONVENTIONS.md and the basis of every later investigation
    2 Parse JSON into typed columns BRONZE Reading the wire format is not interpreting it — provided parsing is permissive and corrupt records are retained, not dropped
    3 Deduplicate on event_id SILVER The 0.4% duplicate rate is a producer health metric; Bronze must retain it so a firmware regression is visible
    4 Convert speed m/s → km/h (unit normalisation) SILVER Makes the column mean one thing for every row; must be applied per producer_version and is reversible by rebuild
    5 Hash driver_id / rider_id SILVER (boundary to Gold) Bronze needs the raw key to discharge PDPL erasure; protect Bronze with access control and 30-day retention instead
    6 Derive a precision-7 geohash from lat/lon SILVER Additive derivation and the PDPL-minimised form; it is a fact about the record, so it belongs to Silver, not to a consumer
    7 Drop lat/lon after deriving the geohash NEVER before Bronze Precise coordinates are the source's actual measurement; minimise on the way out of Bronze, and age raw coordinates out with the retention job (card 20)
    8 Normalise ts device-local → UTC SILVER Storage is UTC by convention; but the original offset must survive in Bronze, since a wrong-timezone producer is only diagnosable from what it sent
    9 Cast speed_kmh string → double BRONZE, conditionally Admissible only in permissive mode with a _corrupt_record column; a strict cast that nulls unparseable values is a silent deletion and belongs in Silver
    10 Filter pings with accuracy_m > 20 NEVER before Bronze Drops ~1% of rows and the entire accuracy distribution; degrading GPS across a device fleet becomes undetectable
    11 Join to zones.csv to attach zone_id SILVER Conforming join to reference data; a fact about the record, needed identically by every consumer
    12 Remove outliers (speed > 200 km/h) NEVER before Bronze (then SILVER as quarantine) 210 km/h may be a real fact about a real vehicle, or a sensor fault, or a unit change — you cannot know at ingest, and after deletion you can never know
    13 Compute rolling_avg_speed_10m GOLD The 10-minute window is a modelling choice made by one consumer; a different consumer wants a different window
    14 Assign a surrogate key to the vehicle dimension GOLD Surrogate keys serve dimensional modelling for BI; they are an artefact of the star schema, not of the world
    15 Repair a malformed record by inferring the missing field NEVER before Bronze Replaces what the source said with what we guessed; the guess is unmarked, unversioned and unfalsifiable afterwards
    16 Enrich with weather via a late-arriving join GOLD Late-arriving enrichment is consumer-specific and point-in-time sensitive (see CS-08); doing it early bakes in an as-of assumption
    17 Aggregate to zone × hour demand counts GOLD Aggregation to a grain is by definition consumer-shaped — this is gold.zone_hourly_demand
    18 Reject a row failing the schema contract SILVER (quarantine, never delete) A contract violation is the earliest warning of a producer change; Bronze must land it, Silver must quarantine it, nobody may delete it
    19 Compute label_actual_duration_min for training GOLD A label exists only for a model; it is a fact about a training question, not about a GPS ping
    20 Apply the PDPL retention delete + VACUUM BRONZE (as a governed lifecycle job, never as part of ingest) The one legitimate deletion from Bronze — executed by a separate, audited retention job under a documented legal basis, not silently by an ingestion pipeline

    Summary counts: BRONZE 4 (of which one conditional, one lifecycle) · SILVER 8 · GOLD 5 · NEVER-before-Bronze 4 (cards 7, 10, 12, 15).

    Note for the facilitator: cards 7, 10, 12 and 15 are precisely the four steps the drafted design in P2 wanted to do at ingest (plus dropping producer_version, which is card 1's inverse). The deck and the draft are the same argument seen twice.

    8.3 Exemplar ADR (hand out at minute 54, not before)¶

    # ADR-001 — Transformation placement for the Masar GPS pipeline

Status:   Accepted
Date:     2026-06-01
Deciders: Data Platform Lead, ML Platform Lead, Data Protection Officer
Context module: M1 — Evolution of Data Architecture

## Context

Masar ingests 15,000,000 GPS events per day from 14,000 vehicles (one ping per
vehicle per 5 s), landing via Kafka into the Lakehouse. Two consumers depend on
this feed: gold.eta_features (ETA and demand models) and gold.fact_trip (BI).

The drafted ingestion job performed deduplication, an accuracy filter, a unit
conversion, PII hashing and dropped producer_version before persisting to
bronze.gps_events. Each step reduced downstream work. Together they made the
landed data an interpretation of the source rather than a record of it.

Raw retention for this feed costs approximately SAR 1,100 per year. The steps
the draft proposed would save approximately SAR 15 per year.

## Decision

We adopt a medallion architecture as progressive refinement, with an explicit
fourth category of transformations that are prohibited before Bronze.

BRONZE — "what did the source actually say?"  Append-only, immutable.
  Permitted, and only these:
    - attach _ingested_at, _source_file, _batch_id
    - parse the wire format into typed columns in PERMISSIVE mode, retaining a
      _corrupt_record column so unparseable rows are landed, never dropped
    - the governed PDPL retention job (separate, audited, documented legal basis)

SILVER — "what is true about the world?"  Rebuildable from Bronze at any time.
    - deduplicate on event_id
    - normalise speed units to km/h, applied PER producer_version
    - normalise ts to UTC, retaining the original offset from Bronze
    - derive precision-7 geohash; conform-join to zones
    - validate against the data contract; route failures to quarantine
    - hash driver_id / rider_id at the boundary out of Silver

GOLD — "what does this consumer need?"  One Gold per consumer.
    - rolling_avg_speed_10m, rolling_zone_demand_15m and other windowed features
    - zone x hour demand aggregates
    - dimensional surrogate keys and the BI star schema
    - point-in-time weather enrichment; training labels

NEVER BEFORE BRONZE — prohibited upstream of Bronze persistence:
    - dropping rows for ANY reason (accuracy filters, outlier removal, contract
      violations, deduplication)
    - dropping or replacing columns (lat/lon after geohash; speed_kmh after
      conversion; producer_version)
    - repairing or inferring values
    - any strict cast that converts an unparseable value to null

producer_version is mandatory in Bronze and is never dropped. It is the only
column that correlates a change in the data with a change in a deployment.

## Consequences

Positive
  + Any Silver or Gold defect is repaired by re-running from Bronze. Bronze
    defects would be unrecoverable, so Bronze is kept minimal and additive.
  + Producer health is measurable: duplicate rate, accuracy distribution,
    parse-failure rate and unit distribution are all computable per
    producer_version because none of them were filtered away at ingest.
  + A semantic producer change (a unit change with an unchanged type) remains
    diagnosable, because both the original values and the producer version
    survive in Bronze.
  + PDPL erasure is executable, because Bronze retains the subject keys needed
    to identify a subject's rows.

Negative — accepted costs
  - Bronze stores ~1.4% of rows that will never be used downstream, and every
    duplicate. Cost: ~SAR 15/year. Accepted.
  - Silver carries more work per run (dedup, unit normalisation, validation),
    increasing Silver compute by an estimated 20%. Accepted.
  - Bronze holds raw lat/lon and raw driver_id, i.e. sensitive-personal and
    personal data. This is mitigated by column-level access control, a 30-day
    retention on raw GPS, audit logging, and the governed retention job — NOT
    by transformation at ingest. This is a real, ongoing obligation and it is
    the price of retaining erasure capability and diagnosability.
  - Consumers must not read Bronze directly. Enforced by access policy; a
    consumer reading Bronze bypasses every quality guarantee in the platform.

## Alternatives considered

A. Filter and normalise at ingest (the drafted design). Rejected: saves ~SAR 15
   per year and forfeits investigation of incidents costing millions. The
   asymmetry is not close.
B. Two Bronze tables — raw and cleaned. Rejected: a second copy is a second
   pipeline, and consumers will read whichever they find first (see CS-01).
C. Retain raw only for 7 days. Rejected: shorter than the observed detection
   lag for semantic defects (14 days at Muqeem, 5 days at Masar), so the
   evidence would expire before the incident is noticed.

    10. Debrief (5 minutes)¶

    1. Re-run the P2 hand count. Compare with the minute-9 counts. The room will have moved from marking one or two of the five steps to marking four or five. Say what changed: not their knowledge of the medallion names, but their possession of a test they can apply — if this is wrong, can I re-run?
    2. The asymmetry is the architecture. Silver and Gold mistakes are a re-run. Bronze mistakes are permanent. Every rule about Bronze follows from that one sentence, and none of them are about cleanliness.
    3. Price it out loud. SAR 1,100/year to retain everything. SAR 15/year saved by the "efficient" design. SAR 2.4M for the incident it would have made undiagnosable. Bronze is the cheapest insurance in the platform and it is the first thing an efficiency review proposes to cut.
    4. Name the four NEVER cards — drop rows, drop columns, repair values, filter outliers — and note that the drafted job in P2 did all four. Efficient, sensible-looking, and it would have quietly removed the platform's ability to explain itself.
    5. Forward reference, stated plainly. "On Day 5 you will investigate an incident in which a producer changed speed_kmh from km/h to m/s while keeping the same field name and the same type. You will solve it with two columns: the original speed_kmh values and producer_version. The design on this slide (P2) deletes one and converts the other. Whether you can solve that incident was decided here, on Day 1, in the ingestion job."
    6. The course question. "What happens to Masar's AI system if Bronze is not immutable?" Every downstream layer becomes unrebuildable, every incident becomes unfalsifiable, and every model degradation becomes a matter of opinion rather than of evidence.

    11. The one sentence participants should leave with¶

    Bronze answers "what did the source actually say", Silver answers "what is true about the world", and Gold answers "what does this consumer need" — so the only transformations permitted before Bronze are additive ones, because a Silver or Gold mistake costs a re-run while a Bronze mistake costs the truth, permanently, for about fifteen riyals a year.
