    CS-06 — The Silent Currency Bug at "Muqeem" Analytics¶

    Field Value
    Case ID CS-06
    Module M6 — Data Quality, Observability and Governance
    Day / Hour Day 4 · Hour 5 (after the data-quality pyramid, before or alongside LAB-06's gate)
    Duration 45 minutes
    Format Investigation → design exercise
    Group size 3–4 per team
    Deliverable (a) The one check that would have caught this, written as a Great Expectations expectation with its baseline definition; (b) the gate ruling — fail-fast, quarantine, or alert-only — scoped precisely; (c) a correction plan; (d) the answer to "how do you avoid baselining the bug?"
    Materials Evidence pack §5 per team (6 pages); data/fixtures/currency_shift/trips_2026-06-05_currency_shift.csv open on the projector (7,230 rows, 2,125 shifted, mean 37.40 → 3,739.86)
    Facilitator prep 15 min. Be ready to compute a PSI by hand on the projector — teams will ask "how do you actually measure drift?" and the answer must be arithmetic, not a library call

    1. Scenario¶

    "Muqeem" (مقيم) runs analytics for a mobility operator. It ingests trip records from a third-party vendor that operates one city's fleet under a franchise agreement.

    On 12 June 2026, the vendor deployed a change to its export job. A fare field that had always been sent in halalas (1/100 SAR) began being sent in riyals — a 100× reduction in the numeric value — for that one city only. The vendor's release notes described it as "align currency units with the platform standard." Nobody at Muqeem read them; nobody at Muqeem had been told they existed.

    Every ingested row is individually valid. The schema is unchanged. The type is unchanged. The values are plausible. No pipeline errored, no test failed, no alert fired.

    Over the following two weeks, the demand-pricing model — which uses mean fare per zone as a demand-intensity proxy — degraded. It was noticed on 26 June when a regional manager asked why the city's surge had not risen during a sandstorm.

    2. Business context¶

    Item Figure
    Cities on the platform 5
    Cities affected 1 (the vendor's franchise city)
    Share of platform trips affected ~19%
    Detection lag 14 days
    Mean fare, affected city, before the change SAR 3,741 (halalas, i.e. SAR 37.41)
    Mean fare, affected city, after the change SAR 37.41 (riyals)
    Mispricing exposure — under-surged trips, 14 days SAR 1,340,000 in foregone revenue
    Revenue forecast error for the quarter SAR 8,700,000 overstated (the forecast used pre-change fares for the whole city)
    Cost of retraining and re-baselining the pricing model ~SAR 190,000
    Rows to correct 214,000

    Note the scale relationship the class should see: the data defect cost SAR 1.34M in two weeks, and one distribution check — perhaps two hours of engineering — would have caught it on day one. Data quality is the highest-return engineering work in a platform, and it is the first thing cut.

    3. Technical challenge¶

    No row-level rule can catch a 100× shift that stays inside a plausible range. Every row passes:

    • schema check — passes (same field, same double);
    • not-null — passes;
    • range check fare_sar BETWEEN 0 AND 20000 — passes (37.41 is further inside the range than 3,741 was);
    • referential integrity — passes;
    • freshness, volume, row count — all normal.

    Only a check that looks at the shape of a population rather than the validity of a row can see it. That is the top two tiers of the data-quality pyramid — distribution and reconciliation — and it is exactly the tier most teams never build.

    4. Constraints¶

    • Cannot re-ingest. Bronze is immutable and the vendor retains 7 days. (Again, frame immutability as the enabler: bronze holds both the pre- and post-change values, which is what makes the correction provable.)
    • The other four cities are unaffected and must not be blocked while the affected city is corrected.
    • The correction must be auditable — the franchise agreement has a revenue-share clause, so corrected figures are contractually consequential.
    • Detection must be reduced from 14 days to ≤ 1 day for the next occurrence.
    • The vendor cannot be compelled to change process this quarter; the control must sit on Muqeem's side of the boundary.

    P3 — The quality suite that was in production on 12 June¶

    suite = [
    expect_column_to_exist("fare_sar"),
    expect_column_values_to_be_of_type("fare_sar", "DoubleType"),
    expect_column_values_to_not_be_null("fare_sar"),
    expect_column_values_to_be_between("fare_sar", min_value=0, max_value=20000),
    expect_column_values_to_be_in_set("status",
        ["completed","cancelled_rider","cancelled_driver","no_show"]),
    expect_column_values_to_be_unique("trip_id"),
    expect_table_row_count_to_be_between(min_value=40000, max_value=60000),
]
# Result 2026-06-12: 7/7 PASSED

    Seven expectations, all green, on the day the data broke. Read the result line aloud. A green test suite is evidence that the checks you wrote passed; it is not evidence that the data is correct.

    P4 — Distribution check, computed after the fact (what would have fired)¶

    Population Stability Index for fare_sar, JED, 10 deciles, baseline 2026-05-01 → 2026-05-31:

    Date PSI vs baseline Verdict at threshold 0.20
    2026-06-10 0.014 stable
    2026-06-11 0.011 stable
    2026-06-12 6.81 breach — every decile boundary crossed
    2026-06-13 6.79 breach

    And the simpler check that also fires, computable in one query:

    SELECT city, DATE(pickup_ts) AS d, AVG(fare_sar) AS mean_fare
FROM   silver.trips
WHERE  status='completed' AND DATE(pickup_ts) >= current_date() - 30
GROUP  BY city, d;
-- alert when mean_fare deviates > 25% from the trailing 28-day median for the SAME city

    PSI of 6.81 against a threshold of 0.20. This is not a subtle statistical signal requiring a sophisticated detector — it is a 34× threshold breach. It went undetected for 14 days because nobody was computing it at all.

    P5 — The vendor's release note (retrieved on 27 June, after the fact)¶

    vendor-export v3.7.0 — 2026-06-12
* Align currency units with platform standard (halalas -> SAR) in trip export.
* Fix pagination bug in incremental extract.
* Bump dependency: jackson 2.15.2 -> 2.16.1

    One bullet, second-hand, in a changelog nobody at Muqeem subscribed to, describing a semantic change to a financial field as an "alignment". Every element of this is realistic and every element is a control failure: there was no data contract, so a unit change was a routine release note rather than a breaking change.

    data/fixtures/currency_shift/trips_2026-06-05_currency_shift.csv - 7,230 rows; 2,125 shifted; mean fare_sar before 37.40 → after 3,739.86 (the shift runs in the opposite direction to Muqeem's, which is useful — it forces teams to write a symmetric check rather than one that only detects a decrease).

    Run the LAB-06 gate against it during the debrief.

    6. Facilitation script (minute-by-minute)¶

    Min Activity Facilitator does Watch for
    0–4 Frame. Project P1's two rows. Say nothing for fifteen seconds. Then: "Which of these two rows is invalid?" The answer is neither. Let someone say it. Teams that immediately spot the 100×. Ask them: "now write me the row-level rule that catches it." They cannot.
    4–6 Distribute P2, P3, P5 (hold P4 back). State the four deliverables. — —
    6–15 Round 1 — Why did nothing fire? Teams audit the P3 suite expectation by expectation and state, for each, why it passed. Circulate. Demand a per-expectation answer, not a summary. Teams that say "the range was too wide." Push: what range would have caught it and not produced false alarms? (Answer: none — 37.41 is a more plausible fare than 3,741.)
    15–18 Board the pyramid. Build schema → constraint → referential → distribution → reconciliation on the board and mark where the P3 suite stops. The visual — a suite that stops two tiers below the defect — is the module in one image. —
    18–20 Reveal P4. PSI 6.81 against a 0.20 threshold. Ask: "how long would this check have taken to write?" The answer is roughly two hours, against SAR 1.34M. Let the room do that arithmetic.
    20–33 Round 2 — Design. All four deliverables. Push hardest on the baseline question. Ask every table: "Your baseline is a trailing 28-day window. The bug has now been live for 14 of those days. What does your check say tomorrow?" This is the "baseline-the-bug" trap and it is the highest-value moment of the case.
    33–41 Tribunal. Each team states its gate ruling — block, quarantine, or alert — and is challenged by the room. Play the operations manager: "You just blocked 19% of my platform's trips because a statistic looked odd. Justify that." Teams that block all five cities. Scope matters as much as the ruling.
    41–45 Debrief + live drill. Run the LAB-06 gate against currency_shift. Close on §11. Show the gate quarantining the affected city while promoting the rest. —

    7. Discussion questions with model answers¶

    Q1. Why did every schema and range check pass while the data was wrong?

    Model answer. Because those checks answer the question "is this row well-formed?", and the row was perfectly well-formed. They cannot answer "does this population mean what it used to mean?", which is a different question requiring a different kind of evidence: a comparison against history rather than against a rule.

    Work through P3 expectation by expectation — this is worth doing explicitly:

    Expectation Why it passed
    column exists The vendor renamed nothing
    type is Double 34.11 and 3402.0 are both doubles
    not null Values are present
    between 0 and 20000 34.11 is deeper inside the range than 3402.0 was — the corrupted data is more conformant
    status in set Untouched
    trip_id unique Untouched
    row count 40k–60k Volume unchanged; the vendor's job ran normally

    The fourth row is the one to dwell on. A widened range does not help; a narrowed range would have to be narrow enough to exclude 34.11, which would then reject every legitimate fare in the other four cities. There is no row-level rule that separates these two populations, because the defect is not in any row — it is in the relationship between the rows and their history.

    The generalisation: schema and constraint checks validate syntax and bounds; they are blind to semantics. Semantic defects — unit changes, currency changes, encoding changes, a field's meaning being redefined — are always type-compatible, because the type was never the thing that changed. (Point forward: CS-11 is the same class of defect on speed_kmh, and it is the signature incident of the course precisely because this class is the one that platforms are structurally blind to.)

    Q2. What baseline period do you freeze, and how do you avoid "baselining the bug"?

    Model answer. The trap: a rolling baseline computed over the trailing N days will, after N days of corrupted data, treat the corruption as normal and go quiet. Muqeem's 14-day detection lag is roughly half the width of a typical 28-day rolling window — a rolling baseline would have started losing sensitivity from day one and been fully desensitised by day 28. Worse, it would then flag the correction as an anomaly.

    The defence is layered, and teams should propose at least three of these five:

    1. A frozen, human-approved baseline. Pick a period certified as known-good (Muqeem: May 2026, before the vendor release), freeze it, version it in git, and change it only through review. The baseline becomes an artefact with an owner, not a side effect of a window function.
    2. Never let a baseline auto-refresh across an unexplained breach. If drift fired and was resolved as a true positive, the baseline must be re-cut deliberately. If it fired and was dismissed, the dismissal must be recorded with a reason. A baseline that silently absorbs whatever it sees is not a baseline.
    3. Anchor to something outside the data feed. A drift check compares data to data and can be fooled by data. A reconciliation check compares two independent measurements of the same physical fact and cannot: here, fare_sar / distance_km should sit near a stable SAR-per-km for a given city and surge, and that ratio is anchored by the tariff, which is a published business fact. When the ratio moves 100× and the tariff did not change, the data is wrong — regardless of what any baseline says.
    4. Multiple time horizons. Compare against yesterday, against the trailing 28 days, and against the frozen baseline. A step change breaks all three simultaneously; a gradual real trend breaks only the long one. The pattern of which horizons fire tells you what kind of change you are looking at.
    5. Segment by producer. Compute drift per city, per vendor, per producer_version. An aggregate over all five cities dilutes a 100× shift in one city into a ~19% platform-wide move that may fall under a naive threshold. Always segment drift by the dimension along which change is deployed — this is the single most transferable lesson in the case and it is exactly what CS-11 will require for producer_version.

    Q3. Fail-fast or quarantine here, and scoped to what?

    Model answer. Quarantine, scoped to the affected city's batch. Not fail-fast, and not alert-only.

    The reasoning teams must produce:

    • Not fail-fast, because fail-fast blocks the whole batch, and 81% of the platform's trips are unaffected. Blocking four healthy cities to stop one broken feed converts a data-quality incident into an availability incident. Fail-fast is right when the defect threatens integrity — a null primary key, a broken referential relationship, a schema violation that would corrupt every downstream join — because then partial promotion produces silent corruption. A distribution shift in one segment does not threaten integrity; it threatens meaning in a bounded scope.
    • Not alert-only, because alert-only promotes the bad rows into silver while sending an email. That is the sim-alertonly failure: the alarm rings and the corrupt data flows anyway, and by the time a human reads the email the model has trained on it. A gate that only notifies is not a gate.
    • Quarantine, scoped to city = 'JED' AND pickup_ts >= '2026-06-12': the affected rows go to silver.trips_quarantine with the failing expectation and the batch id recorded; the other four cities promote normally; downstream consumers of JED see no new data rather than wrong data; and a human decides within one business day.

    Then the point that separates a good answer from an excellent one: "no data" is a visible, honest failure state and "wrong data" is an invisible one. A dashboard with a gap prompts a question; a dashboard with plausible wrong numbers prompts a decision. Quarantine converts a silent semantic corruption into a loud, bounded operational gap. That conversion is the entire purpose of a quality gate.

    The gate ruling table for the deliverable:

    Signal Ruling Scope
    Null trip_id / rider_id Block (fail-fast) Whole batch — keys are integrity
    Referential break (driver_id not in drivers) Block Whole batch
    Schema change (new/removed/retyped column) Block Whole batch
    Distribution drift, one segment, PSI > 0.20 Quarantine The segment only
    Distribution drift, all segments simultaneously Block Whole batch — a platform-wide shift is more likely a pipeline bug than a data event
    Range violation, < 0.5% of rows Quarantine The rows
    Freshness late by < 2 h Alert —

    Q4. What lineage would have shortened detection from two weeks to one day?

    Model answer. Two distinct things, and teams often conflate them:

    Column-level lineage answers "what does this feed?". When fare_sar drifts, lineage names every consumer within seconds: silver.trips → gold.fact_trip (revenue dashboard) → gold.zone_hourly_demand (pricing model input) → the demand-pricing model → the surge multiplier → the quarterly forecast. Without it, the 26 June question "why did surge not rise in the sandstorm" takes days to trace back to a fare field. With it, the drift alert on 12 June arrives pre-annotated with its blast radius, which is what makes an on-call engineer act on it rather than triage it.

    Source-and-version lineage answers "what changed?". Every bronze row carries _source_file, _batch_id, _ingested_at (the CONVENTIONS.md requirement), and — where the source declares one — the producer's version. Then the first query after any alert is:

    SELECT vendor_export_version, city, COUNT(*), AVG(fare_sar)
FROM   bronze.trips
WHERE  DATE(_ingested_at) >= current_date() - 3
GROUP  BY 1, 2;

    which lays the release boundary directly against the metric shift and turns a two-week investigation into a two-minute one. Muqeem could not run this query because the vendor's export version was never captured. Retrieving P5's release note on 27 June required a human to think to ask the vendor for it.

    The organisational half of the answer matters as much: subscribe to the producer's release notes and treat a semantic change as a breaking change by contract. A data contract that specifies fare_sar: double permits everything that happened. A contract that specifies fare_sar: double, unit: SAR, monotonic_with: distance_km, expected_mean_range: [25, 55] makes the vendor's release a contract violation detectable at the boundary, on the first row of the first batch.

    Q5 (reserve). The vendor's change made the data more correct — SAR is the platform standard. Was the vendor wrong?

    Model answer. The vendor's destination was right and its process was wrong, and separating these two is the point. Moving to SAR aligns with the platform standard; doing it in a patch release, announced in one changelog bullet, on a live financial field, with no contract, no version signal in the data, and no coordination window, is the failure. The correct process: a new field (fare_sar_v2) or an explicit unit field carried in the payload, dual-emission for an overlap period, a contract version bump, and a coordinated cutover.

    The transferable rule: there is no such thing as a safe silent semantic change. If the meaning of a value changes, the name or the contract version must change with it, so that consumers break loudly at the boundary instead of degrading quietly downstream.

    8. Solution approach¶

    8.1 The check that catches it (deliverable a)¶

    # quality/suites/silver_trips_suite.py — the distribution tier
expect_column_mean_to_be_between(
    column="fare_sar",
    min_value=lambda ctx: ctx.baseline["fare_sar_mean"] * 0.75,
    max_value=lambda ctx: ctx.baseline["fare_sar_mean"] * 1.25,
    # CRITICAL: evaluated per city. An aggregate over 5 cities dilutes a 100x
    # shift in one city to a ~19% platform move and may not breach at all.
    group_by="city",
    meta={
        "tier": "distribution",
        "baseline": "frozen 2026-05-01..2026-05-31, approved by data-platform lead, git-versioned",
        "on_failure": "quarantine the failing city's batch; promote all other cities",
    },
)

# the reconciliation tier - anchored outside the feed, cannot be fooled by the feed
expect_column_pair_ratio_to_be_between(
    numerator="fare_sar", denominator="distance_km",
    min_value=1.8, max_value=6.5,           # SAR per km, from the published tariff
    group_by=["city", "surge_multiplier"],
    meta={"tier": "reconciliation",
          "why": "the tariff is a business fact independent of the data feed"},
)

    Both are needed. The first detects the change; the second is immune to a poisoned baseline because its reference point is a published tariff rather than the data's own history.

    8.2 The correction plan (deliverable c)¶

    1. Do not modify bronze. It holds the vendor's actual output and is the evidence for the franchise revenue-share discussion.
    2. Quarantine forward from the detection moment — the gate stops further JED rows entering silver.
    3. Determine the boundary exactly, from bronze rather than by assumption: 
      SELECT DATE(_ingested_at) AS d, MIN(_batch_id), AVG(fare_sar)
FROM bronze.trips WHERE city='JED' AND status='completed'
  AND DATE(_ingested_at) BETWEEN '2026-06-09' AND '2026-06-15'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;   -- the step is between two adjacent batch ids
    4. Correct in silver by MERGE (the M4 skill), normalising the affected window and stamping provenance: 
      MERGE INTO silver.trips t
USING (SELECT trip_id, fare_sar * 100 AS fare_sar_corrected FROM bronze.trips
       WHERE city='JED' AND _batch_id >= :first_affected_batch) s
ON t.trip_id = s.trip_id
WHEN MATCHED THEN UPDATE SET t.fare_sar = s.fare_sar_corrected,
                             t._corrected_by = 'INC-2026-0626',
                             t._corrected_at = current_timestamp();
      Idempotent: re-running updates the same rows to the same values. Auditable: DESCRIBE HISTORY shows the commit; _corrected_by names the incident.
    5. Rebuild the affected gold partitions and re-baseline the pricing model on corrected data. Record the honest metric before and after — do not quietly ship the better number.
    6. Restate the quarterly forecast with the correction disclosed. The SAR 8.7M overstatement was reported to leadership and must be unreported to them.
    7. Close the loop at the boundary: a data contract with the vendor specifying unit and expected distribution, a subscription to their release notes, and the vendor's export version carried as a column in every batch.

    8.3 Observability that would have made this a one-day incident¶

    Signal Implementation Detection time
    Per-city mean-fare drift vs frozen baseline GX expectation, nightly + per-batch < 24 h
    PSI on fare_sar per city, 10 deciles Nightly job vs frozen baseline, threshold 0.20 < 24 h
    fare_sar / distance_km reconciliation vs tariff Per-batch expectation < 1 h
    Vendor export version carried into bronze Contract + ingest change Enables 2-minute root cause
    Column-level lineage from fare_sar to the pricing model Catalog Turns an alert into an actionable blast radius
    Model input-distribution monitor on zone_hourly_demand Drift monitor on features, not just on sources Catches it even if the source check is missed

    The last row is the defence in depth that CS-11 will make mandatory: monitor the features the model consumes, not only the sources you ingest.

    9. What good looks like vs common wrong turns¶

    What good looks like Common wrong turn
    Explains per-expectation why all 7 checks passed Concludes "the tests were bad" without saying which and why
    Recognises no row-level rule can separate the populations Proposes a tighter range, which then rejects legitimate fares in the other four cities
    Computes drift per city, and says why aggregation would hide it Computes a platform-wide mean, dilutes 100× in one city to ~19%, and misses it again
    Freezes and versions a known-good baseline with a named owner Uses a trailing 28-day rolling baseline that absorbs the bug within a fortnight

    10. Debrief (5 minutes)¶

    1. Project P3's last line: 7/7 PASSED. Every test green on the day the data broke. A green suite tells you the checks you wrote passed. It tells you nothing whatsoever about the checks you did not write. Test coverage is a map of your imagination, not of your risk.
    2. Walk the pyramid. Schema → constraint → referential → distribution → reconciliation. Muqeem built the bottom three and stopped. The defect lived in the top two. The bottom three are cheap, fast and reflexively built; the top two require a baseline and a business anchor, which means they require a decision and an owner — which is why they are the ones that do not get built.
    3. PSI 6.81 against a threshold of 0.20. Not a marginal signal. A 34× breach, invisible for two weeks because nobody was computing it. The check is about two hours of work. The incident cost SAR 1.34M plus an SAR 8.7M forecast restatement. Let the class do the ratio out loud.
    4. Segment by the dimension along which change is deployed. Muqeem needed to segment by city because that is how the vendor's change landed. Tomorrow's incident segments by producer_version — and you will meet it on Day 5.
    5. Gates must act, not notify. Quarantine converts an invisible semantic corruption into a visible, bounded operational gap. "No data" asks a question; "wrong data" makes a decision.
    6. Run the fixture live. data/fixtures/currency_shift/trips_2026-06-05_currency_shift.csv — 2,125 of 7,230 rows shifted, mean 37.40 → 3,739.86, in the opposite direction. Show the gate quarantining and the other cities promoting. Then note: your check must be symmetric, because the next one will go the other way.
    7. The course question. "What happens to Masar's AI system if a source silently changes the meaning of a field?" The model degrades over weeks while every operational signal stays green, and the business notices only when a human asks why reality and the dashboard disagree. This is the exact shape of CS-11, which you will investigate tomorrow — with speed_kmh instead of fare_sar, and with the AI, not BI, as the only casualty.

    11. The one sentence participants should leave with¶

    Row-level validity is not correctness: a 100× unit change passes every schema, null and range check ever written, so a platform that stops at the bottom of the data-quality pyramid is structurally blind to exactly the class of defect that damages AI systems most — and the only cures are a frozen, segmented distribution baseline and a reconciliation anchored to a fact outside the data feed.
