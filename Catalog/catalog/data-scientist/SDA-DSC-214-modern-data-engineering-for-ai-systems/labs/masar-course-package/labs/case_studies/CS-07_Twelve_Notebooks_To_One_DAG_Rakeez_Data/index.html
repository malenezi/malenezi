    CS-07 — From Twelve Notebooks to One DAG at "Rakeez" Data¶

    Field Value
    Case ID CS-07
    Module M7 — Building the Mini-Lakehouse (Orchestration)
    Day / Hour Day 5 · Hour 1 (before LAB-07, as the design brief LAB-07 implements)
    Duration 45 minutes
    Format Design exercise (wall-based DAG construction + migration plan)
    Group size 4 per team
    Deliverable (a) The DAG on the wall — 12 task cards arranged with dependencies drawn; (b) an idempotency ruling for each task (append / merge / replaceWhere / overwrite) with the write mode named; (c) a four-phase incremental migration plan with a reconciliation gate between phases
    Materials Twelve task cards per team (text in §5 P1, print and cut); large wall space or A1 paper; markers; sticky arrows
    Facilitator prep 15 min. Print and cut the card decks (12 cards × number of teams). Have the LAB-07 orchestration/dags/masar_medallion.py open but not projected

    1. Scenario¶

    "Rakeez" (ركيز) runs the analytics platform for a mobility operator. The platform is twelve notebooks, executed by hand every morning by whoever is available, in an order that lives in one senior engineer's head and, partially, in a wiki page last edited fourteen months ago.

    On Sunday 7 June a new hire ran them. She ran them in the order they appear in the folder listing, which is alphabetical. Notebook 08_gold_zone_demand therefore ran before 11_silver_trips_refresh.

    No error occurred. Every notebook completed successfully. The gold table was rebuilt from Saturday's silver data.

    The 08:00 operations review allocated Sunday's drivers from Saturday's demand map. Nobody noticed until Tuesday.

    2. Business context¶

    Item Figure
    Notebooks executed daily by hand 12
    Time to run them, when nothing goes wrong 55 min of an engineer's morning
    Mornings per year ~250
    Engineer-days consumed annually by manual execution ~29
    Drivers allocated from the stale demand map on 7 June 3,180
    Estimated misallocation cost, one day SAR 340,000 in lost trips and elevated rider wait
    Days before anyone noticed 2
    Engineers on the team 3
    People who know the correct execution order 1 (on annual leave in August)
    Prior incidents of the same class in 12 months (from the wiki's edit history) 4

    The SAR 340,000 is the visible cost. The structural cost is the bus factor of one, and the fact that the platform's correctness depends on a human remembering a sequence at 07:00 — which is a control that degrades with fatigue, holidays, turnover, and the alphabet.

    3. Technical challenge¶

    Convert an implicit, human-ordered, non-idempotent sequence into an explicit, ordered, idempotent, recoverable pipeline — without a rewrite. The logic in the twelve notebooks is correct and battle-tested; it is the execution model that is broken.

    Four properties are missing and must be added:

    1. Explicit ordering — dependencies declared in code, not remembered.
    2. Idempotency — every task safe to re-run, so recovery is "run it again" rather than "reason about what already happened."
    3. Observability — a failed or skipped step must be visible; today a stale table looks identical to a fresh one.
    4. Recoverability — a failure at step 9 must not require manual reconstruction of steps 1–8.

    4. Constraints¶

    • The logic must be preserved, and preservation must be proved by reconciliation, not asserted. The business will not accept "we rewrote it and it looks right."
    • The team is three engineers with a full backlog; no capacity for a big-bang rewrite.
    • Runs must be reproducible by anyone, including someone hired next month.
    • Must be recoverable after a mid-pipeline failure without a senior engineer's intervention.
    • The migration must not stop the daily platform for a single day.

    P2 — The wiki page (last edited 2025-04-19)¶

    Morning run order 1. Run the four ingest notebooks (01–04). 03 can be skipped on weekends. 2. Run 11 and 12 (silver). Note: 11 must come after 05 or the quarantine table is stale. 3. Run 06 and 07. 4. Then the gold ones (08, 09, 10). Order between them does not matter 10 must be last, see incident 2024-11-02. 5. If anything fails, ask Faisal.

    Three failure signatures in one page: a conditional ("can be skipped on weekends") that a tired human will get wrong; a struck-through correction encoding an incident nobody documented properly; and a named person as the recovery procedure.

    P4 — What happened when they tried to fix it by re-running¶

    At 09:40 on 7 June, once the staleness was suspected, an engineer re-ran 08_gold_zone_demand:

    09:41  08_gold_zone_demand  OK  720 rows inserted
-- gold.zone_hourly_demand now contains 1,440 rows for 2026-06-07
-- every hour of every city appears TWICE, with different values
-- the dashboard's SUM() doubles; demand appears to have spiked 100%

    INSERT INTO is not idempotent. The attempted fix made it worse, and the second failure was louder than the first — a doubled demand map is at least visible. Note for the class: three of the twelve notebooks use INSERT INTO at an aggregate grain, so this trap is armed in three places.

    6. Facilitation script (minute-by-minute)¶

    Min Activity Facilitator does Watch for
    0–4 Frame. Read the scenario. Project P3 and read 12/12 OK aloud. Ask: "This run destroyed a day of dispatch. What in this log tells you that?" The answer: nothing does. Write "green is not correct" on the board. Teams that blame the new hire. Redirect immediately and firmly: she followed the only ordering visible to her.
    4–7 Distribute card decks (P1) and P2. Give each team wall space. Physically hand over the cut cards — the tactile step matters for engagement. —
    7–20 Round 1 — Build the DAG. Arrange all 12 cards; draw every dependency arrow. Circulate. One question: "Point at the edge that would have prevented 7 June." Teams that arrange in numeric order without reading the card bodies. Teams that forget 05 → 11.
    20–24 Cross-check. Each team walks to the next team's wall and marks, in a different colour, any edge they believe is missing or wrong. Enforce silence during the walk; disputes are resolved at minute 24, not during. The most-disputed edge is 05_quality_gate → 11_silver_trips, which is exactly the right thing to argue about.
    24–34 Round 2 — Idempotency ruling per task. For each of the 12 cards write the correct write mode: append / merge / replaceWhere / overwrite. Then reveal P4. Hand out P4 at minute 28, mid-round. Teams that had left INSERT INTO on the gold tasks will visibly react. Teams that answer "overwrite" for everything. Ask what that does to bronze.trips history.
    34–41 Round 3 — Migration plan. Four phases, with a reconciliation gate between each. Constraint: the platform runs every day throughout. Ask each team: "Which phase delivers value first, and what would you cut if you had two weeks instead of eight?" Big-bang plans. Also plans that begin with a rewrite of the notebook logic.
    41–45 Debrief. §10. Hand into LAB-07. Close on §11. The bridge: "In the next hour you build this DAG for Masar, then you break it and watch gold get held back." —

    7. Discussion questions with model answers¶

    Q1. Why is "run these in order" a latent production incident?

    Model answer. Because it stores a correctness-critical invariant in the least durable medium available: human memory, and then relies on that memory being accessed correctly under the worst conditions — early morning, time pressure, staff turnover, holidays, and an incident already in progress.

    Four properties make it a latent incident rather than merely a risk:

    • The failure is silent. Running gold before silver produces no error, because the gold job reads a table that exists and contains data. It is yesterday's data, and no component in the system has any way to know that was not the intention.
    • The failure is delayed. Two days elapsed. The blast radius (drivers allocated, trips lost, riders waiting) had fully materialised before anyone knew.
    • It degrades rather than breaks. Four incidents in twelve months, each survived, each reinforcing the belief that the system works. Systems that fail loudly get fixed; systems that fail quietly get normalised.
    • It is anti-correlated with capability. The senior engineer's competence is what makes the arrangement viable, which is precisely what removes the pressure to fix it. The wiki page's step 5 — "ask Faisal" — is a single point of failure written down as a procedure.

    The sharp formulation for the board: an implicit dependency is a dependency that the computer cannot check. The moment the ordering exists only in a person, the system has no ability to distinguish a correct run from an incorrect one — which is exactly what 12/12 OK in P3 proves.

    Q2. Which property — ordering, idempotency, or observability — would have prevented the stale dashboard, and how?

    Model answer. Prevention, detection, and recovery are three different jobs and each property does one:

    • Ordering prevents it. A declared dependency 11_silver_trips_refresh >> 08_gold_zone_demand makes the 7 June run impossible. The scheduler will not start 08 until 11 has succeeded. This is the direct answer.
    • Observability would have detected it. Even with the wrong order, a freshness assertion on the gold task's input ("silver.trips must contain rows with trip_date = {{ ds }}") fails the task loudly at 07:14 instead of producing a green tick. Detection time falls from 2 days to 2 minutes.
    • Idempotency would have made the repair safe. P4 shows what happened without it: the attempted fix doubled the mart. With replaceWhere on the date partition, re-running 08 after 11 is a complete, correct, one-command repair.

    The answer teams should converge on: ordering is necessary but not sufficient. Orchestration guarantees a task runs after its declared upstream succeeded; it cannot guarantee the upstream produced today's data — a task can succeed while writing nothing. That gap is closed by data-aware assertions on inputs, not by the DAG topology. Teams that say "ordering, and that is enough" should be asked: "What if 11_silver_trips_refresh succeeds but the source file was missing, so it merged zero rows?" The DAG is green; gold is stale again.

    So: ordering + input freshness assertions + idempotent writes — the three together, each doing a different job.

    Q3. How do you migrate incrementally without a big-bang rewrite?

    Model answer. The principle: change the execution model first, the code last, and prove equivalence at every step. The twelve notebooks contain correct logic; the value is in orchestrating them, not in rewriting them.

    Phase Duration What changes Risk Reconciliation gate before proceeding
    1. Wrap, do not rewrite 1 week Each notebook becomes a parameterised, callable task (papermill, a %run, or a thin main(ds)). Zero logic changes. The DAG runs them in the wiki's order. Very low DAG output must be byte-identical to a manual run on the same day. Run both for 5 days.
    2. Declare real dependencies 1 week Replace the wiki's linear order with the true dependency graph. Tasks that can run in parallel now do. Add input freshness assertions. Low Same outputs; wall-clock time drops (~55 min → ~18 min). The 7 June failure mode is now impossible.
    3. Make every task idempotent 2–3 weeks Convert INSERT INTO → MERGE / replaceWhere per §8.2, one task at a time, each with its own reconciliation. Make the quality gate exit non-zero on integrity failures. Medium — this is the only phase touching write behaviour Per task: run twice, assert row counts identical and numTargetRowsUpdated = 0 on the second run.
    4. Operationalise 1 week Retries with backoff, SLA alerting, OPTIMIZE/VACUUM as DAG tasks, backfill by date parameter, on-call runbook that says "re-run the DAG" and nothing else. Low A deliberate kill-and-restart drill: fail a task mid-run, re-run the whole DAG, assert a clean identical state.

    Two rules to state explicitly: - Never change orchestration and logic in the same commit. If output changes, you must know which of the two caused it. - Phase 1 delivers most of the value. After one week, the run is one command, reproducible by anyone, with a log. The bus factor of one is already gone. Teams proposing an eight-week plan whose first value lands in week six have mis-sequenced it.

    Q4. What does "one command runs the platform" buy the team culturally, not just technically?

    Model answer. This question is not soft; it is where the durable value is, and it is worth protecting time for.

    • It removes fear. Today nobody re-runs anything, because re-running might double a mart (P4). Fear of re-running is why the 7 June error sat for two days: investigating meant touching production. When every task is idempotent, investigation becomes free, and free investigation is the precondition for a team that fixes things early.
    • It distributes competence. The new hire's failure was an onboarding failure, not a personal one. A DAG is executable documentation: the dependency graph is the wiki page, and it cannot go fourteen months out of date because the platform runs from it.
    • It converts heroism into engineering. "Ask Faisal" is a procedure that fails in August. On-call becomes a role a junior can hold, which is what makes on-call sustainable and what lets the senior engineer work on something new.
    • It changes what the team argues about. Time currently spent reconstructing what ran, in what order, from which data, becomes time spent on the actual questions — grain, feature correctness, cost. The 29 engineer-days per year are the smaller half of that gain.
    • It makes incidents blameless by construction. After the migration there is no ordering for a human to get wrong, so an incident cannot be attributed to a person's memory. Systems that make individual error impossible are the only systems in which blameless post-mortems are honest rather than polite. (Point forward: CS-11's post-incident review depends on exactly this.)

    8. Solution approach¶

    8.1 The correct DAG¶

                     ┌── 01_ingest_trips ──┐
                 │                      │
 (sources) ──────┼── 02_ingest_gps ─────┼──┐
                 │                      │  │
                 ├── 03_ingest_drivers ─┤  │
                 │                      │  │
                 └── 04_ingest_payments ┘  │
                          │               │
                          ▼               │
                 05_quality_gate_trips     │      (must FAIL the run on integrity defects)
                          │               │
            ┌─────────────┼───────────────┘
            ▼             ▼               ▼
   11_silver_trips  12_silver_vehicle  06_silver_drivers   07_silver_payments
            │        _positions │              │                   │
            │             │     │              │                   │
            ├─────────────┴─────┼──────────────┤                   │
            ▼                   ▼              ▼                   │
   08_gold_zone_demand   10_gold_eta_features  09_gold_driver_daily│
            │                   │              │                   │
            └───────────────────┴──────────────┴───────────────────┘
                                ▼
                        13_maintenance  (OPTIMIZE + VACUUM)   [new task]

    Dependency edges that matter and are commonly missed: - 05_quality_gate → 11_silver_trips — silver must not be built from a batch that failed integrity checks. This is the edge teams argue about; the argument is the lesson. - 12_silver_vehicle_positions → 08_gold_zone_demand and → 10_gold_eta_features — both gold marts consume vehicle positions, not just trips. - 06_silver_drivers → 09_gold_driver_daily — the driver dimension must be current before the daily mart joins it. - The four ingests are mutually independent and should run in parallel; the wiki's linear order was never required. - 13_maintenance is a new task, not one of the twelve. Compaction and vacuum belong in the pipeline (CS-02's lesson), not in a runbook.

    8.2 Idempotency ruling per task (deliverable b)¶

    # Task Current Correct Why
    01 ingest_trips append append Bronze is append-only and immutable. Re-run grows history; downstream dedups. Correct as is.
    02 ingest_gps append append Same.
    03 ingest_drivers overwrite overwrite (or SCD-2 merge) A full daily dimension snapshot; overwrite is idempotent. Note the trade-off: overwrite loses driver history — acceptable only because Delta time travel retains it.
    04 ingest_payments append append Same as 01.
    05 quality_gate exit 0 always exit non-zero on integrity failures Not a write-mode issue; a gate issue. A gate that always exits 0 is a log line.
    06 silver_drivers overwrite overwrite Full rebuild of a 3,450-row dimension; idempotent and cheap.
    07 silver_payments INSERT INTO MERGE on payment_id Re-run currently doubles rows.
    08 gold_zone_demand INSERT INTO replaceWhere date = {{ ds }} Grain is city × date × hour; the whole date partition is deterministically rebuilt. This is the task that broke on 7 June and again in P4.
    09 gold_driver_daily INSERT INTO MERGE on (driver_id, date) Grain is driver × day; late trips update prior days, so merge beats replaceWhere here.
    10 gold_eta_features overwrite (whole table) replaceWhere date = {{ ds }} Whole-table overwrite is idempotent but rebuilds all history nightly — correct and wasteful. Partition-scoped replace is both.
    11 silver_trips MERGE on trip_id MERGE on trip_id, 3-day lookback Already correct — this is CS-03's pattern.
    12 silver_vehicle_positions MERGE on event_id MERGE on event_id Already correct.
    13 maintenance (does not exist) OPTIMIZE + VACUUM, idempotent by nature New task.

    Summary for the board: three tasks (07, 08, 09) are actively unsafe to re-run, and they are all at aggregate grain — which is exactly where a doubled row is least visible and most damaging.

    8.3 The reconciliation gate (how equivalence is proved)¶

    Between every phase, and before cutover:

    -- for each table, for the same business date, old pipeline vs new
SELECT 'gold.zone_hourly_demand' AS tbl, COUNT(*) AS rows,
       SUM(demand_count) AS total, MIN(hour_ts), MAX(hour_ts)
FROM   gold.zone_hourly_demand_NEW WHERE date = '2026-06-07'
UNION ALL
SELECT 'gold.zone_hourly_demand', COUNT(*), SUM(demand_count), MIN(hour_ts), MAX(hour_ts)
FROM   gold.zone_hourly_demand_OLD WHERE date = '2026-06-07';
-- assert: identical on every column. Any difference is investigated, never explained away.

    Run in parallel for five business days including a Friday — Friday's demand curve differs materially from Sunday–Thursday's, so a Friday in the parallel run is a genuine test rather than a repetition.

    9. What good looks like vs common wrong turns¶

    What good looks like Common wrong turn
    Builds the DAG from the card bodies (what each reads and writes) Arranges cards in numeric order, reproducing the 7 June bug on the wall
    Includes the 05_quality_gate → 11_silver_trips edge and defends it Treats the quality gate as a side-branch that reports rather than blocks
    Runs the four ingests in parallel Keeps the wiki's linear order, which was never a dependency

    10. Debrief (5 minutes)¶

    1. Read 12/12 OK from P3 aloud. Every task succeeded and the day was destroyed. A system with no dependency model has no concept of what a task was supposed to read, so it cannot distinguish success from disaster. Green is a statement about exit codes, not about data.
    2. P4 is the second lesson and the more expensive one. The attempt to fix it doubled the mart. Without idempotency, your recovery procedure is itself a risk, which is why nobody re-runs anything, which is why problems sit for two days. Idempotency does not merely enable recovery — it makes investigation free, and free investigation is what makes a team fast.
    3. The wiki page is the artefact to remember. A conditional, a struck-through correction encoding an undocumented incident, and a named person as the recovery procedure. Every mature platform has this page. A DAG is that page, made executable and unable to go stale.
    4. Do not blame the new hire. She followed the only ordering the system exposed. The alphabet was the most authoritative source of truth available to her, and it was wrong. When a system's correctness depends on a person not making an obvious mistake, the system is the defect.
    5. Hand into LAB-07. "You will build this DAG for Masar, trigger it once, inject a silver failure, watch gold get held back rather than build stale, then re-run the whole DAG and get a clean identical state."
    6. The course question. "What happens to Masar's AI system if the pipeline runs out of order?" gold.eta_features is built from yesterday's silver.trips, so feature_ts claims today while the values describe yesterday. The model serves confidently wrong predictions, offline evaluation looks fine because the same stale features are used for both, and — as on Day 5 — nothing in BI shows a thing.

    11. The one sentence participants should leave with¶

    An execution order that lives in a person's head is an invariant the computer cannot check, so a run can report 12/12 OK while producing a stale platform — declare the dependencies in code, assert freshness on every input, and make every task safe to re-run, because the point of idempotency is not elegance but that it makes investigation free.
