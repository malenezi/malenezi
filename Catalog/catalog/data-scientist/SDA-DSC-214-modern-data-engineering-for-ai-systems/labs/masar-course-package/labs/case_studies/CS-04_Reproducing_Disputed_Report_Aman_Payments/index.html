    CS-04 — Reproducing a Disputed Report at "Aman" Payments¶

    Field Value
    Case ID CS-04
    Module M4 — Delta Lake, ACID and the Transaction Log
    Day / Hour Day 3 · Hour 5 (after LAB-04's DESCRIBE HISTORY / VERSION AS OF task)
    Duration 50 minutes
    Format Investigation (forensic reconstruction) with a written regulator response
    Group size 3–4 per team; one team member nominated as "the person who signs the response"
    Deliverable (a) The exact query sequence that reconstructs the filed figure; (b) a one-page response to the regulator; (c) a filing-versioning policy that prevents recurrence, including the VACUUM retention number and its justification
    Materials Evidence pack §5 per team (6 pages); a printed DESCRIBE HISTORY output (P3) large enough to annotate
    Facilitator prep 15 min. Be able to explain the difference between VERSION AS OF and TIMESTAMP AS OF without notes, and the exact interaction between VACUUM retention and time travel — teams will find the edge case in §7 Q2

    1. Scenario¶

    "Aman" (أمان) is a Saudi payments processor. On 14 May 2026 it filed its monthly regulatory settlement report for April 2026, generated from gold.settlement_monthly in its Lakehouse.

    On 4 June 2026 — three weeks later — the regulator disputes one figure: total settled value for the city of Jeddah. The regulator's own reconciliation, built from the acquirer side, gives SAR 41,882,340. Aman filed SAR 41,714,905. The difference is SAR 167,435.

    Aman's engineers re-run the report today. It produces a third number: SAR 41,903,118.

    The table has been written to 61 times since the filing. Nobody took a snapshot. The person who ran the filing job has left the company.

    The regulator's letter asks one question: "State the value of the table from which the filed figure was derived, as it stood on 14 May 2026, and demonstrate that the filed figure follows from it."

    2. Business context¶

    Item Figure
    Disputed figure — Aman filed SAR 41,714,905
    Regulator's independent figure SAR 41,882,340
    Difference in dispute SAR 167,435
    Figure if the report is re-run today SAR 41,903,118
    Maximum administrative penalty for an unreproducible regulatory filing SAR 5,000,000
    Days to respond 10 business days
    Filings per year subject to the same exposure 12

    The SAR 167,435 discrepancy is small and is very likely explainable. The unreproducibility is the actual exposure. A processor that cannot state what it filed, or reproduce it, has a control failure — and a control failure is priced far above any single month's discrepancy. Teams that spend the session chasing the SAR 167,435 have misread the case; the regulator asked a question about evidence, not about arithmetic.

    3. Technical challenge¶

    Reconstruct the exact state of gold.settlement_monthly (and everything it derives from) as of 14 May 2026, recompute the filed figure from it, demonstrate the match, and explain the two subsequent deltas — without a backup, a snapshot, or the engineer who ran it.

    Complicating the reconstruction: a PDPL erasure request was executed on 22 May 2026 for seven rider identities, deleting 29 rows from silver. Those rows contributed to the April settlement. Current data must not contain them; the historical explanation must nonetheless account for them. Teams must resolve the apparent contradiction between "reproduce the past exactly" and "the past must no longer exist."

    4. Constraints¶

    • No separate snapshot exists. Do not let a team's answer be "we should have taken one" — that is the policy fix, not the response to the regulator.
    • VACUUM runs weekly, RETAIN 720 HOURS (30 days). 14 May to 4 June is 21 days. Time travel is available — barely. This margin is the case's central tension and the source of §7 Q2.
    • A PDPL erasure ran on 22 May and must be honoured in current data.
    • 10 business days to respond.
    • The response must be intelligible to a regulator's auditor, not only to an engineer.

    5. Evidence pack¶

    P1 — The regulator's letter (extract)¶

    "... the Authority's reconciliation of settled value for Jeddah for the period 2026-04-01 to 2026-04-30 yields SAR 41,882,340 against the SAR 41,714,905 reported in your filing of 14 May 2026. You are directed to state the value of the source table as at the date of filing and to demonstrate that the reported figure follows from it. A response that cannot evidence the state of the data at the time of filing will be treated as a deficiency in record-keeping controls irrespective of the arithmetic difference."

    Read the bolded sentence aloud twice. The regulator is not asking Aman to be right; it is asking Aman to be evidenced.

    P2 — The filing job's log line (the only surviving artefact of the filing run)¶

    2026-05-14 03:11:47 UTC  INFO  settlement_report v1.4.2
2026-05-14 03:11:47 UTC  INFO  reading gold.settlement_monthly period=2026-04
2026-05-14 03:12:09 UTC  INFO  rows=3,412  cities=5
2026-05-14 03:12:09 UTC  INFO  wrote /reports/settlement_2026-04_filed.pdf  sha256=8f3c...a91d

    Note what is absent: no table version, no commit timestamp, no input checksum. The job recorded what it produced and nothing about what it read. This absence is the control failure, and it is the thing the policy deliverable must fix.

    P3 — DESCRIBE HISTORY gold.settlement_monthly (abridged, 61 commits; the decisive rows)¶

    version timestamp (UTC) operation operationParameters operationMetrics
    118 2026-06-04 02:00:11 MERGE predicate: period='2026-04' numTargetRowsUpdated: 41
    117 2026-06-02 02:00:09 OPTIMIZE zOrderBy: [city, period] numFilesAdded: 6, numFilesRemoved: 214
    ... ... ... ... ...
    96 2026-05-22 14:33:52 DELETE predicate: rider_id IN (...) numDeletedRows: 29
    95 2026-05-21 02:00:14 MERGE predicate: period='2026-04' numTargetRowsUpdated: 188
    ... ... ... ... ...
    88 2026-05-15 02:00:07 MERGE predicate: period='2026-04' numTargetRowsUpdated: 121
    87 2026-05-14 02:07:33 MERGE predicate: period='2026-04' numTargetRowsUpdated: 64
    86 2026-05-13 02:00:11 MERGE predicate: period='2026-04' numTargetRowsUpdated: 203

    Version 87, committed 02:07:33 on 14 May, is the last commit before the filing job read the table at 03:11:47. That is the answer to the regulator's first question, and it is derivable from the log alone with no snapshot, no backup, and no surviving engineer. Let teams find it; do not point at it.

    P4 — VACUUM history on the same table¶

    2026-06-01 04:00:00  VACUUM gold.settlement_monthly RETAIN 720 HOURS
                     -> deleted 1,144 files no longer referenced and older than 2026-05-02 04:00:00
2026-05-25 04:00:00  VACUUM gold.settlement_monthly RETAIN 720 HOURS
2026-05-18 04:00:00  VACUUM gold.settlement_monthly RETAIN 720 HOURS

    Today is 2026-06-04. The most recent VACUUM removed files unreferenced since 2026-05-02. Version 87 (14 May) is still readable — with 12 days of margin. The next VACUUM, on 2026-06-08, will move the horizon to 2026-05-09 and the margin becomes 5 days. If the dispute had arrived five weeks later instead of three, the answer would have been "we cannot."

    Ask the room, at exactly this moment: what should you do before lunch? (Answer: pin version 87 — see §8.1 step 0.)

    P5 — The PDPL erasure record¶

    data/fixtures/erasure_request/pdpl_erasure_2026-06-10.json is the course's equivalent fixture; Aman's is the same shape.

    {"request_id":"PDPL-2026-0417","received":"2026-05-20","executed":"2026-05-22T14:33:52Z",
 "legal_basis":"PDPL Art. 18 - right to erasure","subjects":7,
 "rows_deleted":{"silver.payments":29,"gold.settlement_monthly":29},
 "delta_versions":{"silver.payments":73,"gold.settlement_monthly":96},
 "value_removed_sar":{"JED":0.00,"note":"erased rows were rider-identifying; monetary aggregates were preserved by design"}}

    The design decision recorded in that last field is the resolution of the whole PDPL/audit tension, and teams should be made to find it: Aman's gold settlement table stores monetary aggregates keyed by city and period, with rider identity carried only for lineage. Erasure removed the identifying rows without changing the settled totals. Whether the class believes that is an acceptable design is §7 Q3.

    P6 — The three numbers, and where they come from¶

    Number Source
    SAR 41,714,905 Filed 14 May. Derived from table version 87.
    SAR 41,882,340 The regulator's figure, built from acquirer-side records that include late settlements through end of May.
    SAR 41,903,118 Re-run today from version 118, which includes 61 subsequent commits of late-settlement MERGEs.

    The pattern is now visible: 41,714,905 < 41,882,340 < 41,903,118. All three are increasing. This is not a discrepancy; it is a period that was still absorbing late settlements when it was filed, seen at three different moments. This is CS-03's late-arrival problem, wearing a regulator's letterhead — say so explicitly in the debrief.

    6. Facilitation script (minute-by-minute)¶

    Min Activity Facilitator does Watch for
    0–4 Frame. Read P1's bolded sentence twice. State the three numbers. Ask: "Which one is right?" then immediately: "Wrong question. What did the regulator actually ask for?" Write on the board: "The regulator asked for evidence, not for arithmetic." Teams that start reconciling SAR 167,435. That is the trap and roughly half the room falls into it.
    4–6 Distribute the pack; state the three deliverables and nominate the signer at each table. The "who signs" device raises the seriousness noticeably. Use it. —
    6–16 Round 1 — Reconstruct. Find the version, prove it, produce the query sequence. Circulate. Prompt any stuck team with: "P2 tells you when the job read the table. P3 tells you when every commit happened. That is enough." Teams using TIMESTAMP AS OF '2026-05-14' — a plausible answer that resolves to midnight and lands on version 86, the wrong version. This error is gold; save it for the debrief.
    16–20 Board it. One team walks through version 87 and the timestamp argument. Draw the timeline: v86 (13 May 02:00) → v87 (14 May 02:07) → filing job reads (14 May 03:11) → v88 (15 May 02:00). Confirm the class understands why VERSION AS OF 87 is defensible and a date-only TIMESTAMP AS OF is not.
    20–24 Interrupt with P4. Read the VACUUM history aloud. Ask: "How much margin do you have, and what do you do before lunch?" This is the highest-energy moment in the case. Let the realisation land. The answer is: pin version 87 now — CLONE it or extend retention — before the 8 June VACUUM.
    24–36 Round 2 — Respond and prevent. Write the regulator response and the versioning policy. Push on PDPL: "You just told the regulator you can reproduce May. You also told a rider their data is erased. Both are true. Explain how." Teams that promise the regulator a full historical dataset including erased personal data. That is a PDPL breach committed in a compliance letter.
    36–45 Read-aloud. Two nominated signers read their response verbatim. The room plays the regulator's auditor. Challenge with: "You say version 87. How do I know you did not choose the version that flatters you?" (Answer: the log timestamp bounds it independently of the figure; the choice was made by the clock, not by the result.) Responses that read as engineering documentation. Rewrite one live, in plain language.
    45–50 Debrief. §10. Close on §11. Reveal P6's monotonic pattern last — the "discrepancy" was a late-arrival profile all along. —

    7. Discussion questions with model answers¶

    Q1. Why is time travel superior to manual snapshot backups for audit?

    Model answer. Five reasons, in ascending order of importance:

    1. It is not optional. A snapshot exists only if someone remembered. The transaction log is written by every commit, unconditionally, as a byproduct of writing at all. Aman's engineers took no snapshot and still have the evidence.
    2. It is complete. A snapshot captures the state someone thought to capture. The log captures every state, plus who/what/when for each transition — DESCRIBE HISTORY gives operation, predicate, and metrics per commit. That transforms "here is a copy" into "here is the causal history."
    3. It is cheap. Delta versions share unmodified files; a version costs the delta, not the table. Aman has 118 recoverable versions of a multi-terabyte table for a fraction of one full copy. Snapshot economics force a retention trade-off that log economics largely dissolve.
    4. It is queryable in place. SELECT ... VERSION AS OF 87 runs against the production table with the production code path. A restored backup runs against a restored copy, which is a different object in a different location, and every difference is a fresh source of doubt.
    5. It resists the credibility attack. The auditor's real question is "did you pick the version that flatters you?" With time travel, the version is selected by an independently recorded clock — the job log at 03:11:47 against the commit log at 02:07:33 — before anyone knows what figure it produces. With a manual backup, the selection is a human decision made by an interested party.

    State the limitation honestly too: time travel is bounded by retention, and retention is a policy choice that can be set to zero. Which is Q2.

    Q2. What VACUUM retention would have made this impossible, and why?

    Model answer. Anything shorter than the elapsed time from filing to dispute — here 21 days. Concretely:

    Retention Outcome for this dispute
    RETAIN 168 HOURS (7 d, the common default) Version 87's files deleted on ~21 May. Reconstruction impossible. Deficiency finding.
    RETAIN 720 HOURS (30 d, Aman's setting) Readable on 4 June with 12 days' margin. Readable, barely.
    RETAIN 0 HOURS Time travel destroyed immediately; also breaks concurrent readers mid-query.

    The general rule participants should write down: VACUUM retention is a governance decision, not a storage optimisation. The floor is set by the longest interval over which someone may credibly ask "what did it say then" — for regulatory filings that is the dispute window in the regulation, not an engineer's estimate of convenience.

    And the deeper point: retention alone is the wrong instrument for this. Retention is a window that slides; a filing is a fixed point. Relying on a sliding window to protect a fixed point means the protection expires at a date nobody has diarised. The correct control is to pin the filing version — CREATE TABLE ... CLONE gold.settlement_monthly VERSION AS OF 87 (a shallow clone costs almost nothing and freezes the file references) or a retained tag — so that reproducibility is independent of retention entirely. Aman's 12-day margin is not a system that works; it is a system that has not failed yet.

    Push the counter-argument for balance: PDPL and storage cost both push retention down. The resolution is not a single retention number but differentiated retention — short on raw personal data, long on financial aggregates, and pinned on filed artefacts.

    Q3. How do you reconcile "reproduce the past" with "honour a PDPL erasure"?

    Model answer. They conflict only if you assume reproducing the past requires re-materialising personal data. It does not. Three moves resolve it:

    1. Separate the identifying columns from the reportable aggregate. P5 shows Aman did this by design: the settlement figures are aggregates by city and period; rider identity was present for lineage only. Erasure removed the identity rows and did not change any monetary total. The filed figure is therefore fully reproducible without reproducing any erased personal data. This is the architectural answer and it must be made before the erasure request arrives.
    2. Where an aggregate genuinely depends on erased rows, reproduce the aggregate, not the rows, and disclose the erasure as an annotation on the historical view: "version 87 contained 29 rows relating to 7 data subjects subsequently erased under PDPL Art. 18 on 2026-05-22 (request PDPL-2026-0417); their contribution to the reported total was SAR 0.00 / SAR X." The regulator gets the number and the explanation; the data subject gets the erasure. A documented, quantified gap is an acceptable audit artefact; an undocumented one is not.
    3. Never resolve it by refusing the erasure. PDPL erasure is a legal obligation, not an option to be traded against audit convenience. Where a genuine legal-retention obligation conflicts (financial records under a 7-year retention), the correct response is a documented lawful-basis exemption applied to the specific columns required, not a blanket refusal, and it is a legal determination — engineering's job is to make both outcomes technically possible and evidenced.

    The one-line formulation: erasure removes the person, not the accounting. Design your gold layer so that this is true by construction, and the conflict never arises.

    Q4. What operational habit prevents future disputes?

    Model answer. Version-pinning every filing, automatically, in the filing job itself. Compare P2's log line with what it should have been:

    2026-05-14 03:11:47 UTC  INFO  settlement_report v1.4.2
2026-05-14 03:11:47 UTC  INFO  reading gold.settlement_monthly  DELTA_VERSION=87  commit_ts=2026-05-14T02:07:33Z
2026-05-14 03:11:47 UTC  INFO  input_checksum=e7b1...44ac  rows=3,412
2026-05-14 03:12:09 UTC  INFO  pinned  gold.settlement_monthly_filed_2026_04  (SHALLOW CLONE VERSION AS OF 87)
2026-05-14 03:12:09 UTC  INFO  wrote /reports/settlement_2026-04_filed.pdf  sha256=8f3c...a91d

    Four additions, each cheap: record the version read, record the input checksum, pin a shallow clone, and store all of it with the artefact. The whole 10-day fire drill collapses into one query against a table that already exists.

    Then generalise: the filing job should not merely read a version, it should declare one. spark.read.option("versionAsOf", pinned).table(...) makes the report a function of an explicit input, so re-running the filing job a year later reproduces the filed PDF byte-for-byte. A report that is a function of "whatever the table says now" is not a report; it is an observation.

    Add the governance wrapper: a filing register — one row per filing, holding period, table, version, checksum, clone name, report hash, and the name of the person who signed. That register is what turns a 10-day forensic exercise into a 10-minute lookup, and it is the only part of the response the regulator will actually keep.

    Q5 (reserve). The three numbers are all different. Which one should Aman now tell the regulator is correct?

    Model answer. None of them, phrased that way — the question is malformed and saying so is the right answer. April's settled value is not a scalar; it is a quantity that converges as late settlements arrive. Aman should state: the figure filed on 14 May was correct as of version 87 (evidenced); the regulator's figure reflects acquirer records through end of May; today's figure reflects 61 further commits; the three differ by late settlements, which are enumerable trip-by-trip from the commit history. Then propose the actual fix: agree with the regulator a cut-off convention — either file at T+45 when the period has demonstrably closed, or file at T+14 and submit a defined restatement at T+45. The dispute exists because two parties measured a converging quantity at two different times and both called it "April".

    This is CS-03 with a regulator attached. Say that out loud.

    8. Solution approach¶

    8.1 The reconstruction (deliverable a)¶

    Step 0 — before anything else, protect the evidence. The 8 June VACUUM will cut the margin to 5 days.

    CREATE TABLE gold.settlement_monthly_filed_2026_04
SHALLOW CLONE gold.settlement_monthly VERSION AS OF 87;

    Step 1 — establish the version by the clock, not by the figure. Filing job read the table at 2026-05-14 03:11:47 UTC (P2). The latest commit at or before that instant is version 87, committed 02:07:33 (P3). Version 88 lands 2026-05-15 02:00:07, after the read. The version is bounded by two independent logs.

    DESCRIBE HISTORY gold.settlement_monthly;   -- the evidence, unaltered

    Step 2 — recompute the filed figure from that version.

    SELECT SUM(settled_value_sar) AS filed_total
FROM   gold.settlement_monthly VERSION AS OF 87
WHERE  period = '2026-04' AND city = 'JED';
-- 41,714,905.00   == the filed figure. Reproduced.

    Step 3 — explain each subsequent delta, quantified.

    SELECT 87 AS version, SUM(settled_value_sar) FROM gold.settlement_monthly VERSION AS OF 87
  WHERE period='2026-04' AND city='JED'
UNION ALL SELECT 96,  SUM(settled_value_sar) FROM gold.settlement_monthly VERSION AS OF 96
  WHERE period='2026-04' AND city='JED'   -- immediately after the PDPL DELETE
UNION ALL SELECT 118, SUM(settled_value_sar) FROM gold.settlement_monthly VERSION AS OF 118
  WHERE period='2026-04' AND city='JED';  -- today

    Then attribute the movement to specific commits:

    -- every commit that touched the April period after the filing, with its row impact
SELECT version, timestamp, operation,
       operationMetrics['numTargetRowsUpdated'] AS updated,
       operationMetrics['numTargetRowsInserted'] AS inserted,
       operationMetrics['numDeletedRows']        AS deleted
FROM   (DESCRIBE HISTORY gold.settlement_monthly)
WHERE  version > 87
  AND  operationParameters['predicate'] LIKE '%2026-04%'
ORDER  BY version;

    Step 4 — annotate the PDPL erasure. Version 96 is the DELETE. Per P5, value_removed_sar.JED = 0.00: the erasure removed identifying rows and did not move the settled total. State this in the response with the request ID and legal basis.

    Step 5 — reconcile to the regulator's figure. The gap SAR 41,714,905 → 41,882,340 is late settlements landing between 14 May and end of May, enumerable from the commits in step 3.

    8.2 The regulator response (deliverable b) — reference text¶

    RE: Settlement filing 2026-04, Jeddah — response to query of 4 June 2026

1. The filing of 14 May 2026 was derived from gold.settlement_monthly at
   Delta table version 87, committed 2026-05-14 02:07:33 UTC. The filing job
   read the table at 03:11:47 UTC the same day; version 88 was not committed
   until 2026-05-15 02:00:07 UTC. The version is therefore bounded by two
   independently recorded logs and was not selected after the fact.

2. Recomputing the reported measure against version 87 reproduces the filed
   figure exactly: SAR 41,714,905.00. The query and the immutable commit
   history are attached (Appendix A) and the version has been pinned as
   gold.settlement_monthly_filed_2026_04.

3. The difference of SAR 167,435 against the Authority's figure is accounted
   for in full by late settlements recorded between 15 and 31 May 2026,
   itemised by commit in Appendix B (31 commits, 1,204 settlements).

4. On 2026-05-22 Aman executed erasure request PDPL-2026-0417 under PDPL
   Art. 18 for seven data subjects (29 rows, table version 96). The erased
   records were rider-identifying; they carried no settled value for Jeddah
   in the period and the reported total is unaffected. Personal data has not
   been re-materialised to answer this query, and will not be.

5. Control improvement, effective for the 2026-05 filing: every filing now
   records the source table version and input checksum, pins that version as
   an immutable clone, and is entered in a filing register. Reproduction of
   any filing is a single query and does not depend on retention windows.

    8.3 The policy (deliverable c)¶

    Control Setting Justification
    Filing job records source DELTA_VERSION + input checksum Mandatory, job fails without it P2's absence is the root control failure
    Every filing pinned via SHALLOW CLONE VERSION AS OF Mandatory, at filing time Makes reproducibility independent of retention
    Filing register table period · table · version · checksum · clone · report hash · signer Turns forensics into a lookup
    VACUUM retention, financial gold tables RETAIN 2160 HOURS (90 d) ≥ the regulatory dispute window with margin; pinned clones cover beyond it
    VACUUM retention, raw personal data Per DATA_DICTIONARY (24 months rider, 90 d geohash p7) PDPL minimisation; not aligned to the financial number
    Erasure log with per-table version and value impact Mandatory Lets audit and PDPL coexist, evidenced
    Filing cut-off convention agreed with the regulator T+14 file, T+45 restate Removes the converging-quantity dispute at source

    9. What good looks like vs common wrong turns¶

    What good looks like Common wrong turn
    Answers the question asked — evidences the version — before touching the arithmetic Spends the session reconciling SAR 167,435 and never states a version
    Uses VERSION AS OF 87, justified by the job log timestamp against the commit log Uses TIMESTAMP AS OF '2026-05-14', which resolves to midnight and returns version 86 — a wrong number, confidently produced
    Pins the version immediately, before the next VACUUM Notes the 12-day margin and does nothing about it
    Treats VACUUM retention as governance with a stated floor Treats retention as a storage setting, or proposes RETAIN 0 to save cost
    Separates erasure of identity from preservation of aggregates Promises the regulator the full historical dataset, re-materialising erased personal data in a compliance letter
    Recognises the three numbers as one converging quantity measured at three times Declares one of the three "the true number"
    Fixes the job so the next filing is self-evidencing Fixes only this filing, manually
    Writes a response a non-engineer can act on Sends the auditor a notebook
    Names the control failure plainly (the job logged its output and not its input) Blames the departed engineer

    10. Debrief (5 minutes)¶

    1. The evidence existed the whole time. Nobody took a snapshot, the engineer left, 61 commits landed on top — and the answer was still recoverable, precisely because the transaction log is written unconditionally by every write. Auditability you have to remember to switch on is auditability you will not have.
    2. The TIMESTAMP AS OF error. If any team used a date-only timestamp, put version 86 and version 87 side by side and show the different total. A plausible query, a confident answer, a wrong number, delivered to a regulator. Time travel gives you the power to be precisely wrong about the past; the discipline is to bound the version by an independent clock.
    3. Retention is governance. At RETAIN 168 HOURS — a very common default — this case ends with a deficiency finding and up to SAR 5,000,000 of exposure. The margin Aman had was 12 days and shrinking on a schedule nobody had diarised. A control that expires silently is not a control.
    4. PDPL and audit are not opponents if gold is designed so that erasure removes the person and not the accounting. That design decision is made months before the erasure request arrives, or not at all.
    5. Reveal P6's pattern. All three numbers increase. This was never a discrepancy — it was a period still absorbing late settlements, observed at three moments and called "April" three times. It is CS-03's undercount with a regulator's letterhead. The fix is a cut-off convention, i.e. an agreement about when a period is closed, which is the same conversation as the lookback window in M3 and the watermark in M5.
    6. The course question. "What happens to Masar's AI system if we cannot reproduce the state of a table on a past date?" You cannot reproduce a training run; you cannot explain a model's behaviour; you cannot answer "what did the model see" after an incident. On Day 5, CS-11 will require exactly this capability to roll a corrupted feature back to its last trusted version — reproducibility is not only an audit control, it is the remediation mechanism.

    11. The one sentence participants should leave with¶

    A regulator does not ask whether your number was right; it asks you to evidence what your data said at the moment you filed — so record the table version with every filing, pin it, and treat VACUUM retention as a governance floor set by your dispute window rather than a storage setting set by convenience.
