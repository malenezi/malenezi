    CS-08 — The ETA Model That Won Offline and Lost Live at "Wojhah"¶

    Field Value
    Case ID CS-08
    Module M8 — Serving Gold to AI and BI
    Day / Hour Day 5 · Hour 4 (after LAB-08's leakage drill; immediately before CS-11)
    Duration 45 minutes
    Format Investigation → design exercise
    Group size 3–4 per team
    Deliverable (a) A per-feature verdict on the eight features in P3 (valid / leaking / unavailable online) with the reason; (b) the corrected definition of the leaking feature; (c) the CI check that would block the whole class of bug; (d) three sentences to the stakeholder who was told R²=0.93 and must now be told 0.78
    Materials Evidence pack §5 per team (6 pages); ACT-09 card deck available as an extension for fast teams
    Facilitator prep 15 min. Rehearse the "why does leakage inflate the offline metric" explanation with a concrete row — the mechanism must be shown, not asserted

    1. Scenario¶

    "Wojhah" (وجهة) built a new ETA model for its ride-hailing app. In offline evaluation on a held-out month it scored R² = 0.93, against the incumbent rule-based heuristic's 0.61. The review was celebratory. The model shipped in a 20% A/B test.

    After three weeks live, the A/B readout is in. The new model's mean absolute ETA error is 4.9 minutes. The heuristic's is 4.8 minutes. The model is, within noise, no better than the thing it replaced — and it cost a quarter of a data-science team's capacity.

    The product director has asked, in a written message that has been forwarded widely: "Why should I believe any offline number your team ever produces again?"

    2. Business context¶

    Item Figure
    Offline R² claimed at review 0.93
    Offline R² after the leak is removed 0.78
    Incumbent heuristic R² 0.61
    Live MAE, new model 4.9 min
    Live MAE, heuristic 4.8 min
    Data-science capacity spent 1 quarter, 3 people
    Rider cancellations attributable to ETA error > 5 min 8.4% of quoted trips
    Modelled annual value of a 1-minute MAE improvement SAR 12,400,000
    Value actually delivered SAR 0
    Online feature-lookup latency budget p99 ≤ 50 ms

    The honest model at R² = 0.78 is genuinely better than the heuristic's 0.61 and would deliver real value. The tragedy is not that the model was bad; it is that the inflated number destroyed the credibility needed to ship the good one. Make sure the room reaches this — it changes what "the fix" means.

    3. Technical challenge¶

    Diagnose why offline and live diverge and fix it structurally, so the entire class of bug cannot recur — not with a patch to one feature.

    Two distinct defects are present and teams must separate them:

    1. Temporal leakage — at least one feature is computed with information that would not exist at prediction time.
    2. Offline/online skew — the online serving path re-implements feature logic independently, so the value the model sees in production differs from the value it was trained on, even for non-leaking features.

    Both produce "great offline, mediocre live," and the remedies are different. A team that finds only the first will fix the metric and still ship a model that behaves differently in production.

    4. Constraints¶

    • Cannot retrain endlessly — one retrain cycle is budgeted.
    • The fix must generalise: prevent the class, not the instance. A code review comment is not a fix.
    • The online path must stay under p99 ≤ 50 ms.
    • PDPL applies: no raw rider_id may reach the online store or BI (CONVENTIONS.md).
    • The corrected metric must be published downward, honestly, to the same audience that heard 0.93.

    5. Evidence pack¶

    P1 — The offline evaluation, as presented at review¶

    model: eta_gbm_v3     trained 2026-05-02   eval window 2026-05-03 .. 2026-06-01
                      split: random 80/20 on rows
R2 ......... 0.9312
MAE ........ 1.41 min
p90 abs err. 2.9 min
baseline (heuristic): R2 0.6104, MAE 4.77 min

    Two defects are visible in the header alone before any feature is examined. split: random 80/20 on rows — a random split on a temporal problem puts trips from 30 May in the training set and trips from 3 May in the test set, so the model is evaluated partly on its own future. And eval window overlaps the training window entirely. Ask the room to find these before handing out P3; roughly a third of teams will.

    P2 — The live A/B readout¶

    experiment: eta_gbm_v3 @ 20%    2026-06-08 .. 2026-06-28    n = 411,882 quoted trips
                       control (heuristic)   treatment (eta_gbm_v3)
MAE (min)              4.81                  4.93
p90 abs err (min)      9.2                   9.4
rider cancel rate      8.3%                  8.6%

    Offline MAE 1.41 min; live MAE 4.93 min. A 3.5× degradation between the evaluation and the world. That ratio is the signature of leakage — not of overfitting, which degrades gently and symmetrically.

    P3 — The feature table (gold.eta_features) as built for training¶

    # Column Definition as implemented Team verdict
    1 pickup_zone_id Zone of pickup, known at request
    2 dropoff_zone_id Requested destination, known at request
    3 hour_of_day hour(feature_ts) in Asia/Riyadh
    4 day_of_week dayofweek(feature_ts)
    5 rolling_zone_demand_15m COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY pickup_zone_id ORDER BY feature_ts RANGE BETWEEN INTERVAL 15 MINUTES PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)
    6 rolling_avg_speed_10m AVG(payload.speed_kmh) over the last 10 min in the pickup zone, from silver.vehicle_positions
    7 driver_recent_trip_count COUNT(*) of that driver's trips in the current hour
    8 historical_route_duration Median duration_min for (pickup_zone, dropoff_zone, hour_of_day) over the preceding 28 days
    — weather_condition Weather at feature_ts, joined from weather_hourly
    — distance_km Metered distance
    — label_actual_duration_min The label

    Hand this out with the verdict column blank. It is the case's core exercise.

    P4 — The single query that exposes the leak¶

    -- for one trip, what does each feature "see" relative to prediction time?
SELECT trip_id,
       feature_ts,                             -- when the prediction is made
       pickup_ts,
       dropoff_ts,                             -- when the trip ENDED
       driver_recent_trip_count,
       rolling_zone_demand_15m
FROM   gold.eta_features
WHERE  trip_id = 'TRP-20260605-0004417';

trip_id                | feature_ts          | pickup_ts           | dropoff_ts          | driver_recent_trip_count | rolling_zone_demand_15m
TRP-20260605-0004417   | 2026-06-05 08:12:00 | 2026-06-05 08:12:00 | 2026-06-05 08:41:33 | 6                        | 84

    The prediction is made at 08:12. driver_recent_trip_count = 6 counts the driver's trips in the hour 08:00–09:00, which includes trips that started at 08:31 and 08:52 — after the prediction. A driver who goes on to complete many trips in an hour is a driver working a fast, dense zone, and the model has learned to read the future from the count. The feature is a partial answer to the question being asked.

    Make participants notice the second-order subtlety: rolling_zone_demand_15m uses CURRENT ROW, which is arguably correct (the current row is the prediction moment) but silently includes any row with an identical feature_ts — and, more importantly, its online implementation may not respect the same boundary. This is one of the two genuinely arguable features and it deserves discussion, not a verdict.

    P5 — The online serving path¶

    # services/eta_online/features.py   (written by the platform team, February 2026)
def get_features(trip_request):
    z = trip_request.pickup_zone_id
    return {
      "rolling_zone_demand_15m": redis.get(f"masar:features:zone:{z}:demand_15m"),
      "rolling_avg_speed_10m":   redis.get(f"masar:features:zone:{z}:speed_10m"),
      "driver_recent_trip_count": pg.query(                       # <-- different source
          "SELECT count(*) FROM trips WHERE driver_id=%s AND started_at > now() - interval '1 hour'",
          trip_request.driver_id),
      "historical_route_duration": pg.query(                      # <-- different logic
          "SELECT percentile_cont(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY duration_min) FROM trips "
          "WHERE pickup_zone_id=%s AND dropoff_zone_id=%s AND started_at > now() - interval '30 days'", z, d),
      "weather_condition": weather_api.current(z),                # <-- third source
    }

    Three different sources for five features, none of them the table the model was trained from. Note the specific mismatches: - driver_recent_trip_count online is a trailing hour (correct!); offline it was the calendar hour (leaking). The online value is systematically lower than the training value — the model receives a feature whose distribution it has never seen. - historical_route_duration online uses 30 days; offline 28 days. Small, but a silent, permanent, unmonitored difference. - weather_condition online comes from a live API; offline from weather_hourly. Different vocabularies are possible ("Clear" vs clear).

    P6 — Offline/online consistency check, run after the fact¶

    keys compared: 10,000 recent trips
feature                      | match | mismatch | mean abs delta
rolling_zone_demand_15m      | 9,942 |       58 | 0.4
rolling_avg_speed_10m        | 9,988 |       12 | 0.1
driver_recent_trip_count     | 1,204 |    8,796 | 2.7      <-- 88% mismatch
historical_route_duration    | 6,610 |    3,390 | 1.9
weather_condition            | 9,301 |      699 | n/a

    88% of production requests received a driver_recent_trip_count the model had never been trained on. This check took twenty minutes to write and did not exist.

    6. Facilitation script (minute-by-minute)¶

    Min Activity Facilitator does Watch for
    0–3 Frame. Project P1 and P2 side by side: offline MAE 1.41, live MAE 4.93. Read the product director's message aloud. Write "offline 1.41 → live 4.93" on the board. Teams that say "overfitting." Ask: does overfitting produce a 3.5× gap? (No — it degrades gently. This gap has a mechanism.)
    3–6 P1 only. "Two defects are visible in this header before you see a single feature. Find them." 90 seconds, no discussion. Random split on temporal data; train/eval window overlap.
    6–8 Distribute P3–P6. State the four deliverables. — —
    8–20 Round 1 — Per-feature verdict. Every one of the eight rows gets valid / leaking / unavailable-online, plus a reason. Circulate with the single question: "Could this feature have been known at 08:12?" Say nothing else. Teams that mark features valid because "it's in the historical table." The table is not the question; the timestamp is.
    20–25 Board it. Build the verdict table publicly. Argue features 5 and 8 properly — they are genuinely arguable. Do not resolve 5 and 8 quickly. Ambiguity here is the real skill. Teams that want a rule that decides every case mechanically. There isn't one; there is a question.
    25–30 Reveal P6. The 88% mismatch. Ask: "You fixed the leak. Would the live number improve?" (No — the online path still sends a different feature.) This is the moment the second defect lands. Let it land hard.
    30–38 Round 2 — Structural fix + CI check + the three sentences. Push on CI: "Your check must fail a pull request. Write the assertion." Fixes phrased as process ("we'll review features more carefully"). Reject firmly: a process is not a check.
    38–43 Read the three sentences aloud. Two teams. The room plays the product director. Challenge the weakest one live and rewrite it together. Sentences that hide the correction in passive voice.
    43–45 Debrief. §10. Close on §11 and hand into CS-11. The bridge line: "Every feature you just certified as valid assumed the data was correct. Next case: what if it isn't?" —

    7. Discussion questions with model answers¶

    Q1. Why did the leaking feature score so well offline?

    Model answer. Because driver_recent_trip_count computed over the calendar hour contains a compressed answer to the question being asked.

    The mechanism, concretely, using P4's row: the prediction is made at 08:12 and the feature counts the driver's trips in 08:00–09:00 — six of them, several starting after 08:12. What makes a driver complete six trips in one hour? Short trips, in a dense zone, with fast traffic. Exactly the conditions that make this trip short. The feature therefore correlates strongly with the label, not because it predicts duration but because it is partially computed from the same future that produced the label.

    The model, quite correctly, weights it heavily. Offline it is rewarded for doing so, because the evaluation rows also carry future-contaminated features. Live, at 08:12, that future does not exist: the online path can only count trips up to 08:12, which for this driver is two, not six. The model receives a value from a distribution it never saw, applies a large learned weight to it, and produces a confidently wrong number.

    Two amplifiers made it worse: - The random 80/20 split put 30 May trips in training and 3 May trips in test. With a temporal problem, the model gets to see the future in the most literal sense. A temporal split (train ≤ date T, test > T) is mandatory, and it alone would have dropped the reported R² substantially. - Train/eval window overlap (P1: trained 2026-05-02, eval 2026-05-03..06-01) means the evaluation is not held out in time at all.

    The general rule to write down: a feature that is suspiciously predictive is suspicious. A jump from 0.61 to 0.93 on a problem where the dominant term is traffic — which nobody can observe in advance — should have triggered an investigation at review, not applause. Treat an unexpectedly good offline metric as a bug report until proven otherwise.

    Q2. How does serving the same feature table offline and online prevent recurrence?

    Model answer. Because it removes the possibility of divergence rather than the occurrence of it.

    Today there are two implementations of every feature (P5): one in SQL for training, one in Python/SQL for serving, written by different teams at different times against different sources. Two implementations of one definition is CS-01's two-tier problem, arriving in the feature layer. They will diverge, and P6 shows they already have — 88% on one feature, 34% on another, silently, for months.

    The fix is materialise, do not re-implement:

    gold.eta_features  (computed ONCE, in the batch pipeline, point-in-time correct)
        ├──► training set        (read directly)
        └──► publish job ──► Redis  masar:features:zone:{zone_id}   (a COPY of the same values)

    The online store becomes a cache of computed values, not a second computation. Serving reads a value; it never derives one. Consequences:

    • Correctness by construction — there is only one definition, so there is nothing to keep in sync.
    • The p99 ≤ 50 ms budget becomes easy — a Redis GET is ~1–3 ms, versus P5's live Postgres percentile query, which is both slow and a different answer.
    • Feature changes propagate atomically — change the definition, the next publish updates both consumers.
    • PDPL is enforceable at one boundary — the publish job is the single place where rider_id can be dropped and zone-level aggregates emitted, satisfying "never emit raw rider_id into BI or the online store."

    The residual risk is staleness, not skew: the online copy is as fresh as the last publish. That is a monitorable, bounded, honest failure mode — publish lag — and it replaces an unmonitorable one. Make teams state the publish cadence and the staleness SLA explicitly.

    Q3. What automated check catches leakage before a model ships?

    Model answer. Three checks, all runnable in CI, in increasing order of power:

    (1) A point-in-time assertion on the feature table. Every feature row must be derivable from data whose timestamp is <= feature_ts. Implemented as a leakage lint over the feature SQL: any window function whose frame extends to CURRENT ROW or FOLLOWING, any join to a table on a key without a <= feature_ts predicate, any aggregate over a calendar bucket containing feature_ts, fails the build.

    # ci/leakage_lint.py — fails the PR
FORBIDDEN = [
  r"RANGE BETWEEN .* AND CURRENT ROW",       # includes the prediction instant
  r"(UNBOUNDED|\d+\s+\w+)\s+FOLLOWING",       # explicit future
  r"date_trunc\('hour',\s*feature_ts\)",      # calendar bucket containing the prediction
]
# every feature must instead be bounded: ... PRECEDING AND INTERVAL '1' SECOND PRECEDING

    (2) A label-shuffle / future-ablation test. Recompute each feature using only data strictly before feature_ts and compare the value to what the pipeline produced. Any feature whose value changes is reading the future. This is stronger than the lint because it tests behaviour rather than syntax, and it catches leakage that arrives through a join three models deep.

    (3) The offline/online consistency check (P6), run continuously in production. For a sample of served requests, log the feature vector actually used and compare it to the offline table for the same key and timestamp. Alert on any mismatch rate above a threshold. This is the check that would have caught the second defect, which the leakage lint cannot see at all.

    State the principle: (1) and (2) protect against leakage; (3) protects against skew. They are different bugs with the same symptom, and you need all three. Add the process check that costs nothing: a temporal train/test split enforced in the training harness, not chosen per project.

    Q4. How do you rebuild stakeholder trust after an inflated metric is corrected downward?

    Model answer. The correction is the easy part; the credibility is the deliverable. Four moves:

    1. Say the number first, plainly, in the active voice. "Our reported R² of 0.93 was wrong. The correct figure is 0.78. We found the cause." Not "the metric has been revised" and not a paragraph of context before the number. Burying the correction is what makes the next number unbelievable.
    2. Explain the mechanism in one sentence a non-specialist can repeat. "One input counted trips the driver had not yet taken, so the model was partly reading the answer." A stakeholder who can re-explain your bug to their own boss is a stakeholder who has forgiven it.
    3. Publish what changes so it cannot recur, and make it checkable. "Every feature is now checked in CI against 'could this have been known at prediction time'; the build fails otherwise. Training and serving now read the same table." An automated gate is a promise a stakeholder can audit; a commitment to be more careful is not.
    4. Re-anchor on the honest comparison and the real decision. 0.78 against the heuristic's 0.61 is a material improvement worth an estimated SAR 12.4M/yr if it holds live. Ask for a re-run of the A/B with the corrected model, with the success criterion agreed in advance, in live MAE — never again in offline R².

    And the institutional point, which is the one worth the most: the offline metric is not the deliverable; the live outcome is. Wojhah's review celebrated an offline number as if it were a result. The fix is to make the A/B, not the evaluation notebook, the gate for the celebration.

    Q5 (reserve, or use ACT-09 as the extension). Which features in P3 are genuinely arguable?

    Model answer. Two.

    Feature 5, rolling_zone_demand_15m. The frame RANGE BETWEEN INTERVAL 15 MINUTES PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW includes the prediction instant itself. Argument for valid: at 08:12 the zone's demand over 07:57–08:12 is observable; nothing from the future is included. Argument for leaking: rows sharing an identical feature_ts are included, and — the practical concern — the count includes this very request, so the feature is partly a function of the event being predicted; and if any upstream row has a feature_ts assigned at completion rather than request, the frame silently admits the future. Verdict: valid in principle, fragile in practice. Make it robust by bounding the frame to INTERVAL 1 SECOND PRECEDING and by asserting that feature_ts = pickup_ts for every row. The discipline is to remove the ambiguity, not to win the argument.

    Feature 8, historical_route_duration. A 28-day median over prior trips on the same route. Argument for valid: it is strictly historical, and it is the single most useful feature in the model. Argument for leaking: if the 28-day window is computed relative to the training run date rather than each row's feature_ts, then a trip on 3 May receives a median computed from trips through 1 June — a classic and very common point-in-time violation that a lint on the SQL text will not catch. Verdict: valid if and only if the window is anchored per row to feature_ts; leaking if anchored to the job's run date. You cannot tell from the definition as written in P3, which is the lesson: a feature definition that does not state its anchor is not a definition.

    Note also that feature 8 is the one most likely to be unavailable online at acceptable latency if computed live (P5 does exactly this, with a live percentile query) — which is why it must be materialised, not re-derived.

    8. Solution approach¶

    8.1 Per-feature verdicts (deliverable a) — facilitator key¶

    # Column Verdict Reason
    1 pickup_zone_id Valid Present in the request itself
    2 dropoff_zone_id Valid Requested destination, stated at request time
    3 hour_of_day Valid Derived from feature_ts; deterministic and known
    4 day_of_week Valid Same
    5 rolling_zone_demand_15m Arguable → valid if bounded Frame must exclude the current instant; feature_ts must equal pickup_ts. See §7 Q5
    6 rolling_avg_speed_10m Valid Strictly trailing 10 minutes of vehicle positions. (And the feature CS-11 will corrupt — flag this for tomorrow.)
    7 driver_recent_trip_count LEAKING Calendar-hour bucket containing feature_ts; includes trips that start after the prediction
    8 historical_route_duration Arguable → valid if per-row anchored Must be a 28-day window ending at each row's feature_ts, not at the job's run date
    — weather_condition Valid, but skew-prone Offline from weather_hourly, online from a live API — different vocabularies, must be materialised
    — distance_km Valid Estimated route distance is known at request. (Note: metered distance is NOT — if the pipeline uses metered distance_km from the completed trip, this becomes LEAKING. Ask the room.)

    The distance_km note is the best trap in the pack. gold.eta_features.distance_km sourced from silver.trips.distance_km is the metered distance, known only at dropoff. Sourced from the routing engine's estimate at request time, it is valid. Same column name, opposite verdict, depending on provenance — which is the whole point of lineage.

    8.2 The corrected feature (deliverable b)¶

    -- WRONG (leaking): calendar hour containing the prediction
COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY driver_id, date_trunc('hour', feature_ts))  AS driver_recent_trip_count

-- RIGHT: strictly trailing window, closed before the prediction instant
COUNT(*) OVER (
    PARTITION BY driver_id
    ORDER BY feature_ts
    RANGE BETWEEN INTERVAL 60 MINUTES PRECEDING
              AND INTERVAL  1 SECOND  PRECEDING
) AS driver_recent_trip_count

    Both are one line. One is a quarter of wasted capacity and a credibility crisis; the other is not. That is the module in a diff.

    8.3 The structural fix¶

                      silver.trips ┐
        silver.vehicle_positions├──► gold.eta_features ─┬──► training  (read directly)
              weather_hourly    │   ONE definition,     │
                    zones      ─┘   point-in-time       └──► publish_features job
                                    correct                        │
                                                                   ▼
                                              Redis  masar:features:zone:{zone_id}
                                              (a COPY; no logic; PDPL filter applied here)
                                                                   │
                                                                   ▼
                                                          eta_online service
                                                          (GET only, p99 ~3 ms)

    Four properties: one definition, publish-not-recompute, PDPL enforced at the single boundary, latency budget met by construction.

    8.4 CI gates (deliverable c)¶

    Gate Runs Fails the build when
    Leakage lint on feature SQL every PR A window frame reaches CURRENT ROW/FOLLOWING, or a calendar bucket contains feature_ts, or a join lacks a <= feature_ts predicate
    Future-ablation test every PR Recomputing any feature with only pre-feature_ts data changes its value
    Temporal split enforcement training harness The train/test split is not strictly temporal, or windows overlap
    Offline/online consistency hourly, production Mismatch rate on any feature exceeds 1% of sampled requests
    PDPL boundary audit every publish Any rider_id, raw lat/lon, or national_id_hash appears in a BI view or an online-store key

    8.5 The three sentences (deliverable d) — reference¶

    "Our reported offline R² of 0.93 was wrong; the correct figure is 0.78, and we found the cause: one input counted trips a driver had not yet taken, so the model was partly reading the answer. We have removed it, moved training and serving onto the same feature table so they can no longer disagree, and added a build check that fails any future feature which uses information unavailable at prediction time. At an honest 0.78 against the heuristic's 0.61 we still expect a material improvement, and we would like to prove it in a live A/B with the success criterion agreed in advance and measured in live error, not offline score."

    9. What good looks like vs common wrong turns¶

    What good looks like Common wrong turn

    10. Debrief (5 minutes)¶

    1. Offline 1.41 min → live 4.93 min. That 3.5× is not overfitting, which degrades gently. It is the signature of a feature that does not exist at prediction time. Learn the shape: a large, abrupt offline/live gap means leakage; a small, gradual one means overfitting.
    2. One question, applied without exception. Could this feature have been known at prediction time? That single question resolves eight of ten features immediately and identifies the two that need a design decision rather than a verdict. You will use it again in fifteen minutes in ACT-09 against every column of gold.eta_features.
    3. P6 is the second, quieter lesson. 88% of live requests received a driver_recent_trip_count from a distribution the model had never seen. Fixing the leak alone would have moved the live number by almost nothing. Two implementations of one definition always diverge — you saw it at Naql in BI on Day 1, and here it is again in the feature layer. Materialise, do not re-implement.
    4. The tragedy is the credibility, not the model. R² = 0.78 beats the heuristic's 0.61 and is worth an estimated SAR 12.4M/yr. That model may now never ship, because the number that preceded it was not true. Your offline metric is a claim about the future; treat an unexpectedly good one as a bug report.
    5. Hand into CS-11. "Everything you have just certified as a valid, non-leaking, consistently-served feature assumed one thing: that the underlying data means what it says. Tomorrow's incident is the case where every feature is valid, every pipeline is green, and the model degrades anyway — because a source silently changed what a number means. In particular, look again at feature 6."
    6. The course question. "What happens to Masar's AI system if features are computed differently for training and serving?" The model is evaluated in one world and deployed into another, and the only place the difference is visible is the live metric — which arrives three weeks and one quarter of capacity too late.

    11. The one sentence participants should leave with¶

    Ask one question of every feature — could this have been known at prediction time? — and then serve training and inference from the same materialised table, because leakage inflates the number you present and offline/online skew destroys the model you actually ship, and both wear the identical disguise of "great offline, mediocre live".
