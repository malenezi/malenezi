    ACT-04 — ELT Idempotency Relay¶

    Field Value
    Activity ID ACT-04
    Module M3 — ETL vs ELT and the Transformation Layer
    Day / Hour Day 2 · Hour 3 (during or immediately after LAB-03)
    Duration 20 minutes
    Format Physical relay — teams execute a pipeline by hand, with the facilitator injecting failures
    Group size 4 per team (one per relay station); 3–6 teams racing
    Deliverable A correct final table after four rounds including two injected failures, plus a per-station write-mode ruling

    1. Objective¶

    Participants feel idempotency rather than define it. Each team executes a four-stage pipeline by physically moving row cards between trays. The facilitator then kills a stage mid-run and orders a restart. Teams whose stations use INSERT end with duplicated rows; teams whose stations use MERGE or replaceWhere end correct.

    By the end, participants can state which write mode belongs at which stage, and — the harder point — can articulate why idempotency's real value is that it makes re-running free, and free re-runs are what make investigation free.

    2. Materials (printable)¶

    • Row cards (§7): 24 trip cards per team, printed on card stock and cut. Six carry duplicate trip_ids; four are late arrivals; two are corrections.
    • Station mats (§7): four A4 sheets per team, labelled BRONZE / SILVER / GOLD-DAILY / GOLD-DRIVER, each with its write mode blank.
    • Round cards (§8): four sealed cards per team, opened on the facilitator's call.
    • Four trays or marked areas per team. A stopwatch.

    3. Setup (5 minutes)¶

    Per team: four station mats in a row, a tray at each, the 24 row cards face-down in a "source" pile at the head of the line, and the four round cards sealed. Assign one person per station. The person at station 1 is the only one who may draw from the source.

    4. Run of show¶

    Min What happens Facilitator
    0–2 Frame. "You are a pipeline. Cards are rows. Each station has a write mode, and you choose it now — before you know what is coming." Teams write a mode on each mat: INSERT / MERGE on <key> / replaceWhere <predicate> / overwrite. Do not advise. The wrong choice is the lesson.
    2–5 Round 1 — clean run. Open round card 1. Station 1 draws 12 cards (day 1), applies its mode, passes on. Each station applies its own mode. Time it. Note the elapsed time on the board.
    5–6 Checkpoint. Every team counts rows at each station and writes the count on the mat. Correct: bronze 12, silver 12, gold-daily 1 row per (date), gold-driver 1 row per driver.
    6–9 Round 2 — day 2 with late arrivals. Open round card 2: 12 more cards, of which 4 belong to day 1 and 2 are corrections to day-1 rows already processed. Watch the gold stations. INSERT teams now have two rows for day 1.
    9–11 Round 3 — THE KILL. Mid-round, shout "STOP — station 3 has crashed." That person turns away from the table. After 20 seconds: "Recovered. Re-run the whole pipeline from the source." This is the moment the activity exists for. Say nothing else.
    11–15 Re-run. Teams re-run all 24 cards from the source through all four stations. INSERT teams end with 48 bronze rows and doubled gold aggregates. MERGE teams end identical to before.
    15–17 Count and score. §9's scoring. Read the counts aloud, team by team. The spread is dramatic and does the teaching for you.
    17–20 Debrief. §5. Land the "free investigation" point, not just the arithmetic.

    5. Debrief questions¶

    1. Which stations broke, and which survived? (Bronze append is correct — bronze grows on re-run by design and downstream dedups. The gold aggregate stations are where INSERT is fatal, because a doubled aggregate is both invisible and consequential.)
    2. Why is append right for bronze but wrong for gold? (Bronze's grain is "an arrival"; a re-arrival is a real fact and should be recorded. Gold's grain is "one row per date" or "one row per driver per day"; a second row at that grain is not a fact, it is a corruption.)
    3. What did the late arrivals do to a team using replaceWhere date = today? (They were lost — a day-1 row arriving on day 2 falls outside date = today. This is CS-03's exact undercount, produced by hand in nine minutes.)
    4. After the kill, how did you decide whether it was safe to re-run? (Idempotent teams did not decide — they just re-ran. INSERT teams had to reason about what had already happened, under time pressure, with incomplete information. That is the 03:00 on-call experience in miniature.)
    5. What does that mean for how often you would investigate a suspicion? (This is the real answer. If re-running is free, you investigate on a hunch. If re-running is dangerous, you wait until you are sure — and in CS-03 that wait was four months.)
    6. Where does the 3-day lookback come from, and what does it cost? (From the arrival-lag distribution. It costs re-processing three days of rows on every run — seconds — and it buys absorbing 99.98% of late arrivals.)

    6. Answer key — write mode per station¶

    Station Grain Correct mode Why What INSERT does
    1. BRONZE one row per arrival append An arrival is a fact; a re-arrival is also a fact. Immutable, append-only. Re-run grows history on purpose Nothing wrong — append is the correct behaviour here. The row count doubling on re-run is expected and downstream dedup handles it
    2. SILVER one row per trip_id MERGE on trip_id, 3-day lookback Corrections update in place; late arrivals land in the day they belong to; re-run is a no-op Duplicate trip_ids; corrections coexist with the values they were meant to replace
    3. GOLD-DAILY one row per date replaceWhere date IN (lookback window) The whole window is deterministically rebuilt from silver, so a re-run reproduces it exactly Two rows per date. The dashboard SUM() doubles and looks like a demand spike
    4. GOLD-DRIVER one row per (driver_id, date) MERGE on (driver_id, date) Late trips update prior days, so a merge beats a partition replace here Two rows per driver per day; the incentive payment doubles

    The nuance worth ten seconds: stations 3 and 4 both hold aggregates and take different modes. replaceWhere is right when a whole partition can be deterministically rebuilt; MERGE is right when rows for old periods keep changing. Teams that use one mode everywhere get one of the two wrong.

    Expected counts¶

    After R1 After R2 After the kill + full re-run
    Bronze (append) 12 24 48 — correct and expected
    Silver (MERGE) 12 20 (24 minus 6 dup ids, plus 2 corrections applied in place) 20 — unchanged
    Gold-daily (replaceWhere) 1 2 2 — unchanged
    Gold-driver (MERGE) 5 8 8 — unchanged
    Gold-daily if INSERT 1 2 4 — doubled, silently
    Gold-driver if INSERT 5 13 21 — tripled, silently

    8. Round cards (sealed; open on the facilitator's call)¶

    ROUND 1  "Normal day. Process the 12 cards in the source pile."

ROUND 2  "Day two. Twelve more cards. Four of them belong to yesterday.
          Two are corrections to rows you already processed.
          Four are duplicates of ids you have already seen."

ROUND 3  "*** STOP. STATION 3 HAS CRASHED. ***
          Station 3: turn away from the table. Do nothing for 20 seconds.
          ... Recovered. RE-RUN THE ENTIRE PIPELINE FROM THE SOURCE.
          You do not know which rows station 3 had finished."

ROUND 4  "Count every station. Write the counts on your mats.
          Then answer, in one sentence: was it SAFE to re-run?"

    9. Scoring¶

    Criterion Points
    Silver holds exactly 20 rows, one per trip_id, corrections applied 5
    Gold-daily holds exactly 2 rows 5
    Gold-driver holds exactly 8 rows 5
    Late arrivals landed on 2026-06-01, not 06-02 5
    Bronze holds 48 rows and the team can explain why that is correct 3
    Team wrote correct write modes on all four mats before round 1 3
    Fastest correct team 2 bonus
    Penalty: any station where a human had to reason about "what had already happened" −5

    10. Cohort variants¶

    Small cohort (< 8 participants). Run one shared pipeline with the facilitator as station 1, and give every participant a written prediction sheet before each round: "how many rows will each station hold after this round?" Reveal after each round. With few people the race disappears, so the prediction-and-reveal loop supplies the tension. Add the extension: after round 4, ask each participant to change one station's mode and predict the effect, then test it.

    Large cohort (> 20 participants). Run 5–6 teams in parallel as a race, with a visible scoreboard and a strict 90-second limit per round to force errors. Then add a round 5: teams swap tables and must audit another team's final state against the source pile and write a one-line defect report. Auditing someone else's pipeline is closer to the real job than running your own, and it surfaces the doubled-aggregate defect much more vividly than counting your own cards. Duration 28 minutes. If space is tight, run in two waves of three teams with the second wave observing the first's round 3 — watching another team's kill-and-restart is nearly as instructive as suffering it.
