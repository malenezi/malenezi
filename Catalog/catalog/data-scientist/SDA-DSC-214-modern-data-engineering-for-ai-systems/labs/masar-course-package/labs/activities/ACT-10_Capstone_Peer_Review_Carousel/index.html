    ACT-10 — Capstone Peer-Review Carousel¶

    Field Value
    Activity ID ACT-10
    Module Capstone (spans M1–M8)
    Day / Hour Day 5 · Hour 6 (after capstone build time, before final presentations)
    Duration 45 minutes (three 12-minute rotations + a 9-minute synthesis)
    Format Structured rotation — every team reviews three others' capstones against a fixed sheet and receives three reviews of its own
    Group size Teams of 3–4; works with 4–8 teams. With 3 teams, run two rotations
    Deliverable Three completed review sheets received by each team, plus a one-page remediation commitment: the three findings the team will fix before the final presentation, with the evidence each fix will produce

    1. Objective¶

    Three outcomes, and the third is the one that matters most.

    1. Every capstone gets three independent reviews — far more feedback than an instructor can produce, and more varied.
    2. Reviewing teaches more than being reviewed. A team that finds a missing idempotency guarantee in someone else's DAG will never omit one in its own. The carousel converts each team's blind spot into three other teams' teaching material.
    3. Participants rehearse the professional act of a design review: cite evidence, name the consumer who is harmed, propose a specific fix, and separate a defect from a preference.

    2. Materials (printable)¶

    • Review sheet (§7), printed 4 copies per team (three to give away, one to self-assess before the carousel starts).
    • Reviewer briefing card (§6), one per participant.
    • Remediation commitment sheet (§8), one per team.
    • A visible rotation schedule (§5) and a loud timer.
    • Each team needs its capstone running and demonstrable at its home station: the DAG, the Lakehouse tables, the quality suite, the serving path, and its written design document.

    3. Setup (5 minutes before the session)¶

    Arrange teams at fixed home stations around the room. Post the rotation schedule where everyone can see it. Assign each team a letter (A–F).

    One team member stays at the home station for every rotation as the HOST; the rest travel as REVIEWERS. Rotate who hosts if the team prefers, but the host must not change mid-rotation — the reviewers need a consistent respondent.

    Announce the two rules that make the carousel work:

    1. Every finding must cite something you can point at — a line of code, a table, a query result, a missing file. "It feels fragile" is not a finding.

    2. Every finding must name the consumer who is harmed and how. "This is not idempotent" is an observation. "If this task is re-run after a failure, gold.driver_daily doubles and 3,450 drivers are overpaid" is a finding.

    4. Run of show¶

    Min What happens Facilitator
    0–4 Brief. Read the two rules. Walk the review sheet's five sections in 90 seconds. Hand out reviewer briefing cards. Emphasise that a review with no findings is a failed review, not a perfect capstone. Every capstone in the room has at least three findings
    4–6 Self-assessment. Each team completes a review sheet on its own capstone, honestly, and seals it. It is opened at minute 36. This is the highest-leverage two minutes in the activity. The gap between self-assessment and peer assessment is the individual learning
    6–18 Rotation 1. Reviewers travel per §5. HOST gives a 3-minute demo, then answers questions. Reviewers complete the sheet in the remaining 9 minutes and hand it to the host. Circulate. Kill any demo that runs past 3 minutes — reviewers need the time more than hosts do
    18–30 Rotation 2. Same structure, new station. Watch for review fatigue in rotation 2. Prompt: "Section 4 is the one everyone skips. Do section 4 first this time."
    30–42 Rotation 3. Same structure, new station. —
    42–48 Synthesis. Teams return home, open their sealed self-assessment, read their three peer reviews, and complete the remediation commitment sheet. The instruction: "Pick the three findings that appear in more than one review. Those are real. Findings that appear once may be preferences."
    48–51 Round-robin close. Each team states, in one sentence, the single finding it did not see coming. Do not let this become a report-out. One sentence, strictly. It is the most honest three minutes of the course

    (45 minutes if the close is cut; 51 with it. Keep the close — it is worth over-running for.)

    5. Rotation schedule¶

    Six teams (A–F). Reviewers move; hosts stay.

    Rotation A reviews B reviews C reviews D reviews E reviews F reviews
    1 B C D E F A
    2 D E F A B C
    3 F A B C D E

    Every team reviews three others and is reviewed by three others; no pair meets twice.

    Four teams (A–D): rotation 1 A→B, B→C, C→D, D→A; rotation 2 A→C, B→D, C→A, D→B; rotation 3 A→D, B→A, C→B, D→C.

    Five teams (A–E): rotation n — team X reviews the team n positions to its right, for n = 1, 2, 3.

    Three teams: run two rotations of 15 minutes each; every team reviews both others.

    9. Answer key — what reviewers should find¶

    This is the facilitator's list of the defects that are actually present in most capstones at this stage. Use it to prompt a rotation that is producing thin reviews, and to check the quality of the reviews handed in.

    Frequency Defect Where it hides The question that exposes it
    Very common A gold aggregate uses INSERT INTO instead of MERGE/replaceWhere The DAG's gold tasks "Run it twice."
    Very common The quality gate logs and promotes anyway (exit 0 regardless) The gate task's return value "Show me a batch that got blocked."
    Very common Drift check computed on the aggregate, not segmented The GX suite's group_by (absent) "A unit changes on 34% of one cohort. Does this fire?"
    Common No lookback window; the model filters = current_date() The silver model's WHERE "A trip settles at 00:40. Which day does it count in?"
    Common feature_ts set at trip completion rather than at request The feature build's feature_ts derivation "Show me SELECT COUNT(*) WHERE feature_ts <> pickup_ts."
    Common Online features re-implemented rather than published from gold The serving code "Which line of code computes the online value?"
    Common The baseline for drift is a rolling window The GX suite's baseline definition "The bug has been live 14 days. Does your check still fire?"
    Common No OPTIMIZE/VACUUM task in the DAG The DAG's task list "It is month three. How many files are in this table?"
    Occasional producer_version dropped at ingest The ingest job's .drop() or select() "Group by producer version. Show me."
    Occasional Dedup performed in bronze The ingest job "What is your duplicate rate this week?"
    Occasional Raw rider_id reaches a BI view or the online store The serving views / Redis keys "Show me the schema of what BI reads."
    Occasional VACUUM retention set to a default with no justification The maintenance task "A regulator disputes a figure from 25 days ago."
    Occasional Grain of a gold table undeclared or unenforced The design document "How many rows per driver per day, and what enforces that?"
    Rare but severe BI and AI compute the same measure by different paths Two SQL files "Do these two numbers reconcile? Run both."

    Calibration note for the facilitator. A good review sheet has 3–6 findings, at least one per section, each with evidence and harm. A sheet with 12 findings is usually a list of preferences; a sheet with one is usually a reviewer who did not open the code. Say this at the brief.

    10. Debrief questions (use in the 3-minute close, or extend to 8 if time allows)¶

    1. What did you find in someone else's capstone that you then went and checked in your own? (This is the carousel's whole value. Nearly every team has one. Collect three or four answers.)
    2. How big was the gap between your self-assessment and your peers'? (The gap is not embarrassing — it is the measurement. A team that scored itself 26/30 and was scored 18/30 has just learned exactly which blind spots it has, which no instructor could have told them as credibly.)
    3. Which section had the most findings across the room? (Reliably section 3 — quality and observability — followed by section 4. Sections 1 and 2 are well covered by the labs; sections 3 and 4 are where judgement is required and where the course's hardest cases live.)
    4. Did anyone give a finding that turned out to be a preference? (Good. Distinguishing the two is a professional skill, and getting it wrong in a review costs you credibility for the finding that was real.)
    5. Section 5 — how many hosts could answer the course question about their own system? (This is the summative assessment of the entire week, asked by peers rather than by an instructor. A team that can answer it — naming a component, a feature, a control with a threshold, and a business observation with a time lag — has learned what the course was for.)

    11. Cohort variants¶

    Small cohort (< 8 participants — typically 2 teams). A carousel does not work with two teams. Run instead a deep mutual review: 20 minutes each way, with the reviewing team required to complete all five sections in full rather than one finding per section, and to perform three live tests at the host's keyboard — run the DAG twice, kill a task and recover, and trace one gold row back to a raw file. The depth compensates for the absent variety. Add the facilitator as a third reviewer for both teams so each still receives two independent perspectives. Duration 45 minutes unchanged.

    Large cohort (> 20 participants — 6+ teams). Keep three rotations (more produces review fatigue and declining quality) but add a specialist assignment: in rotation 1 every reviewing team reviews sections 1 and 2 only, in rotation 2 section 3 only, in rotation 3 sections 4 and 5 only. Reviews get sharper and faster, section 4 stops being skipped, and each capstone still receives full coverage across its three reviews. Then add a 5-minute plenary defect league table: the facilitator collects all sheets and reads out the three most frequently occurring findings across the whole room. Seeing that eleven of twelve capstones share the same defect is a more powerful lesson than any individual review, and it is the ideal final note before presentations. Duration 50 minutes.
