    ACT-07 — Watermark Clinic¶

    Field Value
    Activity ID ACT-07
    Module M5 — Streaming Data and Event-Driven Architectures
    Day / Hour Day 4 · Hour 2 (immediately after LAB-05's late-data task, or between CS-05 and CS-10)
    Duration 15 minutes
    Format Triage clinic — teams commit to a watermark, then triage 12 late events against it
    Group size 3–4 per team
    Deliverable A committed watermark per consumer with its residual stated, and a ruling on all 12 late events

    1. Objective¶

    Participants stop treating the watermark as a safety setting where longer is safer, and start treating it as a two-sided trade with a cost in both directions, made with a number drawn from an arrival distribution.

    Three specific outcomes: 1. Choose a watermark from data, and state the residual it drops. 2. Understand that a watermark bounds latency and state, never retention — the dropped events are still in bronze. 3. Recognise that different consumers need different watermarks off the same stream.

    2. Materials (printable)¶

    • Commitment card (§6), one per team — filled in before the triage cards are handed out.
    • Late-event cards (§7), 12 per team, printed and cut.
    • Triage sheet (§7), one per team.
    • The arrival-lag distribution (§5 P1) projected or printed.

    3. Setup (2 minutes)¶

    Distribute the commitment card and project the arrival-lag distribution. Keep the 12 event cards face-down until every team has committed a number. The commitment must precede the evidence — that is what makes the triage honest.

    4. Run of show¶

    Min What happens Facilitator
    0–2 Frame. Project the distribution. "Devices in tunnels and basements flush late. You must choose how long to wait. Choose now, before you see what arrives." State both costs explicitly: too short loses data, too long grows state until the job dies.
    2–5 Commit. Teams fill the commitment card: a watermark for the Ops path, one for the AI path, and the residual each accepts. Circulate. Reject any answer without a residual percentage. "You dropped 1.7% of what? Give me events per day."
    5–6 Read the commitments aloud. Write every team's two numbers on the board. The spread is the discussion. Expect Ops 10 s–5 min, AI 5–30 min.
    6–11 Triage. Deal the 12 late-event cards. For each: PROCESSED / DROPPED / STILL IN BRONZE? against the team's own committed watermark. Circulate with one question: "Card 9 — is that event lost?" The answer is no, and most teams have not internalised it.
    11–13 Score. Read the answer key. Teams score their own triage. The score depends on their own watermark, so two teams can both be fully correct with different rulings — say this out loud.
    13–15 Debrief. §5. Land the three outcomes.

    5. Debrief questions¶

    1. Did anyone choose the same watermark for Ops and for AI? (If so, ask what a 4-minute-old harsh-braking alert is worth. Then ask what a 0.8% incomplete feature is worth. They are different questions and they take different answers off the same stream.)
    2. Card 9 arrived 22 minutes late and your watermark is 10 minutes. Is that event lost? (No. It is in bronze.gps_events, which is append-only and complete. It is excluded from the streaming aggregate, and it will be included by the next batch rebuild of gold.eta_features. The watermark bounds latency and state, never retention.)
    3. What is the cost of a 60-minute watermark? (State ≈ events/s × watermark × per-key state. At 620 ev/s peak that is 6× the state of a 10-minute window — and it is exactly how CS-05's INC-2025-0517 reached 41 GB, OOMed, and froze the demand map for 4.5 hours during the morning peak. The long watermark caused a worse outage than any amount of late data would have.)
    4. Card 12 arrives with a timestamp in the future. What does your watermark do? (It advances to that timestamp, and every subsequent legitimate event is then classified as late and dropped. One device with a broken clock can silence the pipeline for the whole fleet, and lag will read zero throughout.)
    5. How would you know your watermark is wrong? (Monitor the drop rate as a first-class metric — count events discarded as late, segmented by producer_version and by vehicle. A rising drop rate means the arrival distribution has moved, and the watermark must be re-derived. A watermark set once and never revisited is a guess that used to be a decision.)
    6. When would you re-derive it? (Quarterly, and immediately after any producer, network or device-fleet change. Same discipline as CS-03's batch lookback window — they are the same decision at different timescales.)

    8. Answer key¶

    Rulings below use the reference watermarks: Ops 30 s, AI 10 min. A team with different committed numbers is scored against its own numbers — say this clearly, because it is the difference between a rule and a decision.

    Card Lag OPS (30 s) AI (10 min) In bronze? Note
    1 3 s P P yes The 96.2% normal case
    2 24 s P P yes Inside 30 s, just
    3 1m42 D P yes The first divergence between the two consumers
    4 4m13 D P yes —
    5 7m45 D P yes Still inside the AI window
    6 13m45 D D yes Beyond both. Absorbed by the next batch rebuild
    7 16m42 D D yes Tunnel flush
    8 16m37 D D yes Same burst. Arrives in the same micro-batch as card 7 — a burst is not 480 batches
    9 22m10 D D yes The key card. Dropped from both streams, not lost. Batch rebuild includes it
    10 1h32 D D yes The 0.01% tail. A device offline for a shift
    11 4m23, DUP D D as late; also a DUPLICATE of card 2 yes Two independent reasons to exclude it. Dedup on event_id catches it if the watermark does not
    12 −11h30 BREAKS THE PIPELINE BREAKS THE PIPELINE yes Advances the watermark 11 h; every later legitimate event is then late and dropped. Lag reads zero throughout

    Lost forever: zero of twelve. Every card is in bronze.gps_events. This is the answer teams most often get wrong and the one that changes how they think.

    The card that breaks everything: 12. The defence is a plausibility check on event time before it can influence the watermark:

    .withColumn("_clock_skew_s", unix_timestamp("_ingested_at") - unix_timestamp("ts"))
trusted   = df.filter(col("_clock_skew_s") > -60)     # only these drive the watermark
untrusted = df.filter(col("_clock_skew_s") <= -60)    # -> quarantine, counted, alerted

    Never clamp ts to now() — that is a silent repair, which CS-09 classifies as never-before-Bronze and CS-11 prices at SAR 2.4M.

    Reference watermarks, with the reasoning¶

    Path Watermark Admits Drops/day (of 15M) Why
    Ops alerts 30 s 98.3% ~255,000 An alert about a 4-minute-old event is worthless; latency dominates
    AI / silver→gold 10 min 99.93% ~10,500 Feature correctness dominates; 10 min is within the 5-min freshness budget at a 30 s trigger
    Training none (batch from bronze) 100% 0 Bronze is append-only and complete

    The accepted failure mode, as a sentence: "Under a 30-second watermark the Ops path misses ~1.7% of events, all of which are late enough that an alert would no longer be actionable; under a 10-minute watermark the AI streaming path misses ~0.07%, all of which are retained in bronze.gps_events and absorbed by the next batch rebuild. Neither path loses data; both trade latency for completeness in the direction their consumer requires."

    9. Cohort variants¶

    Small cohort (< 8 participants). Run as individual commitments, then a single group triage in which each participant owns two cards and must rule on them aloud against their own watermark. With few people the divergence between individuals' watermarks becomes the discussion — two people ruling card 5 differently, both correctly, is the clearest possible demonstration that the watermark is a decision rather than a fact. Add the extension: give one participant a 60-minute watermark and ask them to compute the state cost at 620 events/s.

    Large cohort (> 20 participants). Run as a two-role clinic: half the teams are the Ops consumer, half are the AI consumer, and neither knows the other's committed watermark. Deal the same 12 cards to both. At minute 11, pair one Ops team with one AI team and have them reconcile their rulings card by card — they will disagree on cards 3, 4 and 5, and the reconciliation forces the "one stream, two consumers, two watermarks" insight to be argued for rather than told. Then run a 3-minute plenary on card 12 only. Duration 22 minutes.
