    ACT-08 — Quality-Gate Tribunal¶

    Field Value
    Activity ID ACT-08
    Module M6 — Data Quality, Observability and Governance
    Day / Hour Day 4 · Hour 5 (during or immediately after LAB-06's gate task; pairs with CS-06)
    Duration 20 minutes
    Format Tribunal — teams are the gate and must rule on 10 real defects from the shipped fixtures
    Group size 3–4 per team; one member is the presiding judge who must announce and defend each ruling
    Deliverable A signed ruling sheet: for all 10 scenarios, a verdict (BLOCK / QUARANTINE / ALERT-ONLY), a scope, and the one-line justification

    1. Objective¶

    Participants learn that a quality gate makes three different decisions, not one, and that choosing between them is a judgement about integrity versus meaning versus noise — not about severity.

    Three outcomes: 1. BLOCK when partial promotion would produce silent corruption (integrity defects: keys, referential integrity, schema). 2. QUARANTINE when a bounded subset is untrustworthy and the rest is fine (the default for almost everything). 3. ALERT-ONLY rarely, and never for anything that would reach a model.

    And the scoping rule: the scope of the ruling matters as much as the ruling. Blocking a whole batch because one city drifted converts a quality incident into an availability incident.

    2. Materials (printable)¶

    • Case cards (§7): 10 scenarios, each on A5, all drawn from the shipped fixtures in data/fixtures/.
    • Ruling sheet (§6), one per team.
    • The gate's three options posted large on the wall (§3).
    • Optional: a gavel. It is not a joke — it materially raises the seriousness of the rulings.

    3. Setup (3 minutes)¶

    Post on the wall:

       BLOCK          the whole batch is rejected. Nothing is promoted.
                  Use when partial promotion produces SILENT CORRUPTION.

   QUARANTINE     the failing rows/segment go to a quarantine table with the
                  failing expectation and batch id. Everything else promotes.
                  Use when the defect is BOUNDED and the rest is trustworthy.

   ALERT-ONLY     everything promotes; a human is notified.
                  Use when the signal is INFORMATIONAL and nothing downstream
                  will act on the data before a human does.

    Then write the tribunal's standing rule beneath it:

    A gate that only notifies is not a gate. If corrupt data reaches silver while an email sits unread, you have built an alarm, not a control.

    4. Run of show¶

    Min What happens Facilitator
    0–3 Frame. "You are the quality gate for Masar. Ten batches are in front of you this morning. For each: block, quarantine, or alert — and scope it." Read the standing rule aloud. Emphasise scope. Half the learning is in scope.
    3–12 Deliberate. Teams rule on all 10 cards, writing verdict + scope + justification. Circulate. One question per team: "You blocked case 6. Four cities are fine. What did you just cost the business?"
    12–18 The tribunal. Take cases in this order: 1, 4, 6, 9, 10 (the five that split rooms). The presiding judge of a randomly chosen team announces the ruling; the room may challenge; the judge defends. You play the operations manager under challenge: "You just stopped 19% of my platform because a statistic looked odd." Make them earn it.
    18–20 Debrief. §5. Read the answer key for the five cases not argued. Land the integrity/meaning/noise distinction.

    5. Debrief questions¶

    1. Which cases did the room agree on instantly? (Cases 2 and 3 — null keys and a referential break. Integrity defects are unambiguous, and they are the only ones that justify a whole-batch block.)
    2. What made cases 6 and 9 hard? (Both are semantic: the data is individually valid and collectively wrong. Neither has a row-level rule. And in both, only part of the platform is affected — so scope is the whole decision.)
    3. Did anybody rule ALERT-ONLY on anything that feeds a model? (If so, walk them through the consequence: the model trains on it before a human reads the email. The sim-alertonly failure — the alarm rings and the corrupt data flows anyway.)
    4. Why is "no data" better than "wrong data"? (A gap in a dashboard prompts a question; plausible wrong numbers prompt a decision. Quarantine converts an invisible semantic corruption into a visible, bounded, operational gap — that conversion is the entire purpose of a gate.)
    5. Case 10 — you blocked it. What is now broken? (Nothing yet, and that is the point: an unknown producer_version is not a defect you can see, it is a defect you cannot rule out. Gating on it is how CS-11 becomes a one-day incident instead of a four-day one.)
    6. What is the cost of over-blocking? (Availability. A gate that blocks too readily gets disabled by an operations team under pressure, and a disabled gate protects nothing. Calibration is a safety feature.)

    8. Answer key¶

    # Verdict Scope Reasoning
    1 Negative/absurd/zero fares QUARANTINE the 120 rows A bounded, row-identifiable defect. 7,118 good rows must promote. Note the three sub-cases differ: negative is impossible, > 50,000 is implausible, and fare_sar = 0.00 on a completed trip is the subtle one — it may be a legitimate promotional trip, so quarantine and let a human classify rather than assuming
    2 Null primary keys BLOCK whole batch trip_id is the MERGE key. A null key cannot be merged, cannot be deduplicated, cannot be traced, and 20 nulls collapse to one row under most merge semantics — silent corruption of rows that are not themselves defective. The only unambiguous block in the deck
    3 Out-of-domain city BLOCK whole batch A referential/domain break on a partition and join column. Promoting creates orphan partitions and silently drops rows from every dim_zone join — an integrity defect whose blast radius exceeds the 25 bad rows. (Accept QUARANTINE-25-rows as a defensible minority ruling if the team argues the join is an inner join that would exclude them anyway — but make them say so.)
    4 Dropoff before pickup QUARANTINE the 15 rows Row-identifiable and bounded — but note the escalation: duration_min feeds the model label. A negative label is worse than a missing one, because it is learned from. Quarantine is right; alert-only would be a serious error here
    5 Duplicate trip_id QUARANTINE the 8 dirty-batch dupes; PROMOTE the 120 genuine late trips; MERGE-UPDATE the 6 late-fixture dupes mixed The instructive case: not all duplicates are defects. The 6 late-fixture rows with existing trip_ids are corrections, and a MERGE on trip_id handles them correctly. The 8 dirty-batch dupes are genuine defects. The ruling depends on provenance, not on the symptom — which is why _source_file and _batch_id are mandatory in bronze
    6 100× fare shift, one city QUARANTINE that city's batch only The scoping case. Fail-fast would block four healthy cities — 81% of the platform — converting a quality incident into an availability incident. Alert-only would promote a 100× error into the pricing model. Quarantine the city; promote the rest; page a human within one business day
    7 3.1% missing geohash ALERT-ONLY — The one legitimate alert-only in the deck. Stable at 3.1% for six months, documented, feeds zone-level analysis rather than a model, and the NULL is honest — downstream aggregates can exclude it. Escalate to quarantine only if the rate changes, which is the real signal
    8 Batch 4 hours late ALERT on freshness, BLOCK the gold build the downstream build, not the data Two decisions, and teams that give one have missed it. The data is fine — promote it. The problem is that the gold build already ran without it, so gold is stale. The correct action is to alert on freshness (within the 6 h warn SLA) and re-run the gold build — which is safe precisely because it is idempotent (ACT-04, CS-07)
    9 Speed collapse, one cohort QUARANTINE producer_version = '2.5.0' CS-11 in one card. No row-level rule can catch it; only a distribution check segmented by producer_version. Quarantine that cohort: the ETA model receives fewer positions rather than 3.6×-wrong ones. Missing data asks a question; wrong data makes a decision. Note the safety consequence for the room: promoting silently disables speeding alerts for 1,004 vehicles
    10 Unknown producer_version BLOCK / QUARANTINE the cohort producer_version = '2.6.0' The counter-intuitive one, and the most important. Nothing is visibly wrong — and that is exactly the point. An unknown producer version means an unverified semantic contract. Quarantine until: release notes reviewed for semantic change, contract version checked, unit factor confirmed, and a 24 h shadow comparison of the new cohort's distributions against the incumbent. Case 9 is case 10 four days later, unblocked

    Score: BLOCK 2 (cases 2, 3) · QUARANTINE 6 (1, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10) · ALERT-ONLY 1 (case 7) · case 8 is a two-part ruling.

    The pattern to state in the debrief: quarantine is the default, block is reserved for integrity defects where partial promotion silently corrupts good rows, and alert-only requires all of — stable, documented, understood, and not feeding a model. Only case 7 meets all four.

    9. Cohort variants¶

    Small cohort (< 8 participants). Run as a single tribunal with rotating judges: each participant presides over one or two cases, announces the ruling, and the rest of the room acts as counsel for the operations team (arguing against blocking) and counsel for the model owner (arguing against promoting). The adversarial framing supplies the tension that multiple teams would otherwise create, and it produces better justifications than team deliberation does. Cut to 7 cases if time is tight — keep 2, 5, 6, 9, 10 and any two others.

    Large cohort (> 20 participants). Run as appellate rounds. Round 1: teams of 4 rule on all 10 independently (9 min). Round 2: each team passes its ruling sheet two tables to the right; the receiving team must appeal exactly two rulings in writing, citing the harm the original ruling causes (5 min). Round 3: plenary, taking only the cases with the most appeals — reliably 6, 9 and 10 (6 min). The appeal step forces engagement with someone else's reasoning, which is closer to a real design review than deliberating in a comfortable team. Duration 25 minutes.

    SDAIA Academy — internal use only. Do not distribute.
