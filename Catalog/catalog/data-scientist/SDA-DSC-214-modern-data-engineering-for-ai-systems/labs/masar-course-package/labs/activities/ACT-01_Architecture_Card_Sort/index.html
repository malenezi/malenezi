    ACT-01 — Architecture Card-Sort¶

    Field Value
    Activity ID ACT-01
    Module M1 — Evolution of Data Architecture
    Day / Hour Day 1 · Hour 2 (after the warehouse → lake → lakehouse timeline, before CS-01)
    Duration 15 minutes (20 with the extended debrief)
    Format Team card-sort against two independent axes
    Group size 3–4 per team
    Deliverable 12 cards placed on a 2-axis grid, each with a one-word justification

    1. Objective¶

    By the end participants can, for any workload put in front of them:

    1. Say whether it belongs on a warehouse, a lake, or a Lakehouse, and why that is a statement about the workload's requirements, not about fashion; and
    2. Say which medallion layer it should read — bronze, silver, or gold — and why reading the wrong layer is a governance failure rather than a performance choice.

    The activity's real purpose is to establish that these are two independent questions. Teams that collapse them ("gold means warehouse") have the wrong model, and the sort is designed to expose that within four minutes.

    2. Materials (printable)¶

    • Card deck: §7, 12 cards, printed on A5 and cut. One deck per team.
    • Grid mat: one A2 sheet per team, drawn as below (or two rows of sticky-note headers on the table).
    • Markers, one per team.
                      BRONZE          SILVER          GOLD
              ┌──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐
   WAREHOUSE  │              │              │              │
              ├──────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┤
   LAKE       │              │              │              │
              ├──────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┤
   LAKEHOUSE  │              │              │              │
              └──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┘

    3. Setup (2 minutes before the session)¶

    Cut one deck per team and shuffle it. Place a grid mat and a deck face-down on each table. Do not explain the grid in advance — the first instruction reveals it.

    4. Run of show¶

    Min What happens Facilitator
    0–1 Instruction. "Twelve workloads. Two questions per card: which architecture serves it, and which medallion layer does it read? Place every card. You have eight minutes." Do not define the axes further. Ambiguity is productive here.
    1–9 Sort. Teams place all 12. Every card gets a one-word justification written on it. Circulate. One question per table only: "Card 4 — who is harmed if that reads bronze?"
    9–12 Gallery walk. Each team moves one table right and marks, in a second colour, any card it would move. Silent. Enforce silence. Disputes are for minute 12.
    12–16 Resolve the contested cards. Cards 3, 7, 9 and 11 attract the most movement; work those four publicly. Build the master grid on the board as you go.
    16–20 Debrief. §5. Land the "two independent axes" point explicitly.

    5. Debrief questions¶

    1. Which cards did every team place identically, and which split the room? (The splits are where the judgement lives. Cards 1, 2, 5 are near-unanimous; 3, 7, 9, 11 are not.)
    2. Did any card belong on a warehouse? (Push hard. Almost none require one; several are conventionally served by one. The distinction between "requires" and "is usually" is the whole M1 argument.)
    3. Which cards read bronze — and would you let a business user run them? (Bronze access is a governance decision. Only cards 3 and 9 justify it, and only for named roles.)
    4. Did anyone place a card in "warehouse + bronze"? (Nobody should. Warehouses do not hold raw immutable feeds. That empty cell is itself a finding about what a warehouse is.)
    5. What happens if the fraud investigator (card 3) is given gold instead of bronze? (The investigation fails: gold has been deduplicated, filtered, conformed and aggregated. The evidence of the fraud may be exactly the row that silver dropped.)

    6. Answer key¶

    # Card Architecture Layer Rationale
    1 Executive daily KPI dashboard Lakehouse (warehouse acceptable as a logic-free cache) Gold Conformed, aggregated, fast, stable grain. This is what gold is for. A warehouse copy is defensible only if it computes nothing (CS-01's cache invariant)
    2 ETA-model training on raw GPS Lakehouse (lake acceptable) Silver + Gold Reads silver.vehicle_positions for validated positions and gold.eta_features for the point-in-time feature set. Never bronze — training on unvalidated duplicates poisons the model
    3 Ad-hoc fraud investigation Lakehouse (lake acceptable) Bronze Contested. The fraud may live in exactly what silver removed — a duplicate, an outlier, a quarantined row. Investigation needs the unmodified record. Restricted access, named role, audited
    4 Real-time surge pricing Lakehouse Gold (fed by a stream) Reads gold.zone_hourly_demand / the online store. Never bronze — a duplicate ping inflates surge (CS-05)
    5 Monthly regulatory submission Lakehouse Gold, version-pinned Needs conformance and reproducibility. The pin is the point (CS-04)
    6 Data-scientist exploratory notebook Lakehouse or Lake Silver Needs breadth and trustworthiness, not a pre-modelled grain. Gold is too narrow; bronze is too raw for exploration to be meaningful
    7 Nightly ML feature build Lakehouse Silver → Gold Contested. It reads silver and writes gold. Teams that place it in one cell have missed that a pipeline spans layers — a good discovery
    8 PDPL erasure request execution Lakehouse Bronze + Silver + Gold (all) Erasure must reach every layer holding the subject's data. Requires ACID delete + VACUUM — this is the card that only a Lakehouse can serve; a plain lake cannot delete a row transactionally
    9 Debugging "why is yesterday's count wrong?" Lakehouse Bronze Contested. You are reconciling gold against the raw record; you need what actually arrived, including late and duplicate rows (CS-03)
    10 Driver-facing weekly earnings statement Lakehouse Gold Conformed, reproducible, and legally consequential. Must match the number that paid them
    11 Streaming operations alerting (speeding, geofence) Lakehouse Silver (streaming) Contested. Needs unit-normalised, deduplicated positions — bronze would alert on duplicates and on m/s values (CS-11) — but cannot wait for a gold batch build
    12 Historical trend analysis over 3 years Lakehouse (warehouse conventionally) Gold Stable grain, wide time range, well-served by a partitioned/ZORDERed mart. The classic warehouse workload, and the one where a Lakehouse must prove itself on cost and speed

    Grid summary: every card lands on Lakehouse; no card lands on warehouse+bronze; the medallion axis spreads 3 bronze / 3 silver / 6 gold (with card 7 spanning and card 8 spanning all three).

    The finding to land: the architecture axis is nearly degenerate — a Lakehouse serves everything — while the medallion axis discriminates sharply. That is the design claim of M1 made visible on a table, and it is why the rest of the course is about layers rather than about platforms.

    8. Cohort variants¶

    Small cohort (< 8 participants). Run as one table, facilitator-led, with each participant placing two cards and defending them to the room before placement. Total 12 defences at ~40 seconds each. Add a rule: any other participant may challenge a placement once, and the challenge must cite a consumer who would be harmed. This converts the activity from a sort into a seminar and works better than splitting six people into two thin teams. Duration unchanged.

    Large cohort (> 20 participants). Run as a two-round tournament. Round 1: teams of 4 sort only the six odd-numbered cards (5 min). Round 2: teams pair up (now 8 people) and merge their grids for the full 12, resolving disagreements (7 min). Then a single plenary resolution of the four contested cards. The merge step is where the learning happens at scale — two teams that placed card 3 differently must now argue it. Use a wall-sized shared grid for the plenary so the whole room sees one master answer. Duration 20 minutes.

    SDAIA Academy — internal use only. Do not distribute.
