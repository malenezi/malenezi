    Three results worth quoting on a slide:

    • The Gini coefficient of digital transactions against population is 0.0372 — deliberately small, because the inequality in this programme sits in the level of adoption (50.0% to 67.8%) rather than in the concentration of volume. A class that expects a dramatic Lorenz curve learns something useful when it does not get one.
    • Jazan ranks 7th of 13 on a raw digital-transaction choropleth and 13th of 13 per capita — a six-place fall, and the whole Case C argument.
    • The truncated-axis bar pair (national adoption, y-axis from 60) carries a Tufte lie factor of 36.8: a 3.50% real change drawn as a 128.71% one.
