    Saudi Public Data Sources — a vetted guide¶

    مصادر البيانات العامة السعودية¶

    Verified 14 September 2026 — re-verify before each delivery.

    Who this is for¶

    The Tayseer dataset is synthetic, deliberately, so that nothing in the training room is sensitive. When a participant takes this course home, the first thing they need is a real source. This guide is the vetted shortlist: four source families, what each actually holds, the exact live URLs, how you get data out, what the licence permits, and — where the licence is not stated at all — what that means in practice.

    Everything below was checked on 14 September 2026. Saudi government web estate is being consolidated actively; the DGA closed 236 government websites in 2024 while consolidating toward 581 platforms under a unified design system. Put the date you checked next to every URL you put on a slide, and re-check before each delivery. The gotchas in Part 3 are not hypothetical — each one has bitten somebody.

    Confidence levels used throughout: High means verified first-hand against the live source; Medium means verified from a secondary source or from page titles only; Low means not reachable from the research environment and therefore not verified first-hand at all.

    Part 1 — The four source families¶

    1. GASTAT — General Authority for Statistics · الهيئة العامة للإحصاء¶

    The single most useful source in this list, and the only one with a licence permissive enough to build a real deliverable on.

    Field Detail
    Main site https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/
    Statistical database https://database.stats.gov.sa/home/landing
    Census 2022 section https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/statistics?index=119025 — five sub-sections including Administrative Area Information
    Use policy https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/use-policy
    Confidence High — licence text read directly from the use-policy page

    What it holds. Population by region, demographics, households, labour force, ICT access and usage surveys, CPI and GDP. Census 2022 in full, including the Administrative Area Information tables that carry population by region — the denominators a per-capita analysis needs.

    Access mechanism. Per-release PDF plus XLSX on each publication page; a separate statistical database with its own interface; microdata by request. There is no public REST API. The advertised RSS feed at /en/rss/stats returns 404 — do not build anything on it.

    Licence for reuse — stated precisely. The use policy permits reuse including commercial reuse, on three conditions: GASTAT is acknowledged as the official source; modifications are clearly indicated; and modified material is not presented as official statistics. Logos are excluded from the grant. In practice this means a participant may build on GASTAT data for a real workplace deliverable with attribution — which is unusual enough among the four sources here to be worth naming out loud in class. It is the only one of the four where redistribution is affirmatively permitted.

    Update cadence. Quarterly for GDP and labour; monthly for CPI; annual for the ICT survey and population estimates; the census roughly decennially.

    2. DGA — Digital Government Authority · هيئة الحكومة الرقمية¶

    Field Detail
    Main site https://dga.gov.sa/en
    Digital transformation indicators https://dga.gov.sa/ar/DT-indicators
    Digital experience maturity https://dga.gov.sa/en/CX-indicators/digital-experience-maturity-index-2025
    Report PDFs Published under https://dga.gov.sa/sites/default/files/YYYY-MM/...
    Confidence Medium — the site returns 403 to automated fetches, so page content was verified from titles and from secondary announcements

    What it holds. Two separate annual indices, and they are routinely confused.

    Index Arabic What it measures Scope
    Qiyas — Digital Transformation Measurement Index مؤشر قياس التحول الرقمي Entity-level digital maturity 240 entities, 95 criteria
    DXMI — Digital Experience Maturity Index مؤشر نضج التجربة الرقمية User-experience maturity of individual services Service-level

    There is no DGA product called a "Digital Government Maturity Index". The World Bank GovTech Maturity Index (GTMI) is a third, non-Saudi index frequently confused with both. Conflating them in a SDAIA room is conspicuous.

    Access mechanism. Public web pages and PDF reports. The site blocks automated fetching, so budget for manual screenshotting. A scripted scraping exercise built against dga.gov.sa will fail in the room.

    Licence for reuse — not stated. No reuse licence was found, and the block on automated access prevented verification. What that means in practice: cite, do not redistribute. Quote a figure with its source and the date you checked it; do not republish a DGA table, chart or report PDF in a handout or a public deliverable. Absence of a stated licence is not permission — the default is all rights reserved.

    Update cadence. Annual, both indices.

    3. The Saudi Open Data ecosystem¶

    Three things travel under this heading and they are not equivalent. The national portal itself could not be verified first-hand; the two reachable alternatives can be taught with, on stricter terms.

    3a. The national open data portal¶

    Field Detail
    Portal https://open.data.gov.sa — linked from the GASTAT footer
    Second indexed host https://od.data.gov.sa
    Confidence Low — all *.data.gov.sa hosts were unreachable from the research environment. This is an egress restriction, not evidence of a dead portal

    What it holds. Not first-hand verified. Dataset counts, the category list, the available download formats, the API shape and the exact licence name are all unconfirmed and must not be stated as fact in class. Describe it as the national portal and send participants to read it themselves.

    Licence for reuse. Per my.gov.sa, an "Open Data License" permitting sharing and derivative works conditional on attribution and on disclosing the licence type when redistributing. The exact licence name and full text were not verified. In practice: treat it as attribution-plus-licence-disclosure, and read the licence on the portal itself before any participant publishes anything derived from it.

    Ownership. Sources disagree on whether the portal is operated by SDAIA or by the DGA. Verify on-network before asserting either in a SDAIA context.

    3b. DataSaudi — Ministry of Economy and Planning¶

    Field Detail
    URL https://datasaudi.sa/en
    Confidence Medium — reachable and inspected; licence read from the site footer

    What it holds. Interactive economic and social visualisations for the Kingdom and all thirteen regions, including density maps. It is the closest public analogue to the Case C regional comparison, which makes it an excellent live demo.

    Access mechanism. Browser only in practice — the site is JavaScript-rendered, so a plain HTTP fetch returns metadata and nothing else. Any retrieval exercise against it needs a headless browser.

    Licence for reuse — not stated as an open licence. The footer carries "Copyright. All Rights Reserved © 2026". In practice: good for demonstration in a live session, risky to redistribute. Show it on screen; do not paste its charts into a deliverable.

    3c. RCRC Open Data¶

    Field Detail
    URL https://opendata.rcrc.gov.sa
    Platform An Opendatasoft instance republishing GASTAT census tables, with an explore UI and an API console
    Confidence Medium — reachable and inspected

    Access mechanism. The browser explore UI works normally and exports cleanly. The API path is disallowed by robots.txt — use the UI, not the console, in any exercise you set.

    Licence for reuse — not verified. The underlying census tables are GASTAT's and carry the GASTAT terms; RCRC's own terms for the republished instance were not verified. In practice: prefer going back to stats.gov.sa for anything you intend to publish, and use RCRC for convenience of export only.

    4. Vision 2030¶

    Field Detail
    Main site https://www.vision2030.gov.sa/en
    Annual reports hub https://www.vision2030.gov.sa/en/annual-reports
    NTP Annual Report 2025 https://www.vision2030.gov.sa/media/uxhfb5if/ntp_en_annual_report_2025.pdf — KPI appendix, pp. 79–86
    Vision 2030 Annual Report 2025 https://www.vision2030.gov.sa/media/ecdjfopq/vision2030_annual_report_2025_en.pdf — over 30 MB
    Annual Report 2024 https://www.vision2030.gov.sa/media/25042025ev1/En-Annual%20Report-Vision2030-2024.pdf
    Executive summary (2024 ed.) https://www.vision2030.gov.sa/media/jgvb3mrb/executive-summary-vision2030.pdf
    Confidence High for the URLs, the structure and the cadence; the contents of the 2025 annual report PDF itself are unverified

    What it holds. The Vision document and the annual reports of the Vision Realization Programs. The national KPI figures live inside the VRP annual-report PDFs, not on the site as data. The single most useful artefact in this entire guide is the KPI appendix of a VRP report: the NTP Annual Report 2025 carries, on pp. 79–86, a table with columns for baseline value, baseline year, target, target year, actual and actual year. That is a real, published baseline/target/actual table, and it is the exact shape of the scorecard participants are asked to build in the capstone.

    Access mechanism. PDF only. The site is JavaScript-rendered, so a plain HTTP fetch returns metadata. Transcribe the appendix by hand or with a PDF table extractor; there is no data endpoint.

    Licence for reuse — not stated. No reuse licence was found. In practice: cite with attribution; do not assume redistribution rights. A KPI table transcribed for internal analysis is normal practice; republishing the report's tables or figures is not covered by anything you can point to.

    Update cadence. Annual, published in Q2 of the following year — the 2025 report was released on 25 April 2026.

    Part 3 — Gotchas¶

    Every item here was checked on 14 September 2026 and every one has cost somebody time. Each is paired with what to do instead.

    # The trap What to do instead
    1 The census portal is dead. saudicensus.sa and portal.saudicensus.sa no longer resolve — DNS failure, not a 404. Old bookmarks and old slides are broken Census 2022 now lives inside stats.gov.sa. Go via https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/statistics?index=119025 and its Administrative Area Information sub-section
    2 Several government sites block automated access. dga.gov.sa, sdaia.gov.sa and parts of my.gov.sa return 403 or a WAF block to scripts while working normally in a browser Retrieve by hand and screenshot. Do not set a scraping exercise against any of these — it will fail live in the room
    3 Vision 2030 and DataSaudi are JavaScript-rendered. A plain HTTP fetch returns metadata only Use a headless browser if you must automate, or download the PDFs directly by URL. For teaching, prefer the PDF route — it is one step, and it works
    4 Stale GASTAT pages outrank current ones. An internet-usage page from 10 May 2018 reporting 2017 data still ranks above the 2025 ICT release Check the publication date on every page before quoting it. Never quote a GASTAT figure from a search-result snippet
    5 Several stats.gov.sa deep links 404 Use the /en/statistics?index=… form rather than a deep link copied from an older document
    6 No 2025 or 2026 Saudi population estimate has been published The current official figure is the mid-2024 estimate, 35,300,280. Anything more recent is somebody's projection — say so if you use one
    7 No 2026 UN E-Government Survey exists The most recent edition is 2024, rank 6 with an EGDI of 0.96022. Do not cite a "2026 EGDI"
    8 There is no "Digital Government Maturity Index" DGA runs two indices — Qiyas (transformation, entity-level) and DXMI (experience, service-level). The World Bank GovTech Maturity Index is a third, non-Saudi index. Name the one you mean
    9 Ownership of the national open data portal is disputed in sources — SDAIA or DGA Verify on-network before asserting either, particularly in a SDAIA context where the room will know
    10 Government website consolidation is ongoing. 236 sites closed in 2024 alone Version-date every link in course materials and re-check the week before delivery

    One further trap, flagged as instructor judgement rather than as a research finding. Every index above is a composite with its own weighting. Quoting a change in an index score as if it were a change in a quantity — "digital transformation rose 1.16 points" — is the same class of error as averaging an average. Percentages of composites do not travel. This appears on the deck's gotchas slide and is the instructor's call, not a verified fact about any of these sources.

    2 Whether the national open data portal is operated by SDAIA or by the DGA Sources disagree. Unresolved

    Part 5 — Three worked exercises with real data¶

    Each of these is doable after the course with nothing but a browser and the course's own notebook environment. Each respects the access constraints in Part 3 — none of them scrapes a site that blocks scripts, and none redistributes material whose licence is not stated.

    Exercise 1 — Rebuild Case C on real population data¶

    Source. GASTAT, Census 2022 Administrative Area Information. Licence: reuse permitted with attribution, including commercially. This is the only one of the three exercises whose output you may publish.

    Retrieval steps. 1. Go to https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/statistics?index=119025 and open the Administrative Area Information sub-section. Do not use a deep link from an older document — several 404. 2. Download the population-by-region table as XLSX. Check the publication date on the page before you take the figures; stale pages outrank current ones in search. 3. Verify your transcription by summing: the thirteen regional figures must come to exactly 32,175,224. If they do not, you have taken a Saudi-only or non-Saudi-only column. 4. Take any count you care about for the same thirteen regions from your own organisation's reporting — service volumes, registrations, applications, visits.

    The chart it should produce. Two sorted horizontal bars side by side: the raw count, and the same count per 1,000 residents. Compute the rank on each and the rank shift between them. The deliverable is not the pair of charts but the one-sentence subtitle that states the denominator, the period and the comparison, in under fifteen words — and the written answer to the Case C question, what is the raw-count map a map of?

    What it teaches. Exactly what Case C teaches on synthetic data, with the participant's own numbers and the real census denominator. A raw-count map is, to a very good approximation, a population map. Expect the rank shift to be large; in the Tayseer data the largest is six places.

    Exercise 2 — Build a real baseline/target/actual scorecard¶

    Source. Vision 2030, National Transformation Program Annual Report 2025, KPI appendix, pp. 79–86: https://www.vision2030.gov.sa/media/uxhfb5if/ntp_en_annual_report_2025.pdf. Licence: none stated. Cite with attribution; keep the output internal.

    Retrieval steps. 1. Download the PDF directly by URL. Do not attempt to scrape the site — it is JavaScript-rendered and will return metadata only. 2. Go to pp. 79–86. The appendix carries six columns: baseline value, baseline year, target, target year, actual, actual year. 3. Transcribe six to ten KPIs by hand, or with a PDF table extractor, into a CSV with exactly those six columns plus the KPI name. Hand transcription of ten rows takes about fifteen minutes and is the honest route. 4. Record the report version and the date you downloaded it in the CSV header. The 2025 report was released 25 April 2026; a 2026 report will supersede it in Q2 2027.

    The chart it should produce. A bullet chart per KPI: a bar for the actual, a marker for the target, a light band for the range between baseline and target, sorted by proportion of the gap closed. Annotate each bar with its baseline year and actual year — different KPIs in that appendix have different reference years, and a scorecard that hides that is making a comparison it has not earned.

    What it teaches. The shape of a real public-sector scorecard, and the single most common defect in one: rows whose actual and target are measured in different years, presented in one column as though they were contemporaneous. It is also the direct rehearsal for the capstone deliverable.

    Exercise 3 — Chart a ranking that is not a quantity¶

    Source. UN E-Government Development Index, country page for Saudi Arabia: https://publicadministration.un.org/egovkb/en-us/Data/Country-Information/id/149-Saudi-Arabia. Four editions are on the record: 52nd in 2018, 43rd in 2020, 31st in 2022, and 6th in 2024 with an EGDI of 0.96022. There is no 2026 edition.

    Retrieval steps. 1. Open the country page in a browser and read the rank and index value for each edition. Four editions is a small enough series to transcribe by hand, and the page is reachable without any special handling. 2. Record both the rank and the index value for each edition in two separate columns. The exercise depends on having both. 3. Note the edition years explicitly — 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024, biennial. Do not interpolate the missing years, and do not extend the series to 2026.

    The chart it should produce. A single line chart of index value over the four editions, with the rank annotated at each point as text rather than plotted. Invert nothing and plot no rank axis. Then write the annotation that explains why: a rank is a position in a field whose composition changes between editions, so the nine-place move between 2018 and 2020 and the twenty-five-place move between 2022 and 2024 are not comparable quantities, while the index value is at least on a stable scale. If the participant wants a rank chart as well, it must be a second panel with its own explicit caveat.

    What it teaches. The composite-index trap, on a real series a Saudi analyst will be asked about. It also rehearses the Minard units discipline — read what the number actually is before you encode it — and the honest refusal that Case C teaches: a technically correct chart aimed at the wrong question tells you nothing, and saying so out loud is the analyst's job.

    Re-verification checklist before each delivery. Open every URL in Part 1. Confirm the census route through stats.gov.sa still works. Check whether a 2025 or 2026 GASTAT population estimate has appeared, and whether a 2026 UN E-Government Survey exists — if either has, facts 6, 7 and 9 change. Check whether the Vision 2030 2026 annual report has been published. Re-read the GASTAT use policy; it is the only licence in this guide that permits redistribution, and the course's advice to participants depends on it.
